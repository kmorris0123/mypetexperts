## How to choose a cat harness

### Pick a harness made for cats

The SF SPCA says to use a harness designed for cats. The leash should attach at the back of the harness, not at the neck. Never clip a leash to a collar.

### Get the fit right

Fit comes first. The SF SPCA says you should be able to slip two fingers between the harness and your cat's body. Best Friends Animal Society says it should be snug enough that your cat can't wiggle out, but not too tight.

Measure your cat's neck and chest with a soft tape before you shop. Then compare the numbers to the size chart. The RC Pets page says to measure girth first and size up if your cat falls between sizes.

### Look for soft, light materials

Best Friends Animal Society suggests soft, lightweight, high-quality materials. Mesh, soft cotton and nylon are all used in the picks above. Check the product page for materials, and watch for rubbing after the first few outings.

### Check that your cat wants to walk

Not every cat enjoys it. Best Friends Animal Society says walking may not suit a cat that shows stress from the harness or leash. The SF SPCA says indoor enrichment is often better for fearful cats.

## Safety tips

- **Get your cat ready first.** The SF SPCA says your cat should be up to date on recommended vaccines and have a microchip.
- **Start indoors.** Leave the harness near your cat's food or bed for a few days. Then try it on for short times, with treats, before you add the leash.
- **Carry your cat outside.** Put the harness on away from the door and carry your cat out, as the SF SPCA advises. Pick a quiet, sheltered spot, like a fenced yard.
- **Keep the leash slack.** Best Friends Animal Society says never to pull on the leash. If your cat is startled, keep the leash slack until you can reach them so they can't slip out of the harness.
- **Never jerk the leash or tie your cat up.** The SF SPCA says never to leave a cat tied outside, even for a moment.
- **Stay away from traffic and other hazards.** Best Friends Animal Society says to keep your cat well away from them.
- **Stop if your cat is scared.** The SF SPCA says stalling, crouching or trying to escape can be signs of fear.
