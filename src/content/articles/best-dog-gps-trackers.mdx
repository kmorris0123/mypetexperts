## The best GPS trackers for dogs

<ProductPick id="fi-series-3-plus">
Fi says this collar's battery lasts up to 3 months and that it fits dogs from XS to XL. It is rated IP68 and IP66K, and it has LED lights and activity tracking in the app. Skip it if you want to keep your dog's current collar, because the tracker is built into Fi's collar. A membership is required.

<ProductPick id="tractive-gps-dog-6">
Tractive's DOG 6 weighs 1.4 ounces and is made for dogs above 9 pounds. It clips onto a collar or harness you already own and uses LTE cell networks. Tractive says the battery lasts up to 2 weeks with Power Saving Zones. A subscription is required, and it is not meant for dogs under 9 pounds.
</ProductPick>

<ProductPick id="tractive-gps-dog-6-xl">
The DOG 6 XL is for dogs above 44 pounds. Tractive says its battery lasts up to 6 weeks with Power Saving Zones, and it adds brighter LEDs and louder sound. At 2.5 ounces it is too large for small dogs. A subscription is required.
</ProductPick>

<ProductPick id="garmin-tt-15-mini">
This collar sends location to a paired Garmin handheld by radio, up to 4 miles, so it does not depend on cell service. Garmin says it fits necks as small as 9.5 inches, and battery life is 16 to 30 hours of typical use. It also has training features. Skip it if you want a phone app, because you need a separate handheld such as the Alpha 100 or Astro 320.
</ProductPick>

<ProductPick id="halo-collar-5">
Halo's collar adds GPS fences you draw anywhere to its tracking. Halo lists up to 48 hours per charge, an IP67 rating, and a fit for necks of 8 to 30.5 inches. Its feedback options include sound, vibration and static. Skip it if you don't want a collar with those options. It is for dogs over 5 months old and 10 pounds or more, and a membership is required.
</ProductPick>

<ProductPick id="spoton-gps-fence">
SpotOn makes a wireless GPS fence collar. SpotOn says the core fence works without a required subscription, and you can make unlimited fences. Tracking and breach alerts are an optional paid plan. It is for dogs at least 15 pounds with necks of 10 inches or more, so it's not for toy breeds.
</ProductPick>

## How to choose a GPS tracker for dogs

### GPS and Bluetooth trackers are not the same

The AKC says GPS trackers work across wide areas using satellites and cell networks. Bluetooth trackers only reach about 30 feet and rely on a nearby phone to share their location. The AKC also says manufacturers caution against using Bluetooth item trackers on living things. For a dog who may run off, choose GPS.

### Check the subscription

The AKC says GPS trackers with a SIM card need a plan, and it lists $5 to $10 a month as a typical range. Check what the plan covers and how long you must sign up for before you buy. SpotOn says its core fence needs no required plan, and Garmin's collar uses radio to a handheld instead of a cell network.

### Match the size to your dog

Makers set minimum sizes. Tractive's DOG 6 is for dogs above 9 pounds, and its XL models are for dogs above 44 and 55 pounds. SpotOn asks for 15 pounds and a 10-inch neck. Halo asks for 10 pounds and a dog over 5 months. A tracker that is too heavy for a small dog is a poor fit.

### Think about where you walk

Cell-based trackers need network coverage. Garmin's collar uses radio to a handheld, so it works differently. Pick the type that matches where you hike or hunt.

### Look at battery and water ratings

Battery claims range from about 16 to 30 hours (Garmin) to up to 3 months (Fi). Those are manufacturer claims and depend on how you use the device. If your dog swims, check the water rating. Ratings here run from 1 ATM (Garmin) to IP68 (Tractive and Fi).

### Decide if you want a fence

A tracker shows where your dog is. A fence collar lets you set a boundary. Halo and SpotOn offer fences. See our guide to [no-pull dog harnesses](/best-no-pull-dog-harness/) if your dog also pulls on walks.

## Safety tips

- Check the fit. Makers list minimum dog sizes and neck sizes, so follow them.
- Charge the device often and check the battery before each outing. A dead tracker can't help you.
- Look at the skin under the collar now and then for rubbing or redness.
- Don't rely on a tracker alone. Keep your dog's ID tag on the collar.
- Garmin's TT 15 Mini and the Halo Collar 5 offer training feedback such as stimulation. Read the manufacturer's directions before you use any of it, and talk to your vet or a trainer if you have questions.
