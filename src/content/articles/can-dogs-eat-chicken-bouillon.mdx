Better not. Bouillon cubes and powder are very high in sodium (salt), which can upset a dog's stomach and, in serious cases, cause seizures. Some bouillon and broth products also contain onion or garlic, which can harm a dog's red blood cells. Plain, unsalted broth is the safer choice.

  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline, especially if your dog ate a whole cube, a lot of powder, or any product with onion or garlic.

## Why is bouillon bad for dogs?

There are two problems. The first is salt. The ASPCA says bouillon cubes used for stock and gravy are high in sodium. That can cause stomach problems and, in serious cases, seizures.

The second is what else is in the product. The AKC notes that some broths have onions, garlic, or salt added for taste. The ASPCA says onion and garlic can irritate the stomach and damage red blood cells, which may lead to anemia. Anemia means the blood can't carry enough oxygen.

## How does too much salt hurt a dog?

The Merck Veterinary Manual says salt poisoning is unlikely when a dog can control its sodium and has fresh water. The risk goes up when a dog eats a lot of salt, especially without water nearby.

The first sign is usually vomiting within a few hours. The Merck Veterinary Manual says it can then lead to:

- Weakness
- Diarrhea
- Muscle tremors
- Seizures

The ASPCA also lists being very thirsty and peeing often as signs of too much salt. There is no special antidote. A vet treats the signs and checks the blood. Merck says to bring a dog back to normal water levels slowly, over a few days, so a vet should guide this.

## Can onion or garlic powder in bouillon hurt a dog?

It can. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, and powdered onion and garlic can all damage a dog's red blood cells. Concentrated forms, like powders, flakes, and dry onion soup mixes, are the highest risk. Merck also says garlic is 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.

The tricky part is timing. Signs often don't show up until a few days later. They can include:

- Tiredness and weakness
- Not wanting to eat
- Fast breathing
- Yellow skin, gums, or eyes (jaundice)
- Dark urine

Because of the delay, don't wait for signs. Read the label, and call your vet if you see onion or garlic in the ingredients.

## What should I do if my dog ate bouillon?

1. **Take away what is left,** and check the label for onion, garlic, and how much is missing.
2. **Offer fresh water** in small amounts.
3. **Call your vet or a poison hotline.** Tell them your dog's weight, how much was eaten, and the ingredients.
4. **Follow their advice.** Don't make your dog vomit unless a vet tells you to. Merck says that only a vet should direct this for salt poisoning.

## Other kinds of bouillon and stock

### Can dogs eat beef bouillon or stock cubes?

Better not. Beef and chicken cubes are the same type of product. Both are salty, and the label may list onion or garlic. The ASPCA warning about the sodium in bouillon cubes applies to all of them.

### Can dogs eat low-sodium bouillon?

Less salt is better for your dog, but it doesn't fix the onion and garlic problem. Read the whole ingredient list. We didn't find a source that gives a safe amount, so skip it.

## What can I use instead of bouillon?

Plain broth with no salt, onion, or garlic is a better choice. The AKC says to pick dog-safe ingredients, and a vet can tell you whether broth suits your dog. Our guide to [chicken broth for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-chicken-broth/) shows what to look for on the label.

Or just serve your dog's regular food. Keep stock, gravy, and soup mixes up high and out of reach, especially around the holidays.
