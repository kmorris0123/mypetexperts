A few plain bread crumbs will not poison a dog, but they are not a good treat. Seasoned crumbs and breaded foods are the real worry. Garlic, onion, salt, and oil can make a dog sick. **Only share plain crumbs, and only a tiny bit.**

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate crumbs with garlic or onion, or a lot of salty food">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline. Do this even if your dog seems fine, because garlic and onion problems can show up days later.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Are plain bread crumbs okay for dogs?

Plain crumbs are basically small pieces of bread. The AKC says plain white and wheat bread are generally safe for dogs that have no allergies. It also says bread is a filler. It gives your dog nothing that regular dog food does not already give, and it adds calories.

The AKC page does not talk about bread crumbs themselves. So this is a careful reading of what it says about bread. A few crumbs on the floor are not a worry. A bowl of them is not a good idea. Dogs with a wheat allergy should skip them.

## What makes bread crumbs risky?

Crumbs are often mixed with other things, or used to coat food. The sources I read do not list what is in store-bought crumbs, so read the ingredient list. Look for these:

- **Garlic or onion (including powders).** The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered forms have all harmed dogs. Garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion. Dried flakes and powders are the most concentrated. The AKC also names garlic as a danger in bread.
- **Lots of salt.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says too much salt makes dogs vomit within a few hours. Worse cases can lead to weakness, diarrhea, shaking, and seizures.
- **Xylitol or raisins.** The AKC warns about xylitol (a sweetener in some sugar-free foods) and raisins in baked goods.

## Is breaded fish okay for dogs?

Not really. Breading is mostly crumbs, and frying adds oil. The AKC does not mention breaded fish directly, but its advice on fish fits here. It says fish should be fully cooked with no extra oil or seasoning, and have no bones. The AKC says too much oil can upset a dog's stomach and may lead to pancreatitis.

Pancreatitis is a painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ that helps digest food. The Merck Veterinary Manual says eating table scraps or other wrong foods is believed to be a common risk. Signs include not wanting to eat, vomiting, weakness, belly pain, and diarrhea.

The AKC says small fish bones can get stuck in a dog's mouth, throat, or gut, and can even poke a hole in it. Plain cooked fish is a better choice. See our guide on [fish for dogs like salmon](/can-dogs-eat-salmon/).

## How many bread crumbs can a dog eat?

I found no source that gives an amount of bread crumbs for dogs, so I won't give one. The AKC advises very small pieces of bread, especially for dogs that carry extra weight. Treats should stay under about 10% of your dog's daily food.

## Signs to watch for

After crumbs with garlic or onion, the Merck Veterinary Manual says signs usually show up several days later. Watch for:

- Tiredness and weakness
- Not wanting to eat
- Fast breathing
- Pale or yellow gums
- Collapse in serious cases

After a lot of salty or oily food, watch for vomiting, diarrhea, belly pain, extra thirst, shaking, or seizures. Call your vet if your dog gets any of these.

## What should I do if my dog ate bread crumbs?

1. Check the label or recipe. Look for garlic, onion, salt, raisins, and xylitol.
2. Take the food away, and keep fresh water out. The Merck Veterinary Manual suggests small drinks often after too much salt.
3. If it was plain crumbs and a small amount, watch your dog at home.
4. If there was garlic, onion, xylitol, or a lot of salt or oil, call your vet or a poison hotline.

## Other kinds of breaded food

### Can dogs eat fish sticks?

Better not. They are breaded and fried, so the oil, seasoning, and salt worries all apply. Offer plain cooked fish instead.

### Can dogs eat garlic bread?

No. The AKC names garlic bread as an example of bread with a toxic ingredient. Read our article on [garlic bread for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-garlic-bread/).

## What can I give my dog instead?

Try a small piece of plain cooked fish with no bones, no oil, and no seasoning. A little plain bread is okay once in a while. For more, read [can dogs eat bread](/can-dogs-eat-bread/) and our guide to [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
