A bite of plain spinach is unlikely to make a healthy cat sick, but cats don't need it, and it isn't a good habit.
Spinach is high in oxalate, a natural substance linked to the most common kind of urinary stone in cats. If your cat
has had urinary problems, skip it and ask your vet.

I could not find a veterinary source that clearly says spinach is safe for cats, or that it is poisonous. The ASPCA
plant list has no spinach entry. So this article explains what we do know and leaves the rest to your vet.

<Callout type="warn" title="Skip spinach if your cat has urinary problems">
  If your cat has had urinary stones, strains to pee, or pees blood, don't offer spinach. A cat that can't pee, or
  cries out while trying, needs a vet **right away**. A full blockage is an emergency.
</Callout>

## Why is spinach a worry for some cats?

Spinach is a well-known high-oxalate food. A published research review lists raw spinach at hundreds to thousands of
milligrams of oxalate per 100 grams, and the numbers vary a lot between studies.

Oxalate matters because of what it can turn into. The Merck Veterinary Manual says calcium oxalate stones make up about
half of all urinary stones in cats. They can form anywhere in the urinary tract. For most cats, nobody knows the exact
cause.

Here is the part that is not known. I did not find a study showing that a bit of spinach causes stones in cats. Stones
seem to have many causes, and Merck says diet may play a role but the link is not fully proven. The careful choice is
to avoid spinach for any cat at risk, and keep it tiny for the rest.

## Do cats need spinach for nutrition?

No. Cornell's Feline Health Center says cats are obligate carnivores. That means they rely on nutrients found only in
animal products. Their natural diet is high in protein, moderate in fat, and very low in carbohydrates.

A complete, balanced cat food already gives your cat what it needs. Spinach is not a required part of that. If your cat
nibbles a leaf, that's not a reason to panic. It's also not a reason to add greens to the bowl.

## Is it okay for my cat to eat a little plain spinach?

For a healthy cat with no urinary history, one or two small bites of plain spinach once in a while is a low risk. No
source I read gives a safe amount, so I can't give you a number. Keep it very small, and don't make it a regular snack.

If you want to try it:

- Wash it well.
- Chop it into tiny pieces.
- Offer one small taste and watch for vomiting or diarrhea.
- Stop if your cat has any tummy trouble.

Cornell says treats should stay under 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories. A leaf of spinach is far below that,
but it also adds almost nothing your cat needs.

## What should I do if my cat ate spinach?

If your cat ate a little plain spinach and seems fine, you don't need to do anything. Watch for vomiting, diarrhea, or
trouble peeing over the next day or two. Call your vet if you see any of those, or if your cat has a history of urinary
stones.

Call your vet right away if your cat strains in the litter box, cries when peeing, or passes little or no urine. Merck
describes a fully blocked urethra (the tube that carries pee out) as life-threatening. Toxins can build up within one
to two days.

## Other kinds of spinach

### Is cooked spinach better than raw for cats?

Somewhat, for the oxalate. The research review above reports that boiling lowers the oxalate in spinach, and some
measured samples dropped by about half or more. Boiled, drained, plain spinach has less oxalate than raw.

That doesn't make it a good food for cats. It still has no known benefit for them, so the same rule applies: a tiny
amount, once in a while, and not for cats with urinary stones.

### Can cats eat creamed spinach or spinach dip?

No. These usually have onion, garlic, salt, butter, or cream. The ASPCA says onion, garlic, and chives can irritate the
stomach and damage red blood cells, which can lead to anemia (a low red blood cell count). Cats are more sensitive to
these than dogs.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate spinach cooked with onion or garlic">
  Call your vet or a poison hotline for advice, especially if your cat ate a lot or seems tired or weak.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

### What about canned or frozen spinach?

I found no source that covers these. If you use them, pick plain spinach with no salt or seasoning, and treat it like
any other cooked spinach: a tiny amount, if any. Check the label for onion or garlic.
