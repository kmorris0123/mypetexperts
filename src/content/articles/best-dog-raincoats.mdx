## How to choose a dog raincoat

### Decide if your dog needs one

The AKC says hairless dogs, senior dogs and lean dogs such as many sighthounds get the most from a raincoat. It adds that dogs with thick coats can benefit when the weather is wet. Floppy-eared dogs may like a hood, since the AKC says trapped moisture raises the risk of ear infections.

### Check the fabric

The AKC says to avoid fleece or flannel on the outside, because they don't repel water well. It names polyester and duck canvas as better choices. A nylon lining dries faster, according to the AKC. Also read the label closely. "Waterproof" and "water-repellent" are not the same thing.

### Measure your dog

The AKC says to measure the neck, chest and belly, and the length from the neck to the base of the tail. Weight-based sizing may not work, because dogs of the same weight can have different shapes. Long-bodied dogs need extra care, so compare your dog's back length to the chart.

### Think about closures and extras

The AKC says Velcro is easy to use, while plastic buckles give a tighter fit for dogs who pull or move a lot. A leash opening or portal lets you clip a leash to a harness under the coat. Reflective trim helps people see your dog on dark, wet walks. If you already use a harness, see our guide to the [best no-pull dog harnesses](/best-no-pull-dog-harness/).

## Safety tips

- **Do the two-finger test.** The AKC says you should slide two fingers under the straps. If you can't, the coat is too tight. If more than three fit, it is too loose.
- **Watch how your dog moves.** The AKC says to watch for stiff movement or discomfort, especially the first time your dog wears the coat.
- **Keep walks short at first.** The AKC says to keep walks short if your dog is unsure, and build up slowly.
- **Follow the care label.** The AKC says some coats need a delicate cycle or cold water. Kurgo's Halifax is hand wash only.
- **Know the limits of a coat.** The AKC says small dogs and dogs with low body fat can struggle to stay warm in rain and cold. If your dog shivers or seems unwell, bring them inside and call your vet.
