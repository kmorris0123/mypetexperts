Yes, cats can eat a few plain blueberries once in a while. The ASPCA lists blueberries as an okay fruit treat for pets.
But cats are meat eaters and don't need fruit, so treat blueberries as a rare extra. **Offer one or two berries at
most, and keep up your cat's regular food.**

## Are blueberries safe for cats?

The ASPCA includes blueberries on its list of fruits that are okay to give to pets. Its article is written for pets in
general and doesn't name cats, so go slowly and watch how your cat reacts. The ASPCA also warns that too much of a food
your pet isn't used to can cause an upset stomach.

If your cat swallowed a blueberry or two, there's no reason to worry. Just keep an eye out for vomiting or diarrhea.

## Do cats need blueberries?

No. Cornell says cats are obligate carnivores. That means they rely on nutrients that come only from animal products.
In the wild, their food has lots of protein and fat and very few carbohydrates. Blueberries are a fruit with sugar, so
they give your cat little that its regular food doesn't already provide.

The Merck Veterinary Manual advises feeding cats a complete, balanced food made for cats and avoiding table scraps.
Think of a blueberry as a fun taste, not as nutrition.

## How can I serve blueberries to my cat?

- **Pick plain berries.** Fresh ones are best. Skip anything sweetened, flavored, or mixed with other ingredients.
- **Wash them first.**
- **Cut a hard berry into small pieces.** The ASPCA suggests bite-sized pieces for harder fruit. A whole berry can be
  tricky for a small cat to chew.
- **Start with one berry.** Wait a day and watch for vomiting or diarrhea before offering more.
- **Stop if your cat has trouble.** Many cats just aren't interested, and that's fine.

## How many blueberries can a cat have?

The sources don't give a number of berries. They do give limits for all treats together. Cornell says treats should
stay under about 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories. The ASPCA suggests no more than 5 percent for pet
snacks. Because cats are small, that's not much. Giving one or two berries now and then keeps you well under the limit.

Ask your vet first if your cat has diabetes, is overweight, or has other health problems.

## Other kinds of blueberry foods

### Can cats eat blueberry muffins?

No, it's best to skip them. A blueberry muffin has only a few berries, and the rest is flour, sugar, and fat. The ASPCA
says people food is often high in fat and sugar, and too much can cause pancreatitis. That's a painful swelling of the
pancreas, and it sometimes means a stay at the animal hospital. Muffins may also contain milk or butter, and the
ASPCA says dairy can cause diarrhea or an upset stomach in pets.

A small crumb of plain muffin that falls on the floor is unlikely to hurt a healthy cat. But a muffin gives your cat
nothing useful.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate a muffin with chocolate chips">
  Some muffins have chocolate chips or other add-ins. Chocolate is toxic to cats. Call your vet, an emergency clinic,
  or a poison hotline **now**, even if your cat seems fine.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

## Better treats for your cat

- Treats made for cats
- A few pieces of your cat's regular food, given as a reward

Whatever you choose, most of your cat's calories should come from a complete and balanced cat food. For the people foods
that are truly dangerous, see our guide to [foods that are toxic to cats](/foods-toxic-to-cats/).
