## How to choose a cat brush

### Match the tool to the coat

Most cats have a smooth outer coat over a soft undercoat, according to the sources we read. Long-haired cats often have fine, silky coats that tangle easily. Petmate suggests these starting points:

- **Short coats:** a bristle brush, once or twice a week.
- **Long coats:** a slicker brush, with a wide-toothed comb first to loosen tangles. Brush daily.
- **Curly coats:** a grooming glove.

Cornell Feline Health Center suggests getting your cat used to daily brushing and combing to help prevent hairballs. The ASPCA says frequent brushing reduces shedding and hairballs too.

### Think about your cat's comfort

Some cats like pins and teeth. Others don't. A soft rubber brush is a gentle way to start. Move to a slicker or comb once your cat is relaxed. Short sessions are better than a long one that ends in a fight.

### Check the size and the fit

Some tools come in sizes. The FURminator cat tool, for example, comes in versions by cat size and coat length. Pick the one that matches your cat's weight and hair. Choose a handle you can hold comfortably, such as a rubber grip.

## Safety tips

- **Check the skin first.** FURminator says to look for sores, bruises and skin problems before brushing. If you see any, see your vet first.
- **Go easy near sensitive spots.** FURminator says to take extra care around the stomach, legs and rear end.
- **Use light pressure.** FURminator says rough brushing can irritate skin, and to stop if you see redness.
- **Handle mats gently.** Petmate says to hold the base of the mat and detangle slowly. Don't use scissors. For severe mats, ask a professional groomer or your vet.
- **Look over the tool.** KONG says to stop using a toy-style product if it is damaged, and to call a vet if a piece is swallowed. The same sense applies to any brush that cracks or loses parts.
- **Skip human products.** Petmate advises against human shampoos or harsh products on cats.
