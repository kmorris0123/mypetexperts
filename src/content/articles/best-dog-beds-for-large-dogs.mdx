## How to choose a bed for a large dog

### Pick the right size

The AKC says large dogs need a bed big enough to stretch out fully. Bully Beds suggests measuring your dog from nose to tail, then adding a few inches. Orvis says to size up if you are unsure. Don't rely on weight alone, because sizes and weight ranges differ by brand.

### Look at the foam

The AKC says orthopedic beds can help older dogs and dogs with joint problems such as arthritis and hip dysplasia. It also says they can support younger dogs. Consumer Reports says both egg-crate and memory foam help sore joints. Memory foam holds heat, so it suits cold climates. Egg-crate foam lets in more air, so it suits warm ones.

### Choose a cover you can wash

Both sources say to look for a removable cover you can wash. The AKC says to wash it often, ideally every week. A waterproof liner helps with accidents.

### Consider an elevated bed

Consumer Reports says elevated beds reduce pressure points and are easy to clean. The AKC says they are breathable and can help keep dogs cool. Consumer Reports adds that in hot climates plastic piping is better than metal, which can get very hot.

## Safety tips

- **Supervise chewers.** Consumer Reports says to avoid materials that can be chewed off and swallowed, and that a heavy chewer may not be safe alone with any bed material.
- **Take away damaged beds.** If your dog is tearing a bed apart, remove it.
- **Check the step in.** Consumer Reports says tall sides or high platforms can be hard for older dogs to get over.
- **Be careful with plug-in heated beds.** The AKC says electric warming beds need extra caution and supervision, especially with a dog who chews.
