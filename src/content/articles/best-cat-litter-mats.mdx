## How to choose a cat litter mat

### Pick the right size

Start with your litter box. The ASPCA says a box should be about one and a half times your cat's length, so bigger boxes and bigger cats call for a bigger mat. Place the mat where your cat steps out, so the paws cross it. If your cat scatters litter far, a larger mat gives it more room to land.

### Look at the surface

Mats catch litter in different ways. Ridges, pockets, mesh, webbing and spikes all aim to pull litter out of paw fur. Fine litter and dust can slip through big gaps, so a mat with a finer texture, like Catit's two-texture design, may help. Choose a surface that feels soft, since your cat has to walk on it every day.

### Check the backing

A mat that slides gets pushed out of place. Look for a non-slip or rubberized bottom. If you want protection from urine and spills, Drymate describes a waterproof bottom layer and an absorbent top.

### Think about cleaning

Every maker says to shake off or vacuum the litter first. After that, steps differ: some mats can be washed on a gentle cycle, some by hand, and one silicone mat can go in the dishwasher. Pick the one you'll actually clean. Check the maker's care notes before washing.

### Check the material

Makers use different materials, including fiber with a waterproof backing, rubber, PVC and silicone. Some state they're free of BPA, PVC or phthalates. If a page doesn't say what the mat is made of, check the packaging before you buy.

## Safety tips

- **Watch how your cat reacts.** The ASPCA doesn't mention litter mats. It does say cats may be drawn to soft, absorbent surfaces. If your cat starts peeing on the mat or near the box, call your vet first. The ASPCA says behavior is unlikely to improve until a health problem is ruled out.
- **A mat doesn't replace box care.** The ASPCA says to scoop at least once a day and fully clean the box every week with mild, unscented soap or an enzymatic cleaner, and to avoid bleach.
- **Keep the box in a good spot.** The ASPCA suggests a quiet place away from food, water and noisy appliances.
- **Don't put the mat where it can trip you.** Make sure it lies flat. Replace any mat that curls up or gets damaged.
- **Check your litter and box too.** The ASPCA says most cats prefer unscented, clumping litter with a soft, sandy texture and an uncovered box.
