## How to choose a self-cleaning litter box

### Check safety sensors first

Look for sensors that stop the machine when your cat is inside or on the step. Consumer Reports notes that the Litter-Robot 4 pauses its turning when it senses a cat's weight on the step or inside the box. The Neakasa M1 page lists infrared and weight sensors that pause the cycle. Read each maker's minimum cat weight too. The Litter-Robot 4 needs a cat of at least 3 pounds for automatic mode.

### Match the size to your cat

Cornell says larger cats need bigger boxes, and kittens and older cats need boxes with low sides. Check the weight range and the entry height before you buy. Consumer Reports found that a tall step was hard for a 17-year-old cat.

### Pick the right litter

Each box works with certain litters. Some need clumping litter, and the Crystal Pro needs its own crystal trays. Using the wrong litter can stop the machine from working. Cornell says to change litter brands slowly, so plan any switch ahead.

### Think about space and power

These boxes plug in and can be large. Consumer Reports said the Litter-Robot 4 and the PetSafe ScoopFree Pro were hard to place in one reviewer's home because of their size and the need for an outlet. Measure your spot first.

### Open or covered?

Cornell says most cats prefer uncovered boxes, because covers hold in odors. The ASPCA says most cats don't like lids. If your cat is nervous about hoods, an open-top box may be a better fit.

### Count your boxes

Cornell and the ASPCA both say to have one box per cat, plus one extra. Put boxes in quiet, easy-to-reach spots, away from food and busy areas.

## Safety tips

- **Watch the first uses.** Stay nearby while your cat gets used to the machine, and turn off any loud or moving features if your cat seems scared.
- **Keep a regular box available.** The ASPCA says that if your cat starts going outside a self-cleaning box, try a traditional box.
- **Call your vet about changes.** Cornell says to see a vet to rule out medical problems when a cat has litter box trouble. Many apps track visits and weight, which can help you notice changes. They don't replace a vet exam.
- **Follow the maker's rules.** Use only the litter the maker lists, and follow weight limits, especially for kittens.
- **Keep it clean.** The ASPCA says to rinse the box out completely once a week with baking soda or unscented soap. Cornell says to replace litter often enough that it looks and smells dry and clean.
