## How to choose a cat scratching post

### Pick a tall, sturdy post

The ASPCA says cats want a post that won't shift or collapse, and one tall enough for a full stretch. A wobbly post may put your cat off. A wide, heavy base helps, which is why the maker of the SmartCat Ultimate stresses its base size.

### Match the material to your cat

The ASPCA suggests offering several materials, including cardboard, carpet, wood, sisal and upholstery. It also notes that some cats prefer vertical surfaces and others prefer horizontal ones. If you're not sure what your cat likes, watch what they scratch now and pick something similar. Pioneer Pet says sisal is strong and rough, and says carpet can snag claws.

### Think about size and the number of cats

Large cats and homes with more than one cat need more room and a more stable build. A compact bench or cardboard scratcher works for small spaces, but it may not give a big cat a full stretch.

### Look for replaceable parts

Scratching surfaces wear out. Catit sells replacement parts for the Vesper line and replacement cardboard for the PIXI, so you can refresh the scratcher instead of buying a new one.

### Put it where your cat will use it

The ASPCA says to place posts near furniture your cat already targets, or where your cat likes to climb. Pioneer Pet suggests putting a post where your cat spends time, rubbing on [catnip](/can-cats-eat-catnip/) to get your cat interested, and praising them when they use it.

## Safety tips

- **Check stability first.** Put the post on a flat floor and make sure it doesn't rock when your cat pulls on it. Follow the assembly steps and tighten all screws.
- **Replace worn parts.** If a scratching surface is shredded or loose, swap it for a new one using the maker's replacement parts.
- **Don't declaw.** The ASPCA strongly opposes declawing as a way to fix scratching.
- **Talk to your vet** if your cat suddenly scratches much more or less than usual, or if a claw looks injured.
