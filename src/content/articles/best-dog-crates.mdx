## How to choose a dog crate

### Pick the right type

The American Kennel Club (AKC) describes four main types:

- **Metal crates** are the most durable, let in a lot of air, and suit chewers.
- **Plastic crates** are portable, easy to clean, and work for travel, and some are airline-approved.
- **Soft fabric crates** are light and often cost less. They suit small dogs but are harder to clean after accidents.
- **Wood crates** look like furniture, but they are heavier and harder to clean.

### Get the size right

Measure your dog standing, from head to feet and from nose to tail tip. Add 3 to 4 inches to each number. Your dog should be able to sit, stand and lie down comfortably.

Don't go much bigger. The AKC warns that a crate with too much room can lead to bathroom accidents. For a puppy, look for a crate with a divider. You buy for the adult size and move the divider as your puppy grows.

### Check airflow

The AKC says a crate needs ventilation holes or mesh so your dog can breathe. Add a washable bed once your dog is past the puppy stage of using soft things as a toilet.

### Think about how you will use it

For car rides, look for tie-down points and, if you can, independent crash testing. For flying, look for airline approval and then check your airline's own rules. For the house, a folding crate is easy to store.

## Safety tips

- **Take off your dog's collar** in the crate. The AKC says to do this for safety.
- **Don't use the crate as punishment.** Don't leave your dog inside until they stop crying.
- **Don't crate longer than your dog can hold their bladder.** The AKC says dogs should not stay in a crate longer than they are comfortable.
- **Watch for stress.** Pacing, shaking, scratching or biting the door, long howling or barking, and drooling are signs of panic, according to the AKC. Talk to your vet or a trainer if you see them.
- **Secure the crate in the car.** GUNNER notes that in a crash an unrestrained dog can become a dangerous projectile, so use a crate with tie-down points and strap it down.
- **Check for damage.** Stop using a crate with bent bars, cracks or a loose latch.
