Cherries are not a good snack for cats. The pits, stems, and leaves contain cyanide, a fast poison. A whole pit can also
get stuck in your cat's gut. Pet Poison Helpline says eating cherries is not recommended for cats, so the best choice is
to keep them out of reach.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate a cherry pit, stem, or leaf">
  Call your vet or an emergency clinic **now**, even if your cat seems fine. Pet Poison Helpline warns that waiting for
  signs to show up may be too late.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why are cherries a problem for cats?

There are two separate dangers, and both come from the pit.

- **Cyanide.** Pet Poison Helpline says pits, leaves, and stems have cyanide in them. The poison comes out when a cat
  bites or breaks the pit open. The ASPCA also lists the stems, leaves, and seeds of cherry plants as toxic to cats.
  It says the plant is most toxic when it is wilting.
- **Blockage.** A cat can swallow a pit whole. Pet Poison Helpline says it can lodge in the digestive tract and block it.
  Surgery may be needed to take it out.

The sources we read give no exact amount of cherry that is dangerous. Pet Poison Helpline links cyanide poisoning to a
cat eating a large number of cherries and breaking the pits. But it says nothing about a safe number. So treat any pit
as a reason to call your vet.

## Is the cherry fruit itself okay?

We could not find a vet source that says the soft flesh is safe or unsafe. Pet Poison Helpline simply says eating
cherries is not recommended for cats. Cherries also come with the pit, stem, and leaf attached, which makes them
awkward to share safely.

Cats do not need fruit. The Cornell Feline Health Center says cats are obligate carnivores. That means they need
nutrients that come only from animal products. A cat on a complete cat food is already getting what it needs.

## What signs should I watch for?

The signs depend on which problem your cat has, according to Pet Poison Helpline.

| Problem | Signs |
| --- | --- |
| Blocked gut | Vomiting, little or no appetite, trouble pooping |
| Cyanide poisoning | Wide (dilated) pupils, swollen (inflamed) gums, trouble breathing, upset stomach, diarrhea, shock |

The ASPCA lists similar cyanide signs for cherry plants: brick-red gums, wide pupils, trouble breathing, panting, and
shock. Any of these signs means an emergency. Go to a vet right away.

## What should I do if my cat ate a cherry?

1. **Take away what is left** so your cat can't eat more.
2. **Count and note what was eaten.** Was it flesh only, or pits, stems, or leaves? How many?
3. **Call your vet and a poison hotline.** Use the numbers in the red box above.
4. **Go to the vet if they say so, or if you see any signs.** Pet Poison Helpline says treatment can include IV
   fluids and medicine for cyanide. Surgery is needed if a pit is stuck in the gut.

If your cat only licked cherry juice or ate a small bit of pitted flesh, still call and ask. Your vet knows your cat's
size and health, and can tell you how worried to be.

## How do I keep cherries away from my cat?

- Store cherries in a closed container or the fridge, not in a bowl on the counter.
- Throw pits and stems in a covered trash can right away.
- Watch for cherry desserts, jams, and drinks that may have pits or stems in them.
- If you have a cherry tree or cut branches in a vase, keep your cat away from the leaves, especially wilted ones.

## What can I give my cat instead?

Cats do not need fruit treats. A treat made for cats is a better fit. The Cornell
Feline Health Center says treats should be occasional, and gives a guide of 10 to 15 percent of daily calories. For
other fruit, see our guides on [apples](/can-cats-eat-apples/) and [blueberries](/can-cats-eat-blueberries/). For a
longer list of things to avoid, see [foods that are toxic to cats](/foods-toxic-to-cats/).
