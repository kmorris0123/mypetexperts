## How to choose a cooling mat for dogs

### How cooling mats work

The AKC describes three common kinds. Pressure-activated gel mats cool as your dog lies on them and recharge after your dog gets up. Some mats are chilled in the fridge first. Others have inserts you freeze. Cooling time and strength differ from one product to the next.

Not every mat uses gel. The K&H products on this list use water instead. K&H says they cool by drawing heat from your dog and letting it go into the air.

### Pick the right size

The AKC says the mat should fit your dog. A small dog can use a big mat, but a big dog cannot use a small one. Check the size chart on the brand's page. The Green Pet Shop, for example, lists a weight range for each size.

### Look at the material

The AKC says tough materials like nylon resist tearing and biting. Soft cotton covers can tear open and expose the gel. This matters most for puppies and chewers. Brands also differ on where you can use a mat, so check whether it is made for indoors, outdoors or both.

### Think about where it will go

K&H says its Coolin' Pet Pad works best on wood, tile or another hard surface, because carpet holds heat. The Green Pet Shop says to keep its pad out of direct sun, hot cars and hot air. Heat makes it cool less well.

### Check the care steps

Most of these products clean by hand. The Green Pet Shop says to use a soft cloth and mild soap, then let it dry fully. K&H says not to machine-wash its pads or bed.

## Safety tips

- **Don't count on a mat to prevent heatstroke.** The AKC says a cooling mat is not a substitute for fresh water, shade, sun protection or breaks. Keep water out for your dog.
- **Watch dogs that overheat easily.** The AKC lists puppies, senior dogs, overweight dogs and short-snouted breeds as higher risk. It also lists dogs with heart disease and some other health problems. Ask your vet how to keep them safe in the heat.
- **Know the signs of heatstroke.** The AKC lists heavy panting, fast breathing, heavy drooling, red gums, hot skin, restlessness and unsteadiness. Later signs include pale or bluish gums, weakness, collapse and tremors.
- **If you suspect heatstroke, act fast.** The AKC says to move your dog somewhere cool, wet them with cool water (not ice or very cold water) and use a fan. Call your vet or go to a clinic, and keep cooling your dog on the way. See a vet even if your dog seems to recover.
- **Stop chewing early.** The AKC says a dog that chews through a cover can make a mess. If your dog swallows part of a mat, call your vet. The Green Pet Shop says to take your pet in if it eats any part of its pad. Don't leave chewers alone with a mat.
- **Check for damage.** Put a mat away if it has rips or leaks. K&H says its bed is not puncture-proof, so keep your dog's nails trimmed and check under the bed for moisture.
- **Watch the temperature.** K&H says to use its bed only below 100°F and never let it freeze.
