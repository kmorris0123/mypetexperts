## How to choose cat litter

### Clumping or non-clumping?

The ASPCA says most cats prefer clumping, unscented litter. The Merck Veterinary Manual says most cats like small, clumping particles, such as clay-based litter. Other materials include pine pellets, recycled paper and silica gel crystals.

### Should the litter have a scent?

Cats can be sensitive to smells, so the Merck Veterinary Manual suggests unscented litter. The ASPCA agrees. Odor control is best done by scooping often.

### What did your cat use before?

The ASPCA says cats may prefer the litter they used as kittens. If you want to change brands, put boxes side by side with different litters and let your cat choose. Some cats do not like change.

### How deep should the litter be?

The ASPCA says cats usually prefer one to two inches. Some litter makers suggest three to four inches. If your cat avoids the box, try less.

### Kittens

Kitten litters are made for young paws. Dr. Elsey's Kitten Attract Litter is meant for kittens from 8 weeks to 1 year old. Ask your vet if you are unsure what to use for a young kitten. If you are also choosing food, see [can kittens eat cat food?](/can-kittens-eat-cat-food/).

## Safety tips

- **Scoop every day.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says to scoop daily and clean the whole box at least once a week. The ASPCA suggests washing it with baking soda or unscented soap and avoiding ammonia-based cleaners.
- **Have enough boxes.** Have one for each cat, plus one extra. Add one per floor in a multi-level home.
- **Pick a big box.** Cats like room to dig and turn around.
- **Put it in a calm spot.** The ASPCA says to avoid food bowls, noisy appliances and busy areas, and to choose a place with more than one way out.
- **Follow the disposal directions.** Several makers here say not to flush their litter, and others limit how much you can flush. Check the label.
- **Call your vet if litter habits change.** If your cat suddenly stops using the box, a health problem may be the cause.
