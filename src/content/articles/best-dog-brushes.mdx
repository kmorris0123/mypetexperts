## How to choose a dog brush

### Match the brush to the coat

The AKC and Petmate describe these tools this way:

- **Slicker brush:** short, fine pins on a flat head. Best for medium, long, wire and curly coats. It removes loose hair and helps remove and prevent mats.
- **Pin brush:** like a human hairbrush. Best for long coats, as a finishing brush to smooth hair. Petmate says groomers often use one last, to fluff the coat.
- **Bristle brush:** best for short coats. It removes loose hair and dander. On double coats, the AKC says it works well as a finishing brush.
- **Undercoat rake:** long, widely spaced pins. Best for double coats of any length. Pick a pin length that matches your dog's coat length.
- **Rubber brush or glove:** best for short coats. It massages the skin and lifts loose hair.
- **Wide-toothed comb:** the AKC says it can help with mats and tangles.

For a double coat, you may need two tools. A rake or de-shedding tool handles the undercoat, and a bristle or pin brush finishes the topcoat.

### What about a de-shedding tool?

A de-shedding tool, like the FURminator, uses a fine metal edge to pull out loose undercoat. It is meant for dogs that shed and have an undercoat. FURminator says not to use it on non-shedding breeds. The AKC article we read doesn't cover this tool type, so follow the maker's directions.

### Check size and comfort

Pick a brush sized for your dog. Some tools come in several sizes, like the FURminator, which is sold by dog weight and coat length. Rubber-tipped pins can help avoid scratching the skin, says the AKC. Choose a handle that feels secure and comfortable in your hand.

### How often to brush

Petmate says vets suggest brushing every few days, or at least once a week. Brushing every couple of days also helps stop mats from forming. During heavy shedding seasons, FURminator says you may need to use its tool more often.

## Safety tips

- **Check the skin first.** FURminator says to look for sores, bruises and skin problems before using its tool. If you find any, see your vet before brushing.
- **Use a light touch.** The AKC says pressing hard with a slicker can hurt your dog. FURminator says deep or rough brushing can irritate skin, and to stop if you see redness.
- **Take extra care in sensitive spots.** FURminator says to go gently near the stomach, legs, genitals and rear end.
- **Take out mats first.** FURminator says to remove tangles before using its tool. Petmate says to start at the edge of a mat and not pull hard. If a mat won't loosen, ask a groomer or your vet. Scissors can cut skin.
- **Keep sessions calm.** Petmate suggests a quiet, comfortable spot and a steady schedule so your dog knows what to expect.
- **Clean the tool.** FURminator says you can wash its edge with warm water and mild soap, and dry it fully before storing.
