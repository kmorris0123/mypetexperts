Yes, cats can eat shrimp, but only **plain, fully cooked, and in small amounts**. Cooking kills germs that raw seafood can carry. Shrimp is a treat, not a meal. Skip the shell, the sauces, and any garlic or onion.

<Callout type="info" title="The short version">
  - A few small pieces of plain cooked shrimp once in a while is fine for most healthy cats.
  - Don't serve raw shrimp.
  - Don't add butter, salt, garlic, onion, or sauce.
  - Keep treats to a small part of your cat's day. Cornell says no more than 10 to 15 percent of daily calories.
</Callout>

## Is cooked shrimp safe for cats?

Plain cooked shrimp is a reasonable occasional treat. The sources we read do not warn against it. They do warn against raw meat and seafood, and against the extras people add to shrimp.

Cook it well. The FDA says shrimp is done when the flesh turns firm, pearly, and opaque (not see-through). Let it cool, then cut it into small pieces.

Keep it plain. No salt, no butter, no sauce, and no seasoning mixes. The ASPCA says too much salt can lead to thirst, vomiting, diarrhea, and in serious cases tremors or seizures.

## Why shrimp should not replace cat food

Shrimp is just one food. The Merck Veterinary Manual says diets made from only a few foods are often missing nutrients that cats need. Complete cat food is made to cover everything your cat needs, so shrimp should sit on top of that, not replace it.

Cornell suggests treats stay under 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories. For shrimp, that means a few small bites, not a bowl. If your cat has a health problem or eats a special diet, ask your vet first.

## Why raw shrimp is a bad idea

Don't give your cat raw shrimp. Cornell does not recommend raw food for cats, either as meals or treats. It says raw foods can carry live bacteria, viruses, and parasites, and that there is no scientific proof they help cats. The ASPCA also warns that raw meat can carry germs like Salmonella and E. coli, which can make both pets and people sick.

The FDA says it is always best to cook seafood all the way through. Freezing can kill parasites, but it does not kill all harmful bacteria.

## Other kinds of shrimp

### Cooked shrimp

**Yes, if it's plain and fully cooked.** Steam or boil it with nothing added, let it cool, and offer a few small pieces. This is the safest form.

### Raw shrimp

**No.** Raw shrimp has the same germ risks as other raw seafood. Cooking is the better choice. If your cat already ate a little raw shrimp, watch for vomiting, diarrhea, or low energy, and call your vet if you see them or if you are worried.

### Shrimp shells and tails

**Peel them first.** None of the sources we read talk about shrimp shells or tails directly, so we can't give a firm rule. Hard, sharp bits are not easy to chew, and Merck says a blockage in the stomach or intestines is a common problem in cats. Taking the shell and tail off is the simple way to avoid the question.

Call your vet if your cat ate a shell or tail and then starts to:

- Throw up or refuse food
- Seem very tired
- Have diarrhea
- Seem to hurt when you touch the belly

Merck lists all of these as signs of a possible blockage.

### Fried, breaded, or seasoned shrimp

**No.** Shrimp cocktail, scampi, tempura, and shrimp cooked in garlic butter all add extras your cat doesn't need. The ASPCA says onions and garlic (along with chives) can irritate the stomach and damage red blood cells, which can lead to anemia, and that cats are especially sensitive.

## Can a cat be allergic to shrimp?

It can happen with any food. Merck says the most common food allergies in cats are fish, beef, chicken, and milk products. Shrimp is not on that list, and we found no source that gives numbers for it.

Signs of a food allergy include itching, small crusty bumps, hair loss, and red or swollen skin. Merck says the only reliable test is a special diet your vet guides you through. If your cat gets itchy after trying a new food, stop it and talk to your vet.

## How to serve shrimp safely

1. Buy plain shrimp with no seasoning.
2. Cook it all the way through until it is firm and opaque.
3. Peel it and take off the tail.
4. Let it cool, then cut it into small pieces.
5. Offer just a few bites. Try one new food at a time.
