Yes, dogs can eat a small piece of a plain tortilla, corn or flour. The ingredients are not poisonous to dogs. The
trouble is what comes with them: salt, fat, and onion or garlic. Our sources don't talk about tortillas by name, so this
answer comes from what they say about corn, wheat, and people food in general.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check the label first">
  Some tortillas, and most tortilla dishes, contain onion or garlic. Both can damage a dog's red blood cells. If your
  dog ate something with onion or garlic in it, call your vet. You can also call a poison hotline:

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Are corn tortillas okay for dogs?

Corn tortillas are made from corn, and the AKC says corn is fine for dogs in small amounts. It describes corn as a
source of protein, carbohydrates, fiber, and other nutrients. The AKC also says vets who study dog nutrition generally
consider corn a fine part of a balanced diet.

The AKC adds one note: make sure your dog isn't allergic to corn before you feed it. Try a small bite first.

## Are flour tortillas okay for dogs?

Flour tortillas are made from wheat. The AKC says the gluten in wheat isn't harmful to dogs by itself. But wheat is one
of the foods dogs react to most often. The AKC puts it third on the list, after beef and dairy.

Most dogs have no problem with it. If your dog gets repeated diarrhea or other stomach trouble after eating wheat, talk
to your vet.

## What makes a tortilla a bad choice for dogs?

A plain tortilla is simple. A tortilla dish is not. Watch for these:

- **Onion and garlic.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says these can damage red blood cells and cause anemia in dogs.
  Signs often show up days later, so watch your dog closely. Garlic is more toxic than onion. Read more in our guide to
  [whether dogs can eat garlic](/can-dogs-eat-garlic/).
- **Salt.** The ASPCA says too much salt can make dogs very thirsty and may lead to vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and
  seizures. Fried chips and salted shells add up fast.
- **Fat and processed foods.** The AKC says high-fat and processed foods should be limited, even when they aren't toxic.
  Butter, oil, and fried shells fall in this group.
- **Fillings.** Cheese, beans, meat, sauce, and seasoning mixes can all hide salt, onion, or garlic. Tacos, burritos,
  and quesadillas are not good dog snacks.

## How to give your dog a tortilla

1. Pick a plain tortilla. Check the ingredients for onion or garlic powder.
2. Tear off a small piece, about the size of a treat.
3. Skip butter, oil, salt, sauce, and fillings. The AKC says to share people food plain and in bite-size pieces.
4. Try one new food at a time. Then watch for stomach upset or itching over the next day or so.

The AKC says people foods can be occasional treats but should never replace your dog's balanced dog food. The AKC
doesn't give an exact amount of tortilla, so keep it small.

## What if my dog ate a whole tortilla?

Our sources don't say how much tortilla is too much, so call your vet for advice, especially if your dog is small.
Call sooner if the tortilla had onion,
garlic, lots of salt, or xylitol (a sweetener that is poisonous to dogs, which the ASPCA lists as a danger in some baked
goods).

See your vet right away if your dog vomits again and again, seems weak, has pale gums, or won't drink.
