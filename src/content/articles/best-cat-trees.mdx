## How to choose a cat tree

### Pick a stable tree

The ASPCA says cats want a post that won't shift or fall over when they use it. Look for a wide base and check the weight limit on the product page. If you have more than one cat, think about how much weight could be on the tree at once. Check the screws every so often. Armarkat's page for its Classic B6605 tree says screws need regular tightening, and the same habit makes sense for any tree.

### Think about height

Cornell Feline Health Center says a scratching post should be at least as tall as your cat standing on their hind legs with front legs stretched out. Measure your cat, then measure your room. Check the floor space too, since taller trees often have wider bases.

### Look at the scratching surfaces

The ASPCA says cats have their own likes. Some prefer vertical scratching and others prefer horizontal. They may also like different materials, such as sisal, carpet, wood or cardboard. Sisal rope is a common choice on trees. If your cat ignores one surface, try another.

### Check materials

Three of these trees use pressed wood with fleece or faux fur covers. The fourth is made of Scots pine wood. Read the product page for the model you pick.

### Choose a spot

The ASPCA suggests putting scratching posts where your cat likes to spend time. Putting one next to furniture your cat already scratches can give them a better place to scratch.

## Safety tips

- **Check the weight limit.** Don't go over the limit listed by the maker.
- **Look for wobble.** Shake the tree after you build it. If it moves, tighten the screws or move it to a flat floor.
- **Tighten screws now and then.** Loose screws can make a tree wobble.
- **Replace broken trees.** A shredded surface is fine, since many cats prefer it, according to the ASPCA. A wobbly or cracked tree is not.
- **Follow the instructions.** Assemble the tree with the tools and instructions that come with it.
