Yes, most cats can have a small bite of crab meat, but only if it is **plain and fully cooked**. Cats do not need crab. Skip the shell, the butter, the salt, and anything with garlic or onion. Treat it as a once-in-a-while snack, never a meal.

<Callout type="warn" title="Call your vet if your cat ate crab with garlic, onion, or a piece of shell">
  Garlic and onion can damage a cat's red blood cells. Shell bits can block the gut. Call your vet or an emergency
  clinic if your cat ate either, or if you see vomiting, tiredness, or not eating.

## Is cooked crab meat okay for cats?

Plain cooked crab meat is a reasonable treat in small amounts. None of the sources we read warn against it. They do warn against raw seafood and against the extras people add.

Cornell's Feline Health Center says cats are obligate carnivores. That means they rely on nutrients found only in animal products. Crab is an animal food, so it fits that rule. But a treat is still a treat. Cornell says treats are not nutritionally complete and should stay at 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories. A few small flakes is plenty.

Cook the crab all the way. The FDA says crab flesh is done when it turns firm, pearly, and opaque (not see-through). Let it cool before you offer it.

Keep it plain. The ASPCA says too much salt can cause heavy thirst and, in serious cases, vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. Crab dishes are often salty, so don't share leftovers from a seasoned boil.

## Can cats eat raw crab?

No. Skip raw crab. Cornell does not recommend raw food for cats. It says raw foods can carry bacteria, viruses, and parasites, and that there is no scientific proof they help cats. The ASPCA also says raw meat can carry germs like Salmonella and E. coli that can make pets and people sick.

The FDA also says to cook crab and most other seafood all the way through. It says crab spoils quickly, and that you should not eat seafood that smells sour, rancid, or fishy. Cooked seafood that smells like ammonia should be thrown out. Don't leave it out for more than 2 hours (1 hour if it is hotter than 90°F).

## Is crab shell safe for cats?

Take the meat out of the shell first. The sources we read do not talk about crab shells directly, so we can't give a firm rule. Hard, sharp pieces are not easy to chew. The Merck Veterinary Manual says a blockage in the stomach or intestines is a problem in cats, and it often causes vomiting and loss of appetite.

Watch for these signs if your cat swallowed shell:

- Vomiting
- Not eating
- Tiredness
- Loose stool
- A sore or swollen belly

Merck says many cats recover well when a blockage is found and treated quickly. If you see these signs, call your vet.

## Other kinds of crab

### Imitation crab

**Only a tiny plain bite, if at all.** Imitation crab is sold as a stand-in for crab, and the labels can list salt, starch, and other added ingredients. We did not find a veterinary source that rates imitation crab for cats, so we can't give a firm rule. Read the label. Skip anything with garlic, onion, or lots of salt. Cats are better off with their regular food.

### Crab cakes and seasoned crab

**No.** These usually have breading, salt, and seasoning, and some have onion or garlic. The ASPCA warns that onion and garlic can irritate the gut and damage red blood cells.

### Canned crab

**Better not.** We did not find a source that speaks about canned crab. Canned seafood is often packed in salt, so check the label first. If you use it, rinse it and give only a small bite. Cornell also warns against canned fish products made for people, which have been linked to nerve problems. That warning is about fish, not crab, but it is another reason to stick with cat food.

## Can cats be allergic to crab?

Any food can cause an allergy, but crab is not named as a common one. Merck says cats are most often allergic to fish, beef, chicken, and milk products. Allergy signs include itching, often around the head and neck, small crusty bumps, and hair loss on both sides of the body. If your cat itches after eating something new, stop giving it and talk to your vet.

## What to do if your cat ate crab

If it was a few bites of plain cooked crab, watch your cat for the day. Mild stomach upset can happen with any new food.

Call your vet if your cat:

- Ate crab with garlic, onion, or lots of seasoning
- Ate raw or spoiled crab
- Swallowed shell
- Keeps vomiting, won't eat, or seems very tired
