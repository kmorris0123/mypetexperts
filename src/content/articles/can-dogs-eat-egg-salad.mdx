Plain cooked egg is fine for dogs, but egg salad is not a good treat. It often has onion, chives, salt, and a lot of
mayonnaise. **A tiny lick with no onion is unlikely to hurt. Don't share it on purpose, and check what was in it.**

  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline, even if your dog seems fine. Have the recipe or label ready.

  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

## Is egg salad safe for dogs?

Not really. The egg is not the problem. The AKC says dogs can eat eggs when they are cooked and plain, with no oil,
butter, salt, or seasoning. Egg salad is the opposite of plain.

Recipes vary. Many include mayonnaise, salt, mustard, onion, celery, or chives. We found no vet source that talks about
egg salad itself. So the advice here comes from what the sources say about the foods inside it.

## Why can egg salad be bad for dogs?

### Onion, garlic, and chives

The ASPCA says onion, garlic, and chives can upset the stomach and damage red blood cells. That can lead to anemia,
which means the blood can't carry enough oxygen.

Cooking does not make them safe. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered forms can all cause
poisoning. It says the dried and powdered kinds, such as onion powder, are the ones most often linked to it. Garlic
is much stronger than onion.

### Salt

The ASPCA says too much salt can cause vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. It can also make a dog very thirsty
and make it pee more. A small lick has little salt. A bowl of deli egg salad has much more.

### Rich, fatty dressing

The Merck Veterinary Manual says eating table scraps is a common suspected cause of pancreatitis in dogs. That is a
painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ near the stomach. The sources we read do not name mayonnaise on its own.
But a creamy salad is a rich food, and a dog that isn't used to it may get an upset stomach.

## What are the signs to watch for?

After onion or garlic, the Merck Veterinary Manual says signs often don't show up until a few days later. Look for:

- Tiredness or weakness
- Not wanting to eat
- Fast breathing
- Yellow gums or skin
- Dark urine

For a rich or salty food, watch for vomiting, diarrhea, or belly pain. In mild cases of pancreatitis, Merck says a dog
may show few signs. In serious cases, a dog may stop eating, vomit, feel weak, and have belly pain.

## What if my dog ate egg salad?

1. **Check what was in it.** Look at the recipe or the container for onion, garlic, and chives.
2. **Note how much your dog ate** and when. Your vet or the hotline will ask.
3. **Call your vet or a hotline** if it had onion, garlic, or chives, or if your dog ate a lot.
4. **Watch your dog for the next few days.** Call your vet if you see any of the signs above.

If it was a small lick of a plain version, watch your dog. Call your vet if your dog vomits, has diarrhea, or seems
unwell.

## Other egg dishes

### Can dogs eat deviled eggs?

It is best to skip them. The yolk is mixed with mayonnaise, mustard, and often salt. We found no vet source on deviled eggs themselves. Based on the salt and fat they contain, a plain boiled
egg is a better choice. If a dog snags one, it is not likely a big problem, but check the recipe for onion or garlic.

### Can dogs eat raw egg in egg salad?

Egg salad is normally made with boiled eggs. If your recipe used raw egg, be careful. The AKC says raw eggs can carry
salmonella, a germ that can cause fever, vomiting, and diarrhea. It also says raw egg white can block biotin, a vitamin.
Always cook eggs before you feed them to your dog.

## What can I give my dog instead?

A plain boiled egg is the easiest swap. Boil it fully, let it cool, peel it, and give a small piece. Skip the salt and
butter. The AKC says there is no set number of eggs for every dog. Size, age, activity, and health all matter. Eggs are
high in calories, so they should not be a daily food unless your vet says so.
