A tiny piece of ripe, peeled mango is unlikely to hurt most cats, but they don't need it. Cats are meat eaters, and
mango is mostly sugar and water. Never let your cat chew the pit or eat the skin. If you share any, make it a lick or
one small bite, once in a while.

Please note: we did not find a veterinary source that gives cat-specific feeding advice for mango. What follows comes
from general cat nutrition guidance, plus pit and skin warnings from sources about dogs and other fruit. We say so each
time. If your cat has a health problem like diabetes, ask your vet first.

## Why mango isn't a food your cat needs

Cats are carnivores, which means they eat mainly meat. The Merck Veterinary Manual says a small amount of
carbohydrates isn't necessarily bad for cats, but it also tells owners to avoid table scraps. The best food for your
cat is a complete and balanced cat food.

Cornell's Feline Health Center says cats need nutrients found only in animal products. Mango can't replace any of
them. It is a treat at best.

<Callout type="warn" title="Mango is not a meal">
  Cornell says treats should be no more than 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories. For mango, that means a
  very small amount. Most of the food your cat eats should be cat food.
</Callout>

## Is the mango pit dangerous for cats?

Yes, keep the pit away from your cat. We found no cat-specific page about mango pits, so this part comes from related
sources:

- The American Kennel Club says mango pits can be a choking hazard and can get stuck in a dog's digestive tract. It
  also says the pit has small amounts of cyanide, a poison. That page is about dogs, but a cat can choke or get a
  blockage in the same way.
- Pet Poison Helpline says a small dog or cat that swallows a peach pit may get a blockage in the gut. Mango pits are
  large and hard, like peach pits. It also says cats are unlikely to get cyanide poisoning from a pit, because they
  don't chew food well enough to release it. That page is about peaches, not mango.

If your cat swallowed a pit, or is chewing on one, call your vet or Pet Poison Helpline right away.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat swallowed a mango pit">
  Call your vet or an emergency clinic now. Throwing up, drooling, not eating, a sore belly, or trouble pooping can
  mean a blockage.

## How to serve mango to a cat safely

If you decide to share a bite:

1. Pick ripe, plain, fresh mango.
2. Peel it. The AKC says mango skin is hard for dogs to digest, and it suggests peeling the fruit first. We found no
   cat-specific advice, but peeling is a sensible step.
3. Cut away every bit of the pit and its stringy flesh.
4. Cut a piece the size of a pea or smaller.
5. Watch your cat for the next day. Stop if you see vomiting, diarrhea, or your cat seems unwell.

## Other kinds of mango

### Can cats eat mangos (more than one)?

"Mango" and "mangos" are the same fruit. The same answer applies: a tiny bite of peeled fruit, and never the pit.

### Can cats eat mango skin?

No. The skin is tough, and the AKC says it is hard for dogs to digest. We found no source that says it is good for
cats, so peel it.

### Can cats eat dried or canned mango?

We could not find a veterinary source on this for cats, so we can't say what is safe. Check the label for added sugar
or other ingredients, and skip anything with xylitol. The ASPCA says this sweetener can drop blood sugar and harm the liver in pets. When in doubt, ask your
vet.

## What to do if your cat ate mango

A lick or a bite of plain flesh will most likely pass without any trouble. If your cat ate a lot, or has diarrhea or
vomiting, call your vet. If the pit was swallowed, follow the steps in the red box above.

## Other treats to try

Cats are meat eaters, so a treat made for cats suits them better than fruit. Keep treats small, and read our guide on
[foods that are toxic to cats](/foods-toxic-to-cats/) before sharing anything from your plate.
