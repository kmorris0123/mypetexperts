A tiny lick of plain applesauce is unlikely to hurt your cat. But cats don't need it, and it isn't a good regular treat.
**Skip any applesauce with added flavors or sweeteners.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says to avoid feeding cats table
scraps.

## Is applesauce toxic to cats?

Plain applesauce is not a poison for cats. The ASPCA says the stems, leaves and seeds of apple plants are toxic to cats
because they release cyanide, a poison. It does not list the fruit itself as a problem. Applesauce is cooked and mashed
fruit, so it should not hold those parts.

That said, we did not find a veterinary source that calls applesauce a recommended food for cats. The safest view is
that it is not harmful in a tiny amount, but it has no real purpose for a cat.

## Why don't cats need applesauce?

The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are carnivores. They need mostly meat, and a small amount of carbohydrate is not
necessarily harmful. A complete and balanced cat food already gives your cat what it needs. Merck says table scraps
should be avoided.

Merck also says obesity is a serious concern for cats. Too much food can lead to health problems that may shorten a
cat's life. Extra snacks add calories, so keep them small and rare.

## What should I check on the applesauce label?

Look at the ingredient list before you let your cat taste any:

- **Sugar-free products:** The ASPCA says xylitol, a sugar substitute found in many sugar-free foods, is not harmful to
  cats. It is very dangerous for dogs. If a dog lives in your home, keep sugar-free applesauce out of its reach.
- **Added flavors or sweeteners:** Some jars have extra ingredients. Because vets advise against table scraps, choose a
  jar with only apples on the label if you share any.
- **Mixed foods:** Applesauce in baked goods, pouches with other ingredients, or desserts is not plain applesauce. Skip
  it.

The ASPCA also warns that foods high in fat, salt, onions, garlic, and dairy can upset a pet's stomach. None of those
belong in a snack for your cat.

## How do I give my cat applesauce safely?

If you still want your cat to try some:

1. Pick plain applesauce with no added ingredients.
2. Offer a lick off a spoon, no more.
3. Watch your cat for the next day.
4. Stop if you see vomiting or diarrhea, and call your vet if it keeps going.

Many cats will sniff it and walk away. That is fine.

## What if my cat ate a lot of applesauce?

Plain applesauce is unlikely to be an emergency. Your cat may get an upset stomach. Call your vet if your cat keeps
throwing up, has diarrhea, or seems unwell.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate apple seeds, stems, or leaves">
  Call your vet or a poison hotline right away if you think your cat ate parts of an apple plant. The ASPCA lists these
  signs: brick-red gums, wide pupils, trouble breathing, and shock.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## What can I give my cat instead?

The best treat is a small piece of the cat's own complete food, or a treat made for cats. If you like to share fruit,
our guide on [whether cats can eat apples](/can-cats-eat-apples/) explains how to serve a tiny piece of plain apple
safely. Keep all treats small, since they should not replace your cat's meals.
