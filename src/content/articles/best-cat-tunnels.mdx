## How to choose a cat tunnel

### Match it to how your cat plays

Some cats like to dash through and ambush. Look for a longer tunnel, or one with several openings, like the Mad Cat shark tunnel. Cats who like to hide may prefer a short den, like the Burrow or the Camper. If your cat likes to rest after play, a tunnel with a cushion, like the Catit Vesper, gives them a spot.

### Check the size

Makers don't always list the opening size, so read the product page. Pick a tunnel your cat can walk through without squeezing. A shorter den, like the 20-inch Camper, is more of a hideout than a run.

### Think about noise and extras

Crinkle sounds, music and lights excite some cats and scare others. If you're unsure, pick a quiet tunnel or one with an off switch. For a wider look at toys, see [the best cat toys](/best-cat-toys/). If your cat also likes climbing, see [the best cat trees](/best-cat-trees/).

### Look at storage and cleaning

All five tunnels fold for storage, according to their makers. The Catit Vesper's cushion is part of the set. Read the care notes for each one before you wash it.

## Safety tips

- **Check dangling parts.** Cornell Feline Health Center says to avoid toys with small pieces or string-like parts, such as feathers and string, that a cat could bite off and swallow. Most of these tunnels have hanging toys, so look them over often.
- **Supervise play.** KONG says to use the Rave Cave only with supervision and to inspect it often.
- **Stop using damaged tunnels.** KONG says to stop using the Rave Cave if it is damaged. It is sensible to do the same with any tunnel.
- **Open pop-up tunnels with care.** The KONG Camper pops open suddenly. Open it away from your face and from fragile things.
- **Keep the play area clear.** Cornell says to keep heavy objects that could fall away from where your cat plays, and to watch for high spots where a distracted cat could fall.
- **Rotate toys.** Cornell suggests rotating toys so your cat doesn't get bored.
- **Skip toys with cords.** Cornell says to avoid toys with electrical cords that a cat could chew.
