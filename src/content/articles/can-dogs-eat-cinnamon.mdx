Yes, dogs can have a tiny pinch of cinnamon. The ASPCA lists it as non-toxic to dogs. The risk comes from **large
amounts**, cinnamon **essential oil**, and powder your dog breathes in. It also isn't a food your dog needs, so
treat it as an extra, not a regular snack.

  Call your vet if your dog ate a lot of cinnamon, breathed in a cloud of the powder, or got into cinnamon oil. If

  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

## Why is cinnamon mostly okay for dogs?

The ASPCA classifies cinnamon as non-toxic to dogs, cats, and horses. The AKC agrees that the spice itself is not
poisonous. Pet Poison Helpline says it takes more than about **one teaspoon of powder** to cause problems in most
pets. Smaller dogs may react to less.

That does not mean it's good for dogs. The AKC says cinnamon can irritate the mouth and stomach, especially in larger
amounts. It doesn't recommend it as a regular treat.

## What happens if a dog eats too much cinnamon?

According to the AKC and Pet Poison Helpline, a large dose can cause:

- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Changes in heart rate
- Low blood sugar
- Liver disease

Powder is a special problem. If your dog sniffs or breathes in a cloud of it, your dog may cough, choke, or have
trouble breathing.

## Is cinnamon oil safe for dogs?

No. Essential oil is the form most likely to cause trouble. The AKC says it can cause problems at much smaller doses
than the powder. Keep oil bottles, diffusers, and cinnamon-scented products where your dog can't reach them. Call your
vet if your dog licks or swallows any.

## Other kinds of cinnamon

### Cinnamon sticks

Sticks are not poisonous, but the AKC warns that chewing on them can irritate your dog's mouth. Keep them out of
reach, and don't give them as a chew.

### Ground cinnamon

A light dusting on food is fine. Never pour it out in a pile. Dogs that stick their nose in a pile can breathe in
the powder.

### Cinnamon in baked goods

The small amount of cinnamon in most baked goods is not likely to hurt your dog. The AKC says the bigger danger is
other ingredients in the treat, such as xylitol (a sweetener that is very harmful to dogs), chocolate, or raisins. Read
the label before sharing. Our guides to [chocolate](/can-dogs-eat-chocolate/) and [grapes and raisins](/can-dogs-eat-grapes/)
explain why.

### Nutmeg

Nutmeg often sits next to cinnamon in the spice rack, and it is not the same. It contains a substance called
myristicin. Pet Poison Helpline says the small amount in a recipe is very unlikely to cause serious harm. A very large
amount can cause hallucinations, a fast heartbeat, belly pain, and seizures that may last up to 48 hours. Call your vet
if your dog ate a lot of nutmeg.

## What to do if your dog ate cinnamon

1. **Take away what's left** and note about how much was eaten.
2. **Check what else was in it.** Look for xylitol, chocolate, raisins, or nutmeg.
3. **Call your vet** if it was a large amount, if your dog breathed in powder, or if it was oil. Have the label ready.
4. **Watch for signs** such as vomiting, diarrhea, coughing, or unusual tiredness.

If it was a small lick or sprinkle, the sources above suggest serious problems are unlikely. Call your vet if you
are unsure.
