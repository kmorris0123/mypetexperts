Yes, cats can have a small bite of apple flesh once in a while. The seeds, stem and leaves are the problem: the ASPCA says they contain cyanide, a poison. Cats don't need fruit, so treat apple as an extra, not a meal.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate apple seeds, stems or leaves">
  A few seeds are unlikely to cause trouble, but call your vet or a poison hotline if your cat ate several, chewed leaves or stems, or seems unwell.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why are some parts of the apple a problem for cats?

The ASPCA lists the stems, leaves and seeds of apples as toxic to cats. They hold compounds that release cyanide, especially when the plant is wilting. The flesh of the fruit is not on that list.

Pet Poison Helpline adds some calm context. Cyanide sits inside hard seeds, and cats and dogs rarely crack them open. For cats, the more likely result of swallowing seeds is mild stomach upset. Even so, it is easy to cut seeds out, so do it every time.

## What are the signs of apple poisoning in cats?

The ASPCA lists these signs after a cat eats toxic apple parts:

- Gums and inside of the mouth that look brick red
- Wide pupils
- Trouble breathing or fast panting
- Shock

Any of these is an emergency. Call a vet right away.

## How do I serve apple to my cat?

If you want to share, keep it simple:

1. Wash the apple.
2. Cut out the core, seeds and stem.
3. Offer one or two tiny, bite-size pieces of plain flesh.
4. Watch your cat. Stop if you see vomiting or diarrhea.

Don't give your cat apple leaves or twigs from a tree. Many cats will sniff a piece and walk away, and that is fine.

## How much apple is okay?

The sources we read do not give an exact amount, so we won't make one up. The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are carnivores and should eat a complete, balanced cat food. It advises against table scraps. A small bite now and then is plenty, and your cat's regular food should stay the main meal.

Talk with your vet first if your cat has diabetes or another health problem. Apples have natural sugar.

## Other kinds of apple

### Can cats eat applesauce?

Plain applesauce in a tiny lick is the same as plain apple flesh: not toxic, but not needed. Check the label first. Many jars have added sugar, spices or other flavors. The ASPCA warns that xylitol, a sweetener found in many products, is dangerous for pets. Skip any applesauce with sweeteners or extras. Never give your cat a whole cup. A lick is enough.

### Can cats eat green apples?

Yes, the same rules apply. Green apples are still apples, and the sources we read do not treat them differently. The flesh is fine in a small bite. The seeds, stem and leaves are not. Some cats may not like the tart taste.

### Can cats eat apple pie, apple chips or apple juice?

We did not find vet sources on these, so we can't call them safe. The Merck Veterinary Manual advises against table scraps, and baked goods and dried snacks often have added sugar or other ingredients. Stick with a plain piece of fresh apple.

## What should my cat eat instead?

Your cat gets everything it needs from a complete cat food. If you want a treat, ask your vet about treats made for cats.
