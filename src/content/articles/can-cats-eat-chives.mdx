No, cats should not eat chives. Chives belong to the onion family, and the ASPCA lists them as toxic to cats. The
family can damage a cat's red blood cells and cause a low blood count called anemia. Fresh, dried, and cooked chives all
count.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate chives">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline, even if your cat seems fine. Signs can take days to show.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why are chives bad for cats?

Chives are in the *Allium* group, along with onions, garlic, and leeks. The ASPCA names a chemical in these plants,
N-propyl disulfide, as the cause. It breaks down red blood cells. Red blood cells carry oxygen around the body, so
fewer of them leaves a cat weak.

The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are the most sensitive animals to this problem, more than dogs. It also says garlic
and onions are the ones most often linked to poisoning. It doesn't give a separate amount for chives. That means no
one can say how many chives are too many. The safest plan is none.

## Does cooking or drying make chives okay?

No. Merck says cats and dogs have been harmed by raw, cooked, dried, and powdered onion and garlic. Merck also says
concentrated forms, such as dried flakes, powders, and dry onion soup mixes, are the ones most often linked to
poisoning in cats and dogs.

Chives can hide in many foods:

- Baked potatoes with sour cream and chives
- Cream cheese, dips, and spreads
- Scrambled eggs and omelets
- Soups and sauces
- Seasoning blends and chive-flavored snacks

Check the label before you share a bite of anything flavored.

## What signs should I watch for?

Merck says the blood damage usually shows up **3 to 5 days** after exposure, and signs often appear only after a lot of
damage has happened. That is why waiting to see if your cat gets sick is a bad plan. The sources list these signs:

- Weakness and tiredness
- Pale gums, or gums and eyes that look yellow
- Not wanting to eat
- Fast breathing or a fast heartbeat
- Dark or reddish urine
- Vomiting
- Collapse in serious cases

Pet Poison Helpline adds that you may smell onion or garlic on the breath.

## What should I do if my cat ate chives?

Call your vet or a poison hotline right away, and have this ready:

1. What your cat ate (fresh, dried, or in a food) and about how much
2. When it happened
3. Your cat's weight

Merck says treatment is best started early. If your cat ate chives very recently, the vet may give a medicine to make
your cat vomit. Cats with serious signs may need oxygen, IV fluids, or a blood transfusion. Pet Poison Helpline says
the outlook is excellent when treatment starts early.

## How can I keep chives away from my cat?

- Keep chive pots on a high shelf, or grow herbs your cat can't reach.
- Don't share table scraps that might contain onion, garlic, or chives.
- Put garnish, cut herbs, and leftovers away before you leave the kitchen.
- Read labels on dips, sauces, and seasoning mixes.
