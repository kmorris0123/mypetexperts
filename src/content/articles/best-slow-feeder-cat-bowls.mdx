## How to choose a slow feeder for your cat

### Match it to the food

Check what the maker says the feeder can hold. Some list wet food, some are for kibble. A flat bowl or lick mat is easier for wet food than a tall feeder with openings.

### Pick the right difficulty

Start easy. A feeder that frustrates your cat may end with a cat that gives up or knocks it over. Adjustable designs, like the Food Tree's three settings, let you raise the challenge over time.

### Check size and cleaning

Think about where it will sit. The two Catit feeders are much bigger than a bowl. Look for parts that come apart or a note about the dishwasher. Maker pages differ: Outward Hound says top rack only, LickiMat says hand wash.

### Does your cat need one?

Cornell says leaving dry food out all the time can lead some cats to overeat and gain weight. It says feeding at set times with controlled portions can help. A slow feeder can make those meals last longer, but it won't fix a feeding plan on its own.

## Safety tips

- **Watch the first meals.** LickiMat advises supervising your cat for the first few uses, and it is a good habit with any new feeder.
- **Throw out damaged feeders.** Outward Hound says to replace its feeder if it becomes damaged.
- **Use it for cats only.** Outward Hound says its bowl is not a chew toy or a children's toy.
- **Keep water out.** Cornell says fresh water should always be available, especially for cats eating only dry food.
- **Replace food left out.** Cornell says to replace any food left out each day.
- **Weight loss needs your vet.** Cornell says weight-loss programs should be run under veterinary direction, with gradual loss. Call your vet if your cat throws up often or loses weight.
- **Feed cats apart.** In multi-cat homes, Cornell says separate feeding areas may help.
