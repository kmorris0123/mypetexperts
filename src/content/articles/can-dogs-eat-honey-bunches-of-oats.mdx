A few dry pieces of Honey Bunches of Oats will not poison most dogs, but this cereal is not a good snack. It is a
sweet people food, and your dog does not need it. Skip the milk, and **check the label for
raisins, chocolate, or xylitol** before you share anything.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate cereal with raisins, chocolate, or xylitol">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline right away, even if your dog seems fine.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Is Honey Bunches of Oats bad for dogs?

It is not on the ASPCA's list of foods to avoid, and we found no vet source that names it as a danger. But we also
found no vet source that recommends it. A cereal like this is a sweet people food, and the ingredients differ from one
mix to the next. Read the label on your box before you decide.

Dogs do better on a balanced dog food. The AKC says dogs should get most of their food from a balanced commercial diet.
Anything else is an extra.

## What should I check on the cereal label?

Look for these ingredients. Each one can be a problem for dogs:

- **Raisins.** The ASPCA says grapes and raisins can cause kidney damage in dogs. Experts suspect tartaric acid, which
  dogs cannot process well.
- **Chocolate.** The ASPCA says chocolate contains methylxanthines. They can cause vomiting, diarrhea, tremors,
  seizures, and even death. Dark chocolate is the riskiest.
- **Macadamia nuts.** The ASPCA says these can cause weakness, shaking, vomiting, and a high body temperature.
- **Xylitol.** This is a sugar substitute. Pet Poison Helpline says it can drop a dog's blood sugar and harm the liver.
  It is more likely in "sugar-free" foods than in regular cereal.

## Is the milk a problem?

Yes, it can be. The ASPCA says dairy can cause diarrhea or other stomach upset in dogs. The AKC says many dogs are
lactose intolerant. That means they have trouble digesting milk sugar. A full bowl of milk at once can lead to loose
stool, gas, vomiting, or belly pain.

The AKC notes that milk has natural sugars and fat too. Don't let your dog lick the bowl.

## How much cereal can a dog eat?

No vet source we read gives a safe amount of this cereal, so we won't make one up. If you want to share, keep it to
**one or two dry pieces**, once in a while. Don't use cereal as a meal.

If your dog gets loose stool or vomits after eating cereal, stop feeding it. Call your vet if the signs keep going.

## What is the problem with the oats in it?

Not much. Plain oats are not the worry. The AKC says plain, cooked oatmeal is fine in small amounts. But it warns that
flavored oatmeal often has more sugar and can have xylitol. A sweet cereal mix with oats in it is closer to flavored
oatmeal than to plain oats.

## What should I do if my dog ate a lot of cereal?

1. Check the box for raisins, chocolate, nuts, and xylitol.
2. If any of those are in it, call your vet or a poison hotline now. Have the box handy.
3. If it was plain cereal, watch your dog for vomiting or diarrhea over the next day.
4. Call your vet if your dog seems tired, will not eat, or the signs keep going.

## What can I give my dog instead?

Plain cooked oatmeal is a better pick. The AKC suggests about 1 tablespoon of cooked oatmeal for every 20 pounds of
your dog's weight. See our guide to [whether dogs can eat oatmeal](/can-dogs-eat-oatmeal/). For a full list of foods to
avoid, see [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
