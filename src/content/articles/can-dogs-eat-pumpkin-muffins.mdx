It's better not to feed your dog pumpkin muffins. Plain pumpkin is fine for dogs, but a muffin also has sugar, fat, and spices. Some muffins have xylitol, a sugar substitute that is extremely toxic to dogs. A crumb is unlikely to hurt a healthy dog. A whole muffin is a reason to call your vet.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate a muffin made with xylitol, chocolate, or raisins">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline right away. Don't wait for signs.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit unless a vet tells you to. For xylitol, the vomiting can make low blood sugar worse.
</Callout>

## Is pumpkin itself okay for dogs?

Yes. The AKC says dogs can eat plain pumpkin, and most enjoy it. It describes pumpkin as low in fat and high in fiber. The can should say "100% pumpkin" and nothing else.

The muffin is the problem, not the pumpkin.

## Why are pumpkin muffins a problem for dogs?

A muffin has several things that plain pumpkin doesn't.

- **Sugar, butter, and oil.** The AKC says the sugar, fat, and dairy in pumpkin pie can upset a dog's stomach. Too much butter or shortening can cause stomach pain or pancreatitis, a painful swelling of the pancreas. The AKC page is about pie, not muffins. Muffins are made with similar ingredients, so the same worry applies.
- **Spices.** Pumpkin spice is a mix of cinnamon, ginger, nutmeg, allspice, and cloves. The AKC advises avoiding pumpkin spice foods for dogs.
- **Nutmeg.** Pet Poison Helpline says the small amount in a recipe is very unlikely to cause serious harm, though it may upset the stomach. A very large amount can cause signs such as confusion, a fast heartbeat, and seizures. The page says this is very unlikely from food that contains nutmeg.
- **Add-ins.** Chocolate chips, raisins, and nuts are common in muffins. These can be dangerous on their own (see below).

## What about xylitol in muffins?

Xylitol is the biggest worry. Pet Poison Helpline says xylitol is found in baked goods, gum, and candy, and that it is extremely toxic to dogs. Check the label of any sugar-free or "low sugar" muffin or mix.

It can make a dog's blood sugar drop as soon as 10 to 15 minutes after eating it. Signs include:

- Vomiting
- Weakness
- Trouble standing or walking
- Shaking
- Seizures

Xylitol can also cause liver failure. Smaller dogs are at greater risk. Call a vet or the hotline right away, even if your dog seems fine.

## Which muffin add-ins are dangerous?

The ASPCA lists these as dangerous for dogs:

- **Chocolate.** Darker chocolate is riskier. Baking chocolate and cocoa powder are the most concentrated.
- **Raisins and grapes.** These can cause kidney damage. See our guide on [whether dogs can eat grapes](/can-dogs-eat-grapes/).
- **Macadamia nuts.** These can cause weakness, vomiting, and shaking.

## What should I do if my dog ate a pumpkin muffin?

1. **Read the ingredients.** Look for xylitol, chocolate, raisins, and macadamia nuts. Keep the package or recipe.
2. **Work out how much was eaten.** Note the amount and your dog's size.
3. **Call your vet or a hotline** if any of those ingredients were in it, if your dog ate a whole muffin, or if your dog is small.
4. **Watch your dog.** For a plain muffin, a bite or two may cause nothing. Call your vet if your dog vomits, has diarrhea, or seems tired or in pain.

## What can I share instead?

Give plain pumpkin. Mix a spoonful of 100% canned pumpkin into your dog's food. The AKC says that with your vet's approval, plain pumpkin puree can also help with diarrhea or constipation. Avoid pie filling.

You can also bake a dog treat from plain pumpkin. Skip the sugar, spices, and any sweetener. Check our guide on [whether dogs can eat blueberry muffins](/can-dogs-eat-blueberry-muffins/) for another baked treat. See also our list of [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).

Treats should be a small part of your dog's day. A common vet guideline is no more than 10% of daily calories.
