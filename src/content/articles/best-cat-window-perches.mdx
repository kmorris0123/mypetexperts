## How to choose a cat window perch

### Check your cat's weight against the rating

Every maker lists a weight limit. K&H lists 50 pounds for its perches, Tuft + Paw lists 30, and Kitty Cot lists 25. Pick a perch rated well above your cat's weight.

### Measure your glass first

Each perch needs a certain amount of open, flat glass. K&H's single hammock needs at least 29.5" by 14" by 9.5", and it says to avoid textured, louvered or divided-pane windows. Kitty Cot lists small, medium and large widths, so it can suit narrower windows. Measure before you buy.

### Think about blinds and extra features

Some perches fold or unclip so you can still close shades. A removable, washable cover makes cleanup easier. Extras like a cat grass planter or an enclosed level are nice, but they need more window space.

### Know how it attaches

All five picks here attach to glass with suction cups. K&H and Kitty Cot say no tools are needed.

## Safety tips

- **Check the suction cups often.** K&H says to clean the cups and the glass once a month to keep a strong hold. Look at the cups each time you clean, and stop using a perch if a cup won't stick.
- **Keep windows screened.** The ASPCA says cats like to sit up high but can have trouble gripping ledges. It says falls often happen when a cat fixates on a bird and loses balance, and that falls can cause serious injuries. It recommends snug, sturdy screens on every window.
- **Don't count on window guards.** The ASPCA warns that childproof guards may not stop a cat from slipping through.
- **Call your vet after a fall.** The ASPCA says cats that get prompt veterinary care after a fall have a much better chance of surviving. If your cat falls, find them quickly and take them to a vet.
- **Follow the weight limit.** Don't let more cats on a perch than the maker's rating allows.
