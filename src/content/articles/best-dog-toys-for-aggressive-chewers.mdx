## How to choose a toy for an aggressive chewer

### Pick the right size

The AKC says a toy should be sized for your dog so they can't swallow it whole. Check the weight range on the package. KONG adds that power chewers may need to go up one or two sizes. Goughnuts and Benebone also list sizes by dog weight.

### Look at the material

Natural rubber, nylon and Zogoflex are the main tough materials in this guide. Each brand makes its own durability claims, and we can't verify them. The AKC says to avoid toys that break apart easily, because swallowed pieces are a risk.

### Check the shape

The AKC says rubber toys should have a hole at each end. That stops suction from trapping your dog's tongue.

### Think about how your dog plays

Some dogs like puzzles and treats, so a stuffable toy fits. Others want to fetch or tug. Pick a toy for the way your dog plays, so they use it as intended.

## Safety tips

- **Supervise chewing.** KONG, West Paw and Benebone all say to supervise.
- **Inspect toys often.** The AKC says to throw away any toy that is damaged or losing pieces. Pieces can cause choking or a blockage.
- **Replace worn toys.** Benebone says to replace its chews when worn. KONG and West Paw say to stop using a damaged toy.
- **Remove all packaging first.** KONG says to take off packaging before giving a toy to your dog.
- **Don't count on "indestructible."** West Paw says no dog toy is indestructible.
- **Skip real bones.** Toys like Hurley are meant as an alternative. Read why in [can dogs eat cooked bones](/can-dogs-eat-cooked-bones/).
- **Call your vet** if your dog swallows a piece of a toy or acts sick afterward.
