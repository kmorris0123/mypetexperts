A little fresh wheatgrass is unlikely to hurt most cats. The sources we read do not call it a poison, but the ASPCA has no
toxicity details for it. The ASPCA also says **any plant can upset a cat's belly**. Offer only a small amount, grown
without chemicals.

## Is wheatgrass safe for cats?

The ASPCA keeps a plant list for cats. Its wheat grass page does not give a toxicity rating or a list of signs. So we
can't say the ASPCA has cleared it. What the page does say is this: eating any plant material may cause vomiting and
an upset stomach in dogs and cats.

That is the honest summary. Wheatgrass is not named as a danger, but there is no strong vet source that says it is a
good snack. Treat it as an extra, not as food.

## Why do cats like wheatgrass?

Cornell Feline Health Center says cats that chew houseplants are often indoor cats with little access to grass or other
greens. The article says they may be "craving vegetable matter." Chewing can also be part of play and exploring.

Cornell suggests giving a cat oat grass, catnip, or catmint to chew. It warns that a cat may keep snacking on
houseplants anyway. We did not find a vet source that says why cats prefer wheatgrass over other grasses.

## How should I serve wheatgrass to my cat?

These steps follow the points in the sources we read:

- **Offer a small amount.** Wheatgrass is a plant, and the ASPCA says plants can upset the belly. Start with a little
  and see how your cat does.
- **Use a pot you grow yourself, or one made for pets.** You know what went into it.
- **Skip anything sprayed.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says plants treated with weed killer at the label rate are
  generally not hazardous, and the risk drops once the product dries. Still, it says fresh weed killer on plants can
  irritate the eyes, skin, and nose, and can make pets vomit or wobble. Read the label, and keep your cat away from
  anything freshly treated.
- **Keep dangerous plants out of reach.** Cornell lists lilies, English ivy, philodendron, oleander, and poinsettia as
  plants that can be deadly. The list is not complete. If your cat likes to chew leaves, block access to your other plants.

## What if my cat throws up after eating wheatgrass?

One short bout of vomiting is a common reaction to eating plants, and the ASPCA names it as a possible effect. The Merck
Veterinary Manual says to see your vet if vomiting lasts longer than 1 to 2 days or happens more than once or twice a
day.

Merck also says to get your cat checked soon if vomiting comes with any of these:

- Blood in the vomit
- Belly pain
- Weakness or low energy
- Dehydration
- Fever
- Weight loss

Merck says some causes of long-lasting or severe vomiting can be life-threatening. It also says to see your vet if
hairballs or vomiting keep happening.

## Other kinds of wheatgrass

### Wheatgrass juice, powder, and pills

The sources we read do not mention these for cats. Cat grass is meant to be chewed fresh. Ask your vet before you give
a supplement.

### Regular lawn grass

Our guide on [whether cats can eat grass](/can-cats-eat-grass/) covers lawn grass, chemicals, and why cats vomit after
eating it.

## What can my cat chew instead?

Cornell suggests oat grass, catnip, or catmint. It also suggests adding a little lettuce or parsley to your cat's bowl,
or switching to a higher-fiber cat food. Talk to your vet before changing your cat's diet. To learn which foods to keep
away, see our list of [foods that are toxic to cats](/foods-toxic-to-cats/).
