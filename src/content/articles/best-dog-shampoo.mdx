## How to choose a dog shampoo

### Use a shampoo made for dogs

The AKC says to avoid human shampoo. Its pH won't be right for a dog, and it can irritate the skin. Look for a label that says it is made for dogs.

### Match the shampoo to the coat

The AKC notes that single-coated dogs, like Greyhounds, may need baths less often. Double-coated dogs, like Golden Retrievers and German Shepherds, need a shampoo that reaches through the thick undercoat to the skin.

### Look at the ingredients

The AKC suggests soap-free shampoos that soothe and hydrate the skin and coat. It lists aloe vera, oatmeal, vitamin E and coconut oil as helpful ingredients. It also says to avoid artificial fragrances, parabens and dyes, which can cause allergic reactions or other problems. If your dog is sensitive, a fragrance-free formula is an easy place to start.

### Know when to ask your vet

The AKC says vets recommend medicated shampoos for specific needs, such as itching, fungus or bacteria. If your dog scratches a lot or has sore skin, talk to your vet before picking a shampoo yourself.

## Safety tips

- **Read the label for age limits.** The picks above list different minimum ages, from 6 weeks to 12 weeks. Hartz says its shampoo is for dogs of all ages.
- **Keep shampoo out of eyes and mouth.** Wahl's label says to avoid contact with the eyes and mouth.
- **Rinse well.** The AKC says to rinse again even when you think all the suds are gone.
- **Check flea treatments.** Some makers say their shampoo won't wash off topical flea products. Read the label before bath day.
- **Call your vet about skin problems.** Swollen, scabbed or very itchy skin needs a vet visit, not just a new shampoo.
