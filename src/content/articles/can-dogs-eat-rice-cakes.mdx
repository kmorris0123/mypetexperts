Yes, most dogs can have a small bite of a plain rice cake once in a while. It is made from rice, and plain rice is not
poisonous to dogs. But **flavored, sweet, or sugar-free rice cakes can be dangerous**, so check the label first.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate a flavored or sugar-free rice cake">
  Look at the ingredients. If you see **xylitol** (a sugar substitute), call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison
  hotline right away. Do this even if your dog seems fine.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Are plain rice cakes safe for dogs?

Plain rice cakes are probably fine in small bites. The AKC says plain rice is okay for dogs, as long as nothing is
added to it. The AKC also says dogs already get carbohydrates from their regular food. Extra rice can add weight if you
give too much.

We did not find a veterinary source that talks about rice cakes by name. So this advice comes from what vets say about
plain rice and about the things people add to snacks. A rice cake gives your dog very little that their dog food does
not already give.

## Which rice cakes should dogs avoid?

Skip any rice cake with added flavor, sweetness, or toppings. The AKC warns that seasonings and spices can upset a dog's
stomach or be toxic. Here is what to look for on the label:

- **Xylitol.** The FDA says this sugar substitute is found in some baked goods, sugar-free sweets, and some nut
  butters. It can make a dog's blood sugar drop fast.
- **Onion or garlic.** The ASPCA says these can upset the stomach and harm red blood cells. Savory flavors like
  cheddar or ranch may include them.
- **Chocolate.** The ASPCA says chocolate can make dogs sick. Dark chocolate and cocoa carry the most risk.
- **Lots of salt.** The ASPCA says too much salt can cause thirst, vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures.

## What does xylitol do to dogs?

The FDA says xylitol can cause low blood sugar in dogs within 10 to 60 minutes. Signs include vomiting, then weakness,
a wobbly walk, collapse, and seizures. Sometimes the worst effects take 12 to 24 hours to show. The ASPCA adds that
xylitol can also damage the liver.

If you think your dog ate xylitol, get help right away. Don't wait for signs.

## How much rice cake can a dog eat?

No source we read gives an exact amount for rice cakes, so we won't make one up. Keep it to a small bite, not a whole
cake, and only once in a while. The AKC says to talk with your vet before adding rice to your dog's diet and to start
slowly. Watch for loose stool or vomiting after a new food.

## Other kinds of rice cakes

### Can dogs eat flavored rice cakes?

No, it is better to skip them. Flavors can come with salt, sugar, onion, garlic, chocolate, or xylitol. Some of these
can make a dog very sick.

### Can dogs eat rice cakes with peanut butter or other toppings?

Only plain peanut butter with no xylitol, and only a thin layer. The FDA tells owners to read nut butter labels first.
Our guide to [peanut butter for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-peanut-butter/) explains what to check.

### Can dogs eat mini rice cakes or puffed rice?

Plain ones follow the same rule as a plain rice cake: a small amount, once in a while, no flavor. Read the label for
added salt or sweeteners.

## What to give instead

If you want a crunchy snack, plain boiled rice is the option the AKC describes, with no seasoning. Our guide on
[whether dogs can eat rice](/can-dogs-eat-rice/) covers it.

Ask your vet how many treats fit your dog's diet. For a longer list of things to avoid, see
[foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
