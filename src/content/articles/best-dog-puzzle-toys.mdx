## How to choose a dog puzzle toy

### Pick the right type

The AKC lists several kinds: hollow or fillable toys you can freeze, hard plastic mazes, and soft toys that release treats. Fillable toys like the Toppl suit dogs who lick and nudge. Board puzzles like the Nina Ottosson games suit dogs who like to paw and sniff. Weighted feeders like the Wobbler suit dogs who push things around.

### Match the toy to your dog

The AKC says to choose a toy that fits your dog's size and jaw strength. A small dog may do better with a simpler design. Start easy so your dog wins early. Then move up a level.

### Think about cleaning

Food gets stuck in puzzles. Dishwasher-safe toys are easier to clean. Hand-wash puzzles need to dry fully before you use them again.

### Consider a snuffle mat

A snuffle mat is a fabric pad where you hide treats. The AKC says it can slow fast eaters and give mental exercise. It also says a mat is not right for dogs who chew everything or have no interest in treats.

## Safety tips

- **Supervise.** Outward Hound and KONG both say to watch your dog. The AKC says its training-style puzzle toys are for treats, not for chewing on alone.
- **Stop using damaged toys.** KONG, West Paw and Outward Hound all say to stop using a toy that is damaged or broken.
- **Don't let your dog chew the pieces.** Outward Hound says to prevent chewing on puzzle pieces.
- **Count the calories.** The AKC says to track the treats or kibble you put in. You can use part of mealtime food instead of extra treats.
- **Put it away when empty.** Outward Hound says to store its puzzles when empty until the next supervised session.
- **Keep dogs apart.** If you have more than one pet, the AKC says to separate them while they use a snuffle mat.
- **Dogs only.** Outward Hound says its puzzles are for dogs, not children.

If your dog swallows a piece of a toy, call your vet.
