## How to choose cat nail clippers

### Pick a size made for cats

Cat nails are small and thin. A clipper made for cats, or sized for small nails, is easier to line up with the tip. Some brands, like Zen Clipper, sell several sizes. Others sell one size.

### Look at the blade design

Some clippers have a hole the nail slides through, with a blade that cuts in one stroke. Others, like the Zen Clipper, use a blade shaped to let in only the tip. Choose the style you feel most steady using. Clean, sharp stainless steel blades are a plus.

### Check the grip

You will hold a squirming cat in one hand and the clipper in the other. Look for non-slip handles and a shape that fits your hand. Safari lists a left-or-right-hand design on its standard trimmer, and a large finger hole on its deluxe one.

### How often to trim

The ASPCA says to trim a cat's nails every two to three weeks. Cornell says only to trim regularly, and notes that regular trims reduce damage from sharp claws. Cornell adds that trims lower the chance of a nail growing into the foot pad, which can cause infection.

## Safety tips

- **Trim only the tip.** Safari says to hold the paw firmly and trim just the tip of the nail. Avoid the quick, the sensitive part of the nail that holds a blood vessel.
- **Start early.** Cornell says nail care gets easier as your cat gets older, so get kittens used to it.
- **Ask your vet for a lesson.** Cornell suggests this if you are new to it.
- **Keep scratching outlets.** The ASPCA suggests a sturdy scratching post at least three feet tall, covered in rough material like sisal. See our guide to the [best cat scratching posts](/best-cat-scratching-posts/).
- **Call your vet** if a nail looks torn, swollen or infected, or if bleeding doesn't stop.
