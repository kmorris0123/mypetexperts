Plain shortbread is not poisonous to dogs, but it is not a good treat. It is mostly butter and sugar, and it gives your dog nothing useful. One small bite of a plain piece is unlikely to hurt a healthy dog. Shortbread with chocolate, raisins, nuts, or xylitol can be dangerous.

## Why is shortbread not a good treat for dogs?

Shortbread is made from flour, sugar, and a lot of butter. None of these is a poison for dogs. The problem is what a
rich, sugary snack can do to a dog's belly.

- **Sugar** can upset a dog's stomach, according to Pet Poison Helpline.
- **Fat** is a worry too. The Merck Veterinary Manual says eating lots of table scraps is a common trigger for
  pancreatitis. That is a painful swelling of the pancreas, a small organ that helps digest food.

Shortbread is also a snack your dog does not need. A dog that gets one piece may start begging for more.

## Which shortbread is dangerous for dogs?

Check the ingredients. Fancy or store-bought shortbread often has extras that are much riskier than the plain kind.

| Ingredient | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Chocolate (dipped or chips) | Contains theobromine and caffeine, which dogs clear from their bodies slowly. Dark and baker's chocolate are the strongest. |
| Raisins or currants | Grapes and raisins can hurt a dog's kidneys. The ASPCA says dogs can't process a suspected toxin in them. |
| Nuts | The ASPCA says nuts such as almonds, pecans, and walnuts are high in fat and can cause vomiting, diarrhea, and sometimes pancreatitis. Macadamia nuts can cause weakness, shaking, and vomiting. |
| Xylitol (in some sugar-free sweets) | Can drop blood sugar dangerously low and can harm the liver. |

For more on one of these, see our guides on [dogs and chocolate](/can-dogs-eat-chocolate/) and
[dogs and walnuts](/can-dogs-eat-walnuts/).

## What signs should I watch for?

If your dog ate plain shortbread, a belly upset is the main thing to watch for. Pancreatitis signs from the Merck
Veterinary Manual include:

- Throwing up
- Loss of appetite
- Belly pain
- Weakness
- Diarrhea

Mild cases may show only vague signs, like being tired or eating less. Call your vet if these last or get worse.

Signs from the risky ingredients are more serious:

- **Chocolate:** shaking, seizures, a fast or uneven heartbeat, restlessness.
- **Xylitol:** vomiting, weakness, trouble standing or walking straight, shaking, seizures.

## What should I do if my dog ate shortbread?

1. **Take the food away** and keep the package so you can read the label.
2. **Read the ingredients.** Look for chocolate, raisins, nuts, and sweeteners such as xylitol.
3. **Call your vet or a hotline right away** if you see any of those, or if you are not sure what was in it.
4. **Note how much your dog ate.** Vets and hotlines will ask.
5. **For a small bite of plain shortbread,** watch your dog for the next day. Call your vet if you see vomiting, diarrhea, or low energy.

## What about buttermilk biscuits?

Plain buttermilk biscuits are a lot like shortbread: mostly flour and butter. The veterinary sources I read do not
cover them by name, so use the same rules. They are not a good treat, and a small bite of a plain one is unlikely to
be a problem. Check for extras such as raisins or nuts. Biscuits topped with gravy, sausage, or other rich foods add
even more fat.

## What can I give my dog instead?

Keep it simple. A small piece of your dog's usual treats works well, and plain treats made for dogs are the easiest
choice. Ask your vet which people foods suit your dog, especially if your dog has had pancreatitis or other health
problems. For another baked food that gets the same questions, see our guide on
[bread for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-bread/).
