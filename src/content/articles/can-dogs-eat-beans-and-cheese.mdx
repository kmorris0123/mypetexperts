Better not. A tiny bite of plain beans and plain cheese is unlikely to hurt most dogs. But beans and cheese usually come as burritos, dips, or chili, and those can hold onion, garlic, lots of salt, and a lot of fat. Those extras are the real risk.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate beans and cheese with onion or garlic">
  Check the recipe or label. If it had onion, garlic, or chives, or your dog ate a lot, call your vet, an emergency
  clinic, or a poison hotline. Do this even if your dog seems fine, because onion problems can show up days later.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Are beans okay for dogs?

I did not find a vet source that covers plain cooked pinto, black, or refried beans. So there is no firm answer to give.

One related page helps. The AKC says plain edamame (young soybeans) is not toxic to dogs. It adds that a few beans as a treat will not harm most dogs. It also says soy can cause gas and sometimes diarrhea, and that too much fiber can lead to vomiting.

The AKC also says never to share beans cooked with salt, spices, or oil. Canned and refried beans often have these. Ask your vet before you share any beans, and give very little.

For a bean with better backing, see [green beans for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-green-beans/).

## Is cheese okay for dogs?

A little can be. The AKC says low-fat cheeses, such as mozzarella, cottage cheese, and soft goat cheese, are the safest picks. Cottage cheese is the best choice because it has less fat, salt, and lactose than most cheeses. Lactose is the sugar in milk, and some dogs can't digest it well.

Cheese is high in fat. The AKC says too much can lead to weight gain and pancreatitis. Pancreatitis is a painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ near the stomach. The AKC also says some cheeses have garlic, onion, or chives mixed in, so check the label. Our guide to [cheese for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-cheese/) has more.

## Why can beans and cheese dishes be a problem?

### Onion and garlic

The ASPCA says onion, garlic, and chives can upset the stomach and damage red blood cells. That can cause anemia, which means the blood can't carry enough oxygen.

Cooking does not make them safe. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered forms have all harmed dogs. Garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion. Signs often show up a few days later. They include tiredness, weakness, fast breathing, and dark urine.

Look for onion powder and garlic powder on labels of chili, taco seasoning, and dips.

### Salt

Merck says too much salt can make a dog vomit within a few hours. It can then lead to weakness, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. Canned beans, cheese sauce, and seasoning mixes can be salty. Always keep fresh water out for your dog.

### Fat

Merck says eating a lot of table scraps is a common risk for pancreatitis. Signs include not wanting to eat, vomiting, belly pain, weakness, and diarrhea. A little cheese is not the same as a plate of cheesy food, so keep portions tiny.

## Other versions

### Can dogs eat bean and cheese burritos?

No, not on purpose. A burrito has beans, cheese, a tortilla, and often sauce, rice, or seasoning. Each one adds a chance of onion, garlic, salt, or fat. Plain tortillas alone are covered in our guide to [tortillas and dogs](/can-dogs-eat-tortillas/).

### Can dogs eat pinto beans and rice?

I found no vet source on this dish. Plain rice and plain beans, with no salt, oil, or seasoning, would be the lowest-risk version, but ask your vet first.

## What if my dog ate beans and cheese?

1. **Check what was in it.** Look for onion, garlic, chives, a lot of salt, and sauce.
2. **Note how much your dog ate.** Your vet or a hotline will ask.
3. **Call your vet or a hotline** if it had onion or garlic, or if your dog ate a lot.
4. **Watch your dog for several days.** Call your vet for vomiting, diarrhea, belly pain, weakness, or dark urine.

If it was one small lick of something plain, watch your dog and call your vet if anything seems off.

## What can I share instead?

Small bits of low-fat cheese, such as cottage cheese or mozzarella, make a better treat. Plain green beans are another choice. Keep any treat small. For more foods to avoid, see our list of [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
