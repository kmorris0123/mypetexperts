Candy corn is not a good treat for dogs. Regular candy corn is mostly sugar and usually only upsets the belly, but Pet
Poison Helpline says dogs should not eat it, even a little. **Sugar-free candy corn is the real danger,** because it can
contain xylitol, a sweetener that is poisonous to dogs.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate sugar-free candy, chocolate candy, or a lot of candy">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline **right away**. Don't wait for signs. Have the bag and your
  dog's weight ready.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is candy corn bad for dogs?

Pet Poison Helpline gives three reasons to keep candy corn away from your dog.

### It is mostly sugar

Plain candy corn is not toxic. But a pile of sugar can upset a dog's stomach. A big sugary binge can also cause
pancreatitis, which is a painful swelling of the pancreas. Pet Poison Helpline says signs of pancreatitis may not show up
for two to four days.

### Sugar-free versions may have xylitol

Some sugar-free candy corn uses **xylitol** instead of sugar. It may also be listed as "birch sugar" or "wood sugar" on
the label. Xylitol is very dangerous for dogs. Pet Poison Helpline says it can make blood sugar drop fast, in as little
as 10 to 15 minutes. It can also cause sudden liver failure.

The Merck Veterinary Manual adds that xylitol is found in sugar-free gum, candy, mints, peanut butter, and baked goods.
It says dogs are the only common pet known to be harmed this way.

### Wrappers and chocolate come along for the ride

Dogs often swallow wrappers with the candy. Pet Poison Helpline warns that plastic, foil, or cellophane can block the
bowel and may need surgery. Some candy corn is also chocolate flavored, and Halloween bowls often mix candy corn with
chocolate. Our guide explains
[why chocolate is harmful for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-chocolate/).

## What signs should I watch for?

The signs depend on what your dog ate. Pet Poison Helpline lists these:

- **Too much sugar or pancreatitis:** vomiting, diarrhea, belly pain, eating less, and tiredness
- **Xylitol:** vomiting, weakness or stumbling, tiredness, shaking, seizures, and collapse. Later signs can point to
  liver damage.
- **A swallowed wrapper:** vomiting, eating less, straining to poop or not pooping, and tiredness

The Merck Veterinary Manual says low blood sugar from xylitol can start in about 30 minutes. Liver signs may not show up
until 24 to 48 hours later. A dog can look fine at first and still be in trouble.

## What should I do if my dog ate candy corn?

Pet Poison Helpline suggests these steps:

1. **Check the bag.** Is it regular or sugar-free? Look for xylitol or birch sugar on the label.
2. **Guess how much was eaten.** Note your dog's weight too.
3. **Look for missing wrappers** or a chewed bag.
4. **Keep the package** and put away any candy that is left.
5. **Call Pet Poison Helpline or your vet.** The helpline says to call even if your dog ate only one piece.

Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet or poison expert tells you to. Depending on the case, a vet may make your
dog vomit safely, check blood sugar, give fluids through a vein, or give other care.

## Is candy corn safe if it is the regular kind?

It is not toxic, and one piece is unlikely to be a big problem for most healthy dogs. But it has no value for your dog,
and Pet Poison Helpline says not to give it as a treat. Because the risk depends on the candy, your dog's size, and any
wrapper, a call to a vet or hotline is the only way to be sure.

## Candy corn and other corn foods

## What can I give my dog instead?

Pick treats made for dogs, or a few bites of dog-friendly foods. The ASPCA also warns that salty and fatty snacks can
upset a dog's stomach. Keep trick-or-treat bowls up high and shut. Tell kids not to share candy with the dog. For a full
list of what to avoid, see our guide to [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
