## How to choose a cat laser toy

### Decide if you want hands-free or hands-on

Automatic toys keep your cat busy while you do other things, and each one here turns off on a timer. Manual lasers take your time, but you decide when the game ends. That matters, as the next section explains.

### Think about the catch

A laser dot can't be caught. A 2021 survey of 618 cat owners, published in a peer-reviewed journal, found that more laser play went with more repetitive behaviors, like chasing lights and shadows or staring at lights. The authors say the study can't show that lasers cause these behaviors. They also say more research is needed. Only about a third of owners in the survey ended laser play by letting their cat catch something.

The common advice, which the authors describe but did not test, is to end by landing the light on a small prey-like toy or a treat. A manual laser makes that easy.

### Check power and run time

Each toy here uses batteries that are not included in the price. Check the type before you buy: AA for the PetSafe toys, and small LR44 batteries for the Catit. Auto shut-off times are 10 or 15 minutes, according to the makers. Your cat may need less than that.

## Safety tips

- **End with a catch.** Finish each game by landing the light on a toy or treat your cat can grab.
- **Keep sessions short.** Stop if your cat seems tired or stuck on the light. Cornell Feline Health Center also suggests rotating toys so cats don't get bored.
- **Keep the beam off your cat's face.** Aim it at the floor or a wall.
- **Clear the play area.** Cornell says to move heavy objects that could fall and make sure your cat can't fall from a height while excited.
- **Read the user guide.** The makers' pages say to see the guide for full instructions. Keep these toys away from children.
- **Ask your vet** if your cat seems fixated on lights or shadows.
