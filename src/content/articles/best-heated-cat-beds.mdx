## How to choose a heated cat bed

### Look for low heat and a thermostat

Cornell Feline Health Center says cats look for warm places to rest. It also says too much heat can burn an older cat who can't move quickly, and it advises aiming for "warm, not hot." The K&H beds here use thermostats that warm to about 102°F when a cat lies on them. K&H says they stay about 10 to 15°F above room temperature when empty.

### Check whether it has a switch

All three K&H heated products here have no on/off switch. They stay on whenever they are plugged in. K&H says they are made for continuous use, but MET Labs suggests unplugging them during long absences.

### Choose the right style

- **Cushioned bed:** Good for cats who like to curl up with sides around them.
- **Flat mat:** Good for adding warmth to a perch, tree or bed you already have.
- **Outdoor-style pad:** Only for sheltered spots like a garage or shed.
- **No-power mat:** Good if you don't want a cord at all.

### Check the cover and the cord

Pick a cover you can remove and wash. For the K&H beds, you unplug the bed and take out the warmer before washing the cover. Think about cords too. K&H says its Extreme Weather pad has a steel-wrapped cord that deters chewing. No cord can be called chew-proof, so check for damage often.

## Safety tips

- **Let your cat leave the bed.** K&H says not to put its heated pads where a pet can't move off them. Cornell warns about burns for older cats who can't move quickly.
- **Ask your vet first for kittens and frail cats.** K&H says to talk to a vet before using its pads with young kittens, recovering pets, or any pet that can't move off the pad on its own.
- **Don't use an extension cord.** K&H labels its products "Do not use with extension cord."
- **Keep covers on and the pad dry.** Put nothing on the pad except the cover. Don't soak the pad or machine wash it. For the Extreme Weather pad, only the cover is washable.
- **Keep the bed out of drafts.** Cornell says to keep an older cat's bed out of drafty areas.
