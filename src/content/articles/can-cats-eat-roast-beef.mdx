Yes, cats can have a small bite of plain, fully cooked roast beef. Beef is meat, and cats are meat eaters. The problems
come from what is added to it: salt, onion, garlic, gravy, and raw or undercooked centers. Keep it to a tiny treat.

<Callout type="warn" title="Check before you share">
  Roast beef is often cooked with onion, garlic, or seasoning mixes. Cats are very sensitive to onion and garlic. If your
  cat ate seasoned roast beef, call your vet. You can also call a poison hotline:

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is roast beef okay for cats?

Cornell Feline Health Center calls cats "obligate carnivores." That means they need nutrients that come only from
animal foods, and their diet should be mostly protein. Plain cooked beef fits that pattern.

Still, a treat is not a meal. Cornell says treats should stay around 10 to 15 percent of what a cat eats in a day. The
rest should be a complete cat food.

## What makes roast beef risky for cats?

Roast beef itself is not poisonous. The risks are in how it is made:

- **Onion and garlic.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are the most sensitive pets to these foods. They can
  damage red blood cells and cause anemia, which means the body has too few healthy red blood cells. Raw, cooked, and
  powdered forms can all be a problem. Powders and dried flakes are the most common cause in cats.
- **Salt.** The ASPCA says salty foods can make pets very thirsty and make them pee more. Too much salt can lead to
  vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures.
- **Raw or undercooked meat.** The ASPCA says raw meat can carry bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli. Cornell says raw
  meat can also spread other infections, including toxoplasmosis, so it does not recommend it.
- **Bones.** The ASPCA warns that bones can hurt or block the gut and may need surgery. Some roasts are cooked with a bone in.
- **Fatty foods.** The ASPCA says rich, fatty foods can cause vomiting and diarrhea. They can also cause pancreatitis,
  a painful swelling of the pancreas.

## How do I serve roast beef to my cat?

1. Use plain beef that was cooked all the way through, with no rare center.
2. Cut off the fat, and take out any bones.
3. Skip the gravy, sauce, glaze, and the vegetables cooked with the roast. Onions are common in roasting pans.
4. Chop it into tiny pieces.
5. Give just a bite or two, once in a while.

## What about deli roast beef?

Sliced deli roast beef is not the best choice. It can be salty and seasoned, and you may not know what is in it. If you
want to share some, read the ingredient label first. Choose plain slices with no onion or garlic, and give a very
small piece. Fresh plain cooked beef you made yourself is better.

## What if my cat ate roast beef?

A small bite of plain roast beef is not a worry for a healthy cat. Call your vet if your cat:

- Ate a large amount of salty or fatty roast beef
- Ate roast beef cooked with onion, garlic, or seasoning mix
- Ate raw or undercooked beef and now seems sick
- Is vomiting, has diarrhea, or seems weak

The Merck Veterinary Manual says signs of onion or garlic poisoning may not show for several days. They include
tiredness, poor appetite, fast breathing, yellow skin or gums, and dark or reddish pee. Call your vet right away
instead of waiting for signs.
