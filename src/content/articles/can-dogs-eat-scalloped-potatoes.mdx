Scalloped potatoes are not a good food for dogs. Plain cooked potato is fine, but this dish usually adds onion, salt,
cream, and butter. Onion can harm a dog's blood, and rich, salty food can upset the belly. **A tiny taste is unlikely to
be an emergency, but don't share it on purpose.**

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate scalloped potatoes with onion or garlic, or a large amount">

## Why are scalloped potatoes bad for dogs?

The potato is not the problem. The AKC says plain, well-cooked potato is fine for dogs in small amounts. The extras in the
sauce are what cause trouble.

### Onion and garlic

Many scalloped potato recipes use onion, and some use garlic. The ASPCA says onions, garlic, and chives can irritate the
stomach and damage red blood cells. That can lead to anemia, which means the body has too few healthy red blood cells.

Cooking does not remove the risk. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered onion and garlic can
all harm dogs. It says garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion. Powders and dried flakes are most often linked
to poisoning, so check the label on any seasoning mix.

### Cream, milk, and cheese

The ASPCA says dogs do not make much lactase, the enzyme that breaks down milk sugar. Dairy can cause diarrhea or an upset
stomach. The AKC says loose stool, gas, vomiting, and belly pain are possible after dairy. It also says milk is high in
fat. The AKC's potato guide lists milk as an add-in to leave out.

### Butter and fat

The AKC says heavy butter can trigger pancreatitis. That is a painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ that helps with
digestion. The Merck Veterinary Manual says large amounts of table scraps and fatty food are a common trigger in dogs. It
also says that often no exact cause is found, so no one can say that one dish will cause it.

### Salt

Salt is the last worry. The ASPCA says too much salt can cause vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. Merck says
vomiting can start within several hours. A small taste is unlikely to reach that point. A big helping, or a small dog,
raises the risk.

## What signs should I watch for?

Stomach upset is the most likely result of a small taste. Watch your dog for a day or two for:

- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Gas or loose stool
- Not wanting to eat
- Tiredness or weakness
- Belly pain, such as whining or a hunched back

Merck says severe pancreatitis can cause poor appetite, vomiting, weakness, belly pain, dehydration, and diarrhea. Milder
cases may show few signs.

Onion poisoning is slower. Merck says blood changes start within about a day, but signs of anemia usually show up around
days 3 to 5. Look for:

- Tiredness and weakness
- Fast breathing or a fast heartbeat
- Yellow skin or gums
- Dark urine
- Collapse

Because of this delay, call your vet if the dish had onion or garlic, even when your dog looks normal.

## What should I do if my dog ate scalloped potatoes?

1. **Check the recipe.** Look for onion, garlic, chives, and seasoning mixes.
2. **Note how much your dog ate** and how big your dog is.
3. **Call your vet or a poison hotline** if there was onion or garlic, if your dog ate a lot, or if your dog seems sick.
4. **Give your dog fresh water** and watch closely.

Don't make your dog vomit unless a vet says to. Merck says treatment for onion or garlic poisoning may include making the
dog vomit with vet-approved medicine, giving fluids, and, in serious cases, oxygen or a blood transfusion. Merck also
says not to hold back food or water from a dog with pancreatitis on your own. Let your vet decide.

## Can dogs eat leftover scalloped potatoes?

It is best to skip them. Leftovers are just as rich and salty as the first serving. Scrape plates into a covered trash can
and keep the casserole dish out of reach on holiday tables, where dogs can eat fast when no one is watching.

## What can my dog have instead?

A small piece of plain, well-cooked potato is a better choice. Serve it soft, with no butter, salt, spices, or milk. Our
guide on [potatoes for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-potatoes/) explains more. A similar dish is covered in
[potato salad for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-potato-salad/). Plain [carrots](/can-dogs-eat-carrots/) are another easy choice.

Treats of any kind should be no more than about 10% of your dog's daily calories. For the full list of foods to avoid,
see our guide to [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
