## How to choose cat toys

### Match the toy to how your cat plays

Cornell Feline Health Center says toys let cats stalk, pounce and solve problems. That can help prevent weight gain and problems like scratching furniture. Think about what your cat enjoys:

- **Chasing and pouncing:** a lure you move, like the Cat Dancer, or a motion toy.
- **Playing alone:** a track toy or a toy that moves on its own.
- **Grabbing and kicking:** a long plush toy.

### Skip toys with parts that can come off

Cornell advises avoiding toys with small pieces or string-like parts, such as feathers and string, that may come loose and be swallowed. Also avoid anything with an electrical cord your cat could chew.

### You don't need to spend a lot

Cornell notes that high-tech toys are not needed. Paper bags, cardboard boxes and ping pong balls give cats plenty to do. Rotate toys, since cats can get bored with the same ones.

## Safety tips

- **Supervise play.** KONG and Outward Hound both say their toys should be used with supervision.
- **Check toys often.** Throw out any toy that is damaged or has parts coming loose.
- **Put away small or loose parts** after play, and remove packaging before giving a toy.
- **Clear the play area.** Cornell says to move heavy objects that could fall, and make sure your cat can't fall from a height while distracted.
- **Ask your vet** if you are unsure whether a toy is safe. If your cat swallows part of a toy, call your vet right away.
