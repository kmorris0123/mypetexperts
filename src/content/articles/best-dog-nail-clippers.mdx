## How to choose a dog nail clipper or grinder

### Clippers or grinders?

The AKC describes three types of clippers: scissor-style, guillotine-style and grinders. It says to choose the tool you are most comfortable with, or the one that works best for your dog.

A grinder removes a little nail at a time. That can make it easier to avoid the quick if your dog wiggles. Many dogs need to get used to the sound and the vibration first. A clipper is quiet and fast, but one bad squeeze can cut too far.

### What to look for

- **A tool made for dogs.** The AKC says to use a safe tool designed for dogs.
- **A comfortable grip.** You need steady control of a squirmy foot.
- **A guard or stop.** Some tools have a safety stop or nail guard. These can help, but they don't replace slow, careful trimming.
- **Replaceable parts for grinders.** Check how the grinding bits, caps or stones are replaced.
- **Size.** Match the tool to your dog's nails. For clippers, check the size range on the brand's page.

## Safety tips

- Know where the quick is. It is the pink part of the nail with blood vessels. On dark nails, the AKC says to look for a chalky white ring that shows where the quick ends.
- Trim only the tip, straight across. Don't clip past the curve of the nail.
- Hold the toe firmly but gently.
- With a grinder, take off small amounts at a time. Keep long fur out of the way.
- Keep styptic powder or another clotting powder nearby.
- Start young. The AKC says to start early with puppies so they get used to it.
- Most dogs need a trim every three to four weeks, says the AKC. Long nails can cause pain, splayed paws, less grip and tendon injury over time.
- If bleeding won't stop or your dog is in pain, call your vet.
