Banana bread is not a good food for cats. A plain lick is unlikely to be an emergency, but the bread gives your cat
nothing it needs. **The real risks are the extras:** chocolate chips, raisins, and other mix-ins. Keep the slice away
from your cat, and call your vet if yours ate a piece with those inside.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate banana bread with chocolate or raisins">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline. Do this even if your cat seems fine.

## Why is banana bread not good for cats?

The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are carnivores, which means they eat mainly meat. A complete cat food already
gives them what they need. Merck also tells owners to avoid giving table scraps.

Banana bread is a people treat. It is a baked mix of flour, sugar, and other things, and it is not made for cats. The
Merck page also says overfeeding can lead to obesity and other serious health problems. A plain banana is a separate
question, and our guide on [bananas for cats](/can-cats-eat-bananas/) covers it.

## Which banana bread mix-ins can hurt a cat?

Check what is inside before you decide if a crumb matters.

- **Chocolate chips.** The ASPCA says chocolate has methylxanthines. These can cause vomiting, diarrhea, extra thirst
  and peeing, an uneven heartbeat, tremors, seizures, and even death. Dark chocolate is riskier. Baking chocolate and
  cocoa powder have the most. Our guide on [chocolate for cats](/can-cats-eat-chocolate/) has more.
- **Raisins.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says there are anecdotal reports of kidney failure in cats after eating
  grapes or raisins. It also says no case reports have been published for cats, and the known cases are in dogs. The
  risk for cats is unclear, so treat raisins as a reason to call your vet.
- **Nuts.** The ASPCA says nuts like almonds, pecans, and walnuts are high in oils and fat. These can cause vomiting and
  diarrhea, and may inflame the pancreas in some pets. Walnuts are a common banana bread mix-in. See our guide on
  [almonds for cats](/can-cats-eat-almonds/).
- **Xylitol.** This is a sweetener in some sugar-free foods. The ASPCA says cats are not negatively affected by
  xylitol. It is very dangerous for dogs, so keep any sugar-free baking away from a dog.

The sources we read do not say anything specific about other spices or flavorings for cats. If the recipe has
something you are unsure about, call your vet.

## What should I watch for if my cat ate banana bread?

Plain banana bread may upset your cat's belly. Watch for vomiting, loose stool, or not wanting to eat. Signs of
chocolate poisoning include vomiting, diarrhea, extra thirst, tremors, and seizures.

Have the recipe or package ready when you call. Tell the vet how much your cat ate and when. The ASPCA suggests noting
the amount eaten before you call.

## What about raw banana bread batter?

Keep batter away from your cat. The sources we read do not cover banana bread batter. The ASPCA does warn that raw
yeast dough can swell in the stomach and make alcohol. Banana bread usually has no yeast, but batter also has raw
mix-ins like chocolate and raisins. Call your vet if your cat licked or ate some.

## What can I give my cat instead?

Treats made for cats are the easiest choice. You can also offer a few pieces of your cat's regular food as a reward.
