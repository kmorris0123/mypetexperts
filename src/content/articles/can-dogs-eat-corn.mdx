Yes, dogs can eat plain corn kernels in small amounts. Corn is not toxic. **Never give your dog corn on the cob.** The cob does not break down, and if your dog swallows it, it can block the gut. Cooked kernels with no butter, salt, or other add-ins are the best choice.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog swallowed part of a corn cob">
  Call your vet or an emergency clinic **now**, even if your dog seems fine. A swallowed cob can get stuck and may need surgery.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is corn on the cob dangerous for dogs?

Dogs can choke on a cob, and a swallowed piece can cause a serious blockage in the intestines. Pet Poison Helpline says cobs can get stuck and may need expensive surgery to remove. It also says cobs are hard to see on x-rays, which can make a blockage harder to find.

Signs of a blockage, according to the AKC, include:

- Repeated vomiting
- Not wanting to eat
- Diarrhea
- Tiredness and less activity
- Dehydration (not enough water in the body)

If you see any of these, get to a vet right away.

## Are corn kernels safe for dogs?

Yes, in small amounts. The AKC says plain kernels cut off the cob are fine. They have protein, carbohydrates, and an essential fat called linoleic acid. Dogs digest cooked corn much better than raw starch, the AKC notes, and many dog foods already contain corn.

## How do I serve corn to my dog?

PetMD suggests these steps:

1. Cook the corn with nothing added. No butter, salt, or seasoning.
2. Cut the kernels off the cob. Throw the cob away where your dog can't reach it.
3. Give only a small amount. Treats should stay under about 10% of your dog's daily calories.
4. Watch for itching or stomach upset the first time. The AKC says corn allergies are rare, but they can happen.

## Other kinds of corn

Plain corn is simple. Many corn foods have extra ingredients, and those are what you need to check.

### Is canned, frozen, or creamed corn okay?

Frozen kernels, once cooked with nothing added, are the same as plain corn, so a small amount is fine. Canned and creamed corn often have added salt, butter, or sugar, so they are not the best choice. The ASPCA warns that too much salt can make dogs very thirsty and, in serious cases, cause vomiting, tremors, and seizures. Read the label, or pick plain corn instead.

### Can dogs eat cornbread or corn muffins?

A small bite of plain cornbread is unlikely to hurt, but it is not a good treat. The AKC says plain bread is generally safe but has no real nutrition and has a lot of calories. Check the ingredients first. The AKC lists raisins, garlic, and xylitol as dangerous in baked goods. Xylitol is a sweetener found in some sugar-free products. Never give raw dough, because the AKC says it can swell in the stomach and cause alcohol poisoning.

### Can dogs eat corn tortillas?

A small piece is not a worry, according to PetMD. Tortillas are salty, though, so they should not be a regular treat.

### Can dogs eat candy corn?

Regular candy corn is not poisonous, but it is a sugary candy, so skip it. Pet Poison Helpline says its sugar and fat can upset the stomach and may cause pancreatitis, a painful swelling of the pancreas. **Sugar-free candy corn is the real danger.** It may contain xylitol, which can make a dog's blood sugar drop to a deadly level and can harm the liver. Chocolate-flavored versions may also contain real chocolate.

If your dog ate sugar-free candy corn, call Pet Poison Helpline or a vet right away. Signs include vomiting, tiredness, tremors, and trouble walking.

### What about corn dogs, corn nuts, grits, and corned beef?

I did not find a veterinary source that covers these foods directly, so I can't give a firm answer. Corn dogs and fried foods are fatty and salty. Pet Poison Helpline warns that fatty leftovers can lead to pancreatitis. Corned beef is a salty, fatty meat. Ask your vet before sharing any of these.

## What should I do if my dog ate corn?

- **Kernels only:** A few usually need nothing more than watching. Call your vet if your dog vomits or has diarrhea that doesn't stop.
- **A cob or part of one:** Call your vet right away, as described above.
- **Sugar-free candy corn or baked goods with xylitol:** Call a poison hotline or vet right away.

## What can I give my dog instead?

Plain, cooked kernels are a fine treat. Other easy options are [apple slices without the core](/can-dogs-eat-apples/) or [a few slices of banana](/can-dogs-eat-bananas/). For a full list of foods to avoid, see our guide to [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
