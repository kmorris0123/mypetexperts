A kitten can eat a little adult cat food without harm, but it shouldn't be their regular diet. Whether your kitten is 5, 6, or 10 months old, **they're still growing**, and the sources we checked say kitten food should continue until about 9 to 12 months of age.

If you're asking because you ran out of kitten food or your kitten raided the adult cat's bowl, a meal or two isn't an emergency. Just get back to kitten food as soon as you can.

<Callout type="info" title="Quick version">

  - Stolen bites or a one-off meal of adult food: fine.
  - Day-to-day food for a kitten under about 9 months: kitten formula.
  - Around 9 to 12 months: time to talk with your vet about switching.
  - Treats should stay small: no more than about 5% of a kitten's daily intake.

</Callout>

## Why kittens need kitten food

Kitten food is built for fast growth. The Merck Veterinary Manual says growing kittens need extra calories, fat, protein, vitamins, and minerals. The ASPCA puts the energy need at roughly two to three times that of an adult cat, with about 30% of that energy coming from protein.

Merck also notes that growing kittens are more sensitive than adult cats to the quality of their protein and the balance of amino acids. Adult formulas are designed for maintenance, not growth, so a kitten living on them long term may not get what they need.

## What about 5, 6, and 10 months?

Kittens at these ages are no longer tiny, but the guidance doesn't change much:

- **5 and 6 months:** Still kitten food. Cornell says kittens usually do best on three meals a day until six months old.
- **6 months to a year:** Still kitten food, usually with twice-daily meals, according to Cornell.
- **10 months:** You're close to the 9 to 12 month window where Merck and the ASPCA say kittens reach adulthood. Many kittens are ready for the change around now, but some take longer, so ask your vet.

The sources we read give a range rather than one exact date, and none gave a separate rule for larger breeds. If your kitten is a big breed or growing slowly, your vet can judge when they've finished growing.

## When and how to switch to adult food

The ASPCA says to feed kitten-formula food until your cat reaches maturity, at about one year. Once your cat is an adult, Cornell says feeding once or twice a day is appropriate in most cases. Merck says most adult cats do best fed twice daily, and warns that leaving food out all the time can lead to overeating and obesity.

Our sources didn't spell out a step-by-step transition schedule. The usual advice is to change foods gradually rather than overnight, and your vet can suggest how to phase it in for your kitten.

Check that any food you choose says it's complete and balanced for your cat's life stage. The ASPCA also notes cats need taurine, an essential amino acid, so don't swap in dog food. Our guide on [whether cats can eat dog food](/can-cats-eat-dog-food/) explains why.

## When to call your vet

Call your vet if your kitten stops eating, or if vomiting, diarrhea, or low energy lasts more than a couple of days after a food change. The ASPCA suggests checking in if these signs continue for more than two days. Kittens are small and can get sick quickly, so don't wait it out if they seem unwell.

## Bottom line

- Occasional adult cat food: no problem.
- Regular diet before about 9 to 12 months: stick with kitten food.
- Switching: ask your vet, and do it gradually.
