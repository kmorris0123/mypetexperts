Yes, dogs can eat a small piece of plain, fully baked white or wheat bread once in a while. It is not toxic, but it
gives your dog no real nutrition. **Raw dough, raisin bread, garlic bread, and bread with xylitol are dangerous.** Check
what is in the bread first.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate raw dough, raisins, garlic, or xylitol">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline **now**, even if your dog seems fine. Dough can swell in the
  stomach and make alcohol, and signs can be serious.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Is plain bread safe for dogs?

The AKC says plain white and wheat bread are generally fine for dogs, as long as the dog has no allergies. It also says
bread is a filler food. It adds nothing your dog's regular food does not already give. Bread is full of carbs, so too
much can add weight.

Give only very small pieces, and only once in a while. Dogs with a wheat allergy should skip bread completely.

## What kinds of bread are dangerous for dogs?

These are the ones to keep away:

- **Raw dough.** Yeast keeps working in your dog's warm stomach. Pet Poison Helpline says the dough swells and the
  yeast makes alcohol, which gets into the blood. Signs include vomiting, staggering, a swollen belly, and weakness. In
  bad cases the stomach can twist, which can kill a dog.
- **Raisin bread.** The AKC warns that raisins and grapes are highly toxic, and even a few can cause problems. See our
  guide on [grapes and dogs](/can-dogs-eat-grapes/).
- **Garlic bread.** Garlic and onion can harm red blood cells. More on that below.
- **Bread with xylitol.** This sweetener can be in some baked goods. The FDA says it can make a dog's blood sugar drop
  fast. Read the label, especially on "sugar-free" products.
- **Bread with nuts.** The AKC says nuts are high in fat and can upset the stomach. Macadamia nuts are especially bad
  for dogs.

## What to do if your dog ate bread

If it was plain baked bread, a dog usually just needs to be watched. A big amount can still upset the stomach.

If it was dough, or bread with raisins, garlic, nuts, or xylitol, call a vet or hotline right away. Tell them what
your dog ate, about how much, and when.

## Other kinds of bread and baked foods

The same rules apply to every bread: plain and baked is the best case. Extras are where the trouble is. The sources we
read do not name most of these foods one by one, so the answers below come from what they say about the ingredients.

### Whole grain, multigrain, and grain bread

These are still bread. The AKC does not single them out. Plain slices work like plain wheat bread: a small piece
only. Check for seeds, nuts, and raisins mixed in, and skip any loaf with nuts or raisins.

### Toast and toasted bread

Plain toast is just baked bread, so the answer is the same: a small plain piece once in a while. Skip butter-heavy
or jam-topped toast.

### Brioche, pandan bread, and French bread

Plain French bread is like other plain bread. Brioche and sweet breads such as pandan bread are usually richer, with
more sugar and fat, so a bite is plenty. Merck says large amounts of table scraps can lead to pancreatitis, a painful
swelling of the pancreas. Check any sweet loaf or filling for raisins and xylitol.

### Garlic bread

**No.** The AKC says garlic bread can cause belly pain, vomiting, diarrhea, weakness, and collapse. The Merck
Veterinary Manual says dogs can be poisoned by raw, cooked, dried, or powdered garlic and onion. Garlic is about 3 to 5
times stronger than onion. Signs of damaged red blood cells often show up 3 to 5 days later, so a dog can look fine at
first. Call your vet if your dog ate it.

### Naan and pita bread

Plain naan or pita is still bread, so a small plain bite is the limit. Many naan breads are made with garlic, so
check the label or ask the cook. Garlic naan has the same problem as garlic bread.

### Pita chips

Pita chips are best skipped. They are often seasoned, and seasoning can include garlic or onion powder. Merck says
powders are the more concentrated form of garlic and onion. The ASPCA also warns that too much salt can cause
thirst, vomiting, and in bad cases seizures.

### Bread crumbs and breaded fish

Plain bread crumbs are just crushed bread, but store-bought ones are often seasoned. Check for garlic and onion
powder. Breaded fish is usually fried, and Merck lists large amounts of table scraps as a common risk factor for
pancreatitis in dogs. Fried, salty, seasoned fish is not a good snack, so skip it.

### Corn bread

Plain corn bread is a baked bread, so a tiny plain bite is the limit. Many recipes are sweet and buttery, so
check for xylitol, raisins, and nuts, and keep it to a crumb.

### Biscuits, sweet biscuits, and Marie biscuits

In the U.S., buttermilk biscuits are a bread-like baked food. In the U.K. and other places, "biscuits" are cookies,
such as Marie biscuits. Either kind is made with fat and often sugar, and gives a dog nothing useful. Skip them as a habit. A tiny plain
piece is the most you should share. Never share any that have chocolate, raisins, nuts, or xylitol. Our guide on
[chocolate and dogs](/can-dogs-eat-chocolate/) explains why chocolate is risky.

### French toast

French toast is bread soaked in egg and milk, fried in fat, and often topped with syrup or sugar. Because of the fat
and sugar, it is not a good share. Check for xylitol in any sugar-free syrup. Merck says large amounts of table scraps can lead to pancreatitis, so keep it to a tiny bite at most.

## Safer treats to try

A small piece of plain bread is fine once in a while, but dog treats are better. For a few other ideas, see our
guides on [peanut butter](/can-dogs-eat-peanut-butter/) (check for xylitol) and [apples](/can-dogs-eat-apples/).
Treats of all kinds should be no more than about 10% of your dog's daily calories. For a full list, see
[foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
