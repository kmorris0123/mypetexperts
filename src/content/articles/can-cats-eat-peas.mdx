Yes, a few plain peas are okay for most cats once in a while. Veterinary sources we checked don't list peas as a poison for cats. But cats are meat eaters and get nothing they truly need from peas. Keep peas **plain, small, and rare**.

<Callout type="warn" title="Watch what the peas are mixed with">
  Peas from a dinner plate often come with onion, garlic, butter, or salt. Onion and garlic can harm a cat's red blood
  cells. If your cat ate peas cooked with onion or garlic, call your vet or a poison hotline.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why peas are not a food cats need

Cornell's Feline Health Center says cats are obligate carnivores. That means they rely on nutrients found only in animal
products. In the wild, cats eat prey that is high in protein, medium in fat, and very low in carbohydrates.

The Merck Veterinary Manual adds some details. Cats must get an amino acid called taurine from their food, and plant
proteins don't have it. Cats also can't turn the plant color beta-carotene into vitamin A the way many animals can.
They need vitamin A that is already made, from animal foods.

So a pea can be a harmless nibble, but it can't replace any part of your cat's meals.

## Are peas safe for cats?

We did not find a veterinary source that says peas are poisonous to cats. The ASPCA's list of people foods to avoid
doesn't include them. We also did not find a vet source that gives a "safe amount" of peas. That is why we call this
"a little, once in a while" and not "good for your cat."

If your cat has a health problem or eats a special diet, ask your vet before adding any new food.

## How to serve peas to a cat

- **Keep them plain.** No butter, oil, salt, or spices. Never serve them with onion, garlic, or chives.
- **Use plain peas.** Fresh, or frozen and thawed, with nothing added. Skip canned peas, which often have added salt.
- **Start with just one or two.** Watch for vomiting or diarrhea. Stop if you see either.
- **Keep it a treat.** Cornell says treats should not be more than 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories.

## Other kinds of peas

### Are peas in cat food okay?

Yes. Peas are an ingredient in some commercial cat foods. The FDA looked into a possible link between pet foods that
contain peas or lentils and a heart disease called dilated cardiomyopathy. Most of the reports were about dogs. From
January 2014 to April 2019, the FDA received 9 reports for cats and 515 for dogs. The FDA says these reports can't prove
that the diets cause the disease, and it has not confirmed a link. If you are worried about your cat's food, ask your
vet.

### Are sweet pea flowers toxic to cats?

The ASPCA lists the perennial sweet pea (*Lathyrus latifolius*) as non-toxic to cats. Its warnings on that page are for
horses. Eating any plant can still upset a cat's stomach, so keep your cat from chewing on garden plants. If your cat
gets sick after chewing a plant you can't name, call your vet.

### Can cats eat peas with onion or garlic?

No. The ASPCA says onions and garlic are more dangerous to cats than to dogs. They can upset the stomach and damage red
blood cells, which can lead to anemia, a low red blood cell count. Call your vet if your cat ate any.
