## How to choose a cat bed

Cats Protection lists six things that make a bed appealing. Use them as a checklist.

### Comfort and warmth

Pick something soft and cushioned, like a donut-style bed. Cats Protection says cats seek out warm spots, so warmth matters. Cornell Feline Health Center also advises keeping an older cat's bed out of drafty areas.

### Privacy and a good view

Covered or igloo-style beds help some cats feel secure. Cats Protection also says cats like a raised spot where they can watch what is going on. Beds with more than one way out feel safer to cats that like an escape route.

### Easy cleaning

Cats Protection says cleaning matters, and that cats rotate their sleeping spots. Look for a machine-washable cover or insert. Wash the bed regularly, even if it seems unused.

### Beds for older cats

Older cats may find it hard to jump or climb. Cornell suggests box steps, ramps or pet stairs with a non-slip covering for cats that can't reach favorite spots. A low bed with an easy entry is a good fit.

### Sizing

None of our sources give a size rule, so use each maker's size chart. Check the bed's width against how your cat sleeps, whether curled up or stretched out.

## Safety tips

- **Be careful with heat.** Cornell warns that too much heat can be dangerous for cats with reduced mobility. Make sure a heated bed has a thermostat and that your cat can leave it easily.
- **Follow the maker's directions.** Use the heater and cord only as the brand instructs, and check them for damage. If a cord looks chewed or worn, stop using it.
- **Put the bed in a quiet place.** Cats Protection suggests private areas away from disturbances.
- **Make it smell familiar.** Cats Protection suggests adding a familiar-scented item to a new bed.
- **Keep it clean.** Wash covers and inserts regularly using the care instructions.
