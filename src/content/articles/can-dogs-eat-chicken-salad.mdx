Chicken salad is not a good food for dogs. Plain chicken is fine, but the salad often has onion, grapes, raisins, salt, and
creamy dressing. Some of these can make a dog very sick. **A tiny lick of a plain version is unlikely to hurt, but don't
share it on purpose.**

## Is chicken salad safe for dogs?

It depends on the recipe, and most recipes have at least one problem. The chicken is not the issue. The AKC says plain,
unseasoned cooked chicken is fine for most dogs. The extras are the trouble.

Chicken salad recipes change from kitchen to kitchen. Common add-ins include onion, grapes, raisins, nuts, celery, and
a creamy dressing. If you are not sure what is in it, don't give it to your dog.

## Why can chicken salad be bad for dogs?

### Onion, garlic, and chives

The ASPCA says onion, garlic, and chives can upset the stomach and damage red blood cells. That can lead to anemia, which
means the blood can't carry enough oxygen. The risk depends on how much a dog eats.

Cooking does not remove the danger. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered forms can all harm
dogs. Signs often show up 3 to 5 days later and include tiredness, weakness, fast breathing, and dark urine. Dried
forms such as onion powder are the ones most often linked to poisoning, so check seasoning mixes too.

### Grapes and raisins

Grapes and raisins can cause kidney failure in dogs. The Merck Veterinary Manual says it knows of no amount that is safe.
It also says more than one grape or raisin for every 10 pounds of body weight may be enough to cause risk. Vomiting or
diarrhea usually starts within 6 to 12 hours. Our guide explains more about [grapes and dogs](/can-dogs-eat-grapes/).

### Salt

Too much salt can make a dog sick. The ASPCA lists vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures as signs. Merck says
vomiting usually starts within several hours. A lick of salad has far less salt than the amounts that cause these
problems. Still, store-bought and deli salads can be salty.

### Rich dressing and nuts

Fatty food can upset a dog's stomach. The Merck Veterinary Manual says eating large amounts of table scraps is a common
risk for pancreatitis. That is a painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ near the stomach. The ASPCA also lists
nuts such as almonds, pecans, and walnuts as fatty foods that can cause vomiting, diarrhea, and possibly pancreatitis.

The ASPCA says macadamia nuts can cause weakness, tremors, vomiting, and a high body temperature in dogs.

## What if my dog ate chicken salad?

1. **Find out what was in it.** Check the bowl, container, or label for onion, garlic, chives, grapes, and raisins.
2. **Note how much your dog ate.** Your vet or a poison hotline will ask.
3. **Call your vet or a hotline** if any of those items were in it, or if your dog ate a lot. The Merck Veterinary Manual
   says to call a vet right away after onion or garlic, even if your dog seems fine.
4. **Watch for signs.** For onion, look for tiredness, weakness, and dark urine in the next few days. For grapes,
   look for vomiting, diarrhea, and low energy.
5. **Call your vet for stomach upset.** Merck says to call promptly if your dog vomits, stops eating, has belly pain,
   or seems weak after eating fatty scraps.

If it was only a small lick of plain chicken salad with none of these items, watch your dog and call your vet if
anything seems off.

## Can dogs eat plain chicken instead?

Yes. Plain cooked chicken is a better choice. The AKC says unseasoned roasted, poached, grilled, or baked chicken can be
a treat or can be mixed into regular food. Take it off the bone first. The AKC warns that cooked bones can splinter and
cause choking or a cut in the gut.

Some dogs are allergic to chicken, so stop and ask your vet if your dog itches or has an upset stomach after eating it.
Our guide covers [dogs and chicken](/can-dogs-eat-chicken/) in more detail.

## How much human food is okay?

The Merck Veterinary Manual says treats should be under 10% of your dog's daily calories. Most treats are not complete
and balanced, so too many can leave out nutrients your dog needs. Plain chicken counts toward that limit.
