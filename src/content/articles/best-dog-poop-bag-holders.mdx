## How to choose dog poop bags and holders

### Bag size and strength

Pick a bag long and wide enough to cover your hand and the mess. Look for a bag that tears off cleanly and ties shut. Handles can make tying easier.

### Biodegradable and compostable labels

The AKC says "biodegradable" bags may not break down fast or cleanly, but they are still a greener choice, even in the trash. The AKC also says compostable bags only break down in a composter, not a landfill. Some city green-bin programs take dog waste, so check with your local government. Recycled-plastic bags, like the Earth Rated picks above, are another way to cut down on new plastic.

### Holders

Choose a holder that fits your leash type and your bag rolls. A hook for used bags keeps your hands free until you find a trash can. Check the roll size before you buy.

## Safety tips

- Pick up every time. The AKC says dog waste can carry parasites like Giardia and worms, and bacteria like salmonella and E. coli, even when your dog looks healthy.
- Put sealed bags in the trash. The AKC says not to leave bags on the ground or hanging in trees.
- Don't flush unless your waste goes to a sewage treatment plant. Avoid flushing with a septic system, and check local rules.
- Don't use home compost for dog waste. The AKC says home composters may not kill germs, and the result should not go on food gardens.
- If your dog's stool looks unusual, call your vet.
