## How to choose an orthopedic dog bed

### Pick the right foam

The AKC says orthopedic beds are usually made from memory foam or egg-crate foam, and both support tender joints. Consumer Reports says memory foam holds heat, so it suits cold homes. Egg-crate foam is more breathable, so it suits warm homes.

### Get the size right

Choose a bed big enough for your dog to stretch out. The AKC says to match the bed to your dog's size. Check each maker's weight guide too. Casper, for example, lists sizes by the weight of the dog.

### Make sure your dog can get in

Consumer Reports warns that tall beds or high walls can be hard for older dogs to step over. Raised beds can ease pressure on the body, but they still need to be easy to reach.

### Look for a washable cover

The AKC advises a removable, machine-washable cover. This matters most for puppies and for older dogs that may leak urine. Consumer Reports adds that a waterproof cover helps with accidents.

### Think about chewing

Consumer Reports says to skip towels and blankets for chewers and to look for chew-proof beds. A raised, chew-proof frame is one option.

## Safety tips

- Watch the first few days. If your dog chews the bed, take it away.
- Don't give a chewer a bed with loose stuffing or torn fabric.
- Be careful with electric heated beds. The AKC says to watch your dog closely with plug-in warming beds, especially if they chew.
- Let your dog get used to a new bed slowly, with treats and a familiar scent.
- A bed helps with comfort but is not a treatment. If your dog is limping or slow to stand, ask your vet.
