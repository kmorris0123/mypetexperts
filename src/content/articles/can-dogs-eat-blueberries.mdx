Yes, dogs can eat blueberries. The American Kennel Club (AKC) says both fresh and frozen berries are fine as a treat.
Blueberries have vitamin C, fiber, and antioxidants. Give only a few at a time, wash them first, and keep muffins and
other sweet baked goods away from your dog.

## Are blueberries good for dogs?

The AKC says blueberries have a lot of vitamin C and fiber. They also have antioxidants, which are natural plant
compounds. The AKC says these may lessen the effects of brain aging in older dogs.

That is a nice extra, but blueberries are still a treat. Your dog gets what it needs from its regular food.

## How to serve blueberries to your dog

- **Wash them well.** This removes dirt and pesticides, as the AKC advises.
- **Fresh or frozen both work.** Frozen berries can be a cold snack.
- **Keep it plain.** Skip berries in sugary syrup, pie filling, or sweetened yogurt.
- **Watch your dog while it eats.** The AKC notes that choking is a risk, mostly for small dogs.

## How many blueberries can a dog eat?

The AKC does not name a number of berries. It does say all treats together should be no more than 10% of your dog's
daily food. Start with just a few. If you are not sure how many is right for your dog, ask your vet.

## Other kinds of blueberry foods

### Can dogs eat blueberry muffins?

No. The AKC says to skip blueberry muffins. Some muffins have ingredients that can make a dog very sick. Even a
plain one has sugar and fat, and a muffin gives your dog none of the good things a plain berry does. The sources I
read do not name a safe amount, so the safest choice is to give none.

Check the ingredients for these, which are dangerous for dogs:

- **Xylitol.** This sweetener is in some "sugar-free" baked goods. Pet Poison Helpline says it can cause low blood
  sugar and liver failure in dogs. Signs can start within 30 minutes to 2 hours.
- **Chocolate.** Chocolate chips are common in muffins. Dark and baker's chocolate are more dangerous than milk
  chocolate, says Pet Poison Helpline.
- **Raisins.** Pet Poison Helpline says grapes, raisins, and some currants can cause sudden kidney failure in dogs.
  No safe amount is known.

### Can dogs eat blueberry muffins from a store or bakery?

This is the same answer as above, but harder to check. Store and bakery muffins often have long ingredient lists, and
bakery muffins may have no label at all. If you can't read the ingredients, treat the muffin as a possible risk and
call your vet if your dog ate a lot of it.
