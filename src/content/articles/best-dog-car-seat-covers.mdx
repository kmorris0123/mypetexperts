## How to choose a dog car seat cover

### Pick the style: hammock, bench or half

A hammock hangs from the front headrests and makes a barrier behind the front seats. The AKC says barriers can help keep dogs from jumping forward. A bench cover only covers the seat. A half cover leaves room for a passenger.

### Check the size and your seat type

Compare the cover's width with your back seat. Kurgo's Rover Hammock is made for bench seats, and 4Knines sells two widths. Measure before you buy.

### Look at the material and cleaning

Look for waterproof fabric and a non-slip bottom. Then check how it is cleaned. Some covers can go in the washer, and others are spot clean or hand wash only. The care label tells you which.

### Make sure you can reach the seat belts and latch points

Most of these covers have seat belt openings, and some also have openings for the latch system, which attaches child seats. This matters if people share the back seat with your dog.

## Safety tips

- **Use a cover with a restraint, not instead of one.** The AKC compares restraining a dog to buckling up yourself. It lists dog seat belts, car harnesses, and carriers or car seats for smaller dogs.
- **Keep your dog in the back seat.** The AKC says the back seat is better so air bags do not hurt your dog.
- **Check the strap rules.** 4Knines says its front seat cover's optional rear straps should not be used on a seat with an air bag.
- **Don't count on a cover for chewers.** 4Knines says its front seat cover is not chew-proof, so ask whether any cover suits a dog that chews. Watch your dog, and stop using any cover that is torn.
- **Set it up the way the maker says.** Attach all straps and anchors so the cover does not slide when your dog gets in or out.
