No, dogs should not eat baked beans. The beans are not the main worry. The sweet, salty sauce is. It may hold onion or
garlic, which can damage a dog's red blood cells, plus a lot of salt and sugar. One small lick is unlikely to cause a
crisis, but don't share them on purpose.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate baked beans, especially a lot">
  Check the label for onion, garlic, and salt, then call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline for advice.
  Do this even if your dog seems fine, because onion problems can show up days later.

## Why are baked beans bad for dogs?

I did not find a vet source that tests baked beans on their own. The advice here comes from what is often in the
sauce. Recipes and brands differ, so read the label on your can.

Three things matter most:

- **Onion and garlic.** The ASPCA says these can upset the stomach and damage red blood cells, which can lead to
  anemia. Anemia means the blood cannot carry enough oxygen.
- **Salt.** The ASPCA says too much salt can make a dog very thirsty and, in bad cases, cause vomiting, tremors, and
  seizures.
- **Sugar.** The AKC advises serving people food with no added sugar, salt, oil, or seasoning. Sauce for baked beans
  usually has some of these.

## Is onion or garlic in baked beans dangerous?

It can be, because cooking does not remove the risk. The Merck Veterinary Manual says dogs and cats have been
poisoned by cooked onion and garlic, not only raw. It says garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.

The Manual says the most harm comes from concentrated forms, like dried flakes and powders. Many sauces use onion or
garlic powder, so check the label for those words.

Sick dogs may not show it right away. The Manual says signs usually start a few days after eating. Watch for:

- Tiredness or weakness
- Not wanting to eat
- Fast breathing
- Pale or yellow gums
- Dark urine
- Collapse

## Is the salt in baked beans a problem?

It can be if your dog eats a lot. The Merck Veterinary Manual says vomiting usually starts within a few hours of
eating too much salt. It can then lead to weakness, diarrhea, muscle tremors, and seizures.

The Manual gives its amounts by body weight, not by spoonfuls of beans. I did not find a source that says how much
salt is in a serving of baked beans. A small dog that eats a whole can is at more risk than a big dog that licks a
spoon.

## What should I do if my dog ate baked beans?

1. **Take the food away** and keep fresh water out for your dog.
2. **Read the label.** Look for onion, garlic, onion powder, garlic powder, and salt. Also look for any sweetener
   named xylitol. The ASPCA says xylitol, found in some sweet foods, can cause low blood sugar and liver damage.
3. **Call your vet or a poison hotline** and tell them what is in the can and how much your dog ate.
4. **Watch your dog for several days.** Call the vet at once for vomiting, diarrhea, weakness, tremors, pale gums,
   or dark urine.

For salt, the Merck Veterinary Manual says to give small sips of water often. Large amounts of water all at once can
make things worse. Ask your vet how much to offer.

## Other kinds of baked beans

### Can dogs eat baked beans in tomato sauce?

No. This is the usual kind in a can, and the sauce is the part that worries vets. See the sections above. Check the
label for onion, garlic, and salt.

### Can dogs eat pork and beans?

It is best to skip them. Pork and beans usually comes in a similar sweet tomato sauce. I did not find a vet source
about the pork in it, so the same label checks apply.

## What can I give my dog instead?

The AKC says plain green beans are a low-calorie treat for most dogs. Serve them fresh or cooked, with no salt,
butter, or garlic. The AKC also says to avoid canned green beans because of their added salt. Our guide to
[green beans for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-green-beans/) has more.

I did not find a vet source on plain kidney beans or other dry beans for dogs, so ask your vet before sharing
those. Treats of any kind should stay under about 10% of your dog's daily calories. For tomato sauce, see
[tomatoes and dogs](/can-dogs-eat-tomatoes/). For more foods to keep away from your dog, read our list of
[foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
