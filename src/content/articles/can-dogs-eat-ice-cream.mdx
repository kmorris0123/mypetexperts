Ice cream is not a good treat for dogs. A tiny lick of plain vanilla may be fine for some dogs. But many dogs
can't digest milk well, and some flavors or sugar-free kinds can be dangerous. Chocolate and a sweetener called
xylitol are the biggest worries.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate chocolate or sugar-free ice cream">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline right away if the ice cream had chocolate or xylitol.
  Do this even if your dog seems fine.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is ice cream a problem for dogs?

The AKC says many adult dogs are lactose intolerant to some degree. Lactose is the sugar in milk. Dogs that can't
digest it may get loose stool, diarrhea, vomiting, stomach pain, or gas. The ASPCA agrees that milk and other dairy
foods can cause diarrhea or other stomach upset in pets.

Ice cream is also high in fat. The AKC notes that fatty dairy can bring on pancreatitis, a painful swelling of the
pancreas, in some dogs. And an adult dog on good dog food does not need extra calcium from milk or ice cream.

## Is sugar-free ice cream safe for dogs?

Not until you read the label. Some sugar-free foods contain **xylitol**, a sweetener that Pet Poison Helpline says
can cause a life-threatening drop in blood sugar and can damage the liver. The AKC calls xylitol extremely toxic
to dogs.

Signs include vomiting, weakness, loss of coordination, shaking, and seizures. Pet Poison Helpline says they can
start within 10 to 15 minutes, or sometimes hours later. The ASPCA says liver damage can show up within 12 to 24
hours. Get vet help fast. Dogs treated early have an excellent outlook, according to Pet Poison Helpline.

## Is chocolate ice cream bad for dogs?

Yes. Chocolate contains theobromine and caffeine, which the Merck Veterinary Manual says are toxic to dogs. The ASPCA
says darker chocolate is riskier than lighter kinds. Signs include vomiting, panting, extreme thirst, restlessness,
tremors, and in bad cases seizures or heart problems.

Merck says signs usually appear within 6 to 12 hours. Call a vet or hotline instead of waiting. How much is a problem
depends on your dog's size and the kind of chocolate. For more, see our guide on
[chocolate and dogs](/can-dogs-eat-chocolate/).

## What should I do if my dog ate ice cream?

- **Plain vanilla or milk-based, a small amount:** Watch for stomach upset like diarrhea, vomiting, or gas. Call your
  vet if signs are strong or don't go away.
- **Chocolate, sugar-free, or unknown ingredients:** Check the label and call your vet or a hotline now.

## Other kinds of ice cream and cones

### Can dogs eat custard ice cream?

Custard ice cream is still a rich dairy dessert, so the same problems apply: stomach upset from milk and a lot of
fat. I did not find a vet source that tests custard on its own. Treat it like regular ice cream, and check for
xylitol and chocolate.

### Can dogs eat pistachio ice cream?

It's best to skip it. The AKC says pistachios are not toxic on their own. But they are high in fat and salt, and
that can lead to stomach upset and pancreatitis. Shells can be a choking risk, and mold on nut shells can make
dogs weak or cause seizures. Ice cream adds dairy to the mix. Nut flavors can also come with other mix-ins, so read
the label.

### Can dogs eat green tea ice cream?

Better not. The ASPCA says caffeine is a risk for pets. Caffeine can cause vomiting, panting, restlessness,
and heart and nerve problems. I did not find a vet source that gives the caffeine amount in green tea ice cream, so
it's safest to skip it. It also has the same dairy problem as other ice cream.

### Can dogs eat ice cream cones?

The vet sources I read focus on what's in the ice cream, not the cone. The risk is in the filling, so never share a
cone that held chocolate or sugar-free ice cream.

### Can dogs eat cones without ice cream?

I did not find a vet source that covers empty cones. They have no nutrition for your dog, so a treat made for dogs is
a better choice. If your dog grabs one, watch for stomach upset and call your vet if you're worried.

### Can dogs eat durian ice cream?

I could not find a reliable vet source about durian, so I can't say it's safe. Skip it and offer a dog treat.

## What can I give my dog instead of ice cream?

The AKC suggests these cool treats:

- Frozen plain fat-free yogurt
- "Nice cream" made from blended frozen bananas
- Ice cream made for dogs
- Frozen treats your vet has approved

Treats should be no more than about 10% of your dog's daily calories. For a longer list of things to avoid, see our
guide to [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
