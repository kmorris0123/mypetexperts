## How to choose a puppy chew toy

### Match the toy to your puppy's chewing style

The AKC says the right toy depends on your puppy's size, activity level, chewing habits and breed. Look for one that is both comforting and fun. Gentle chewers do well with softer toys. Puppies who chew hard need a tougher one. If you have a power chewer, see our guide to [dog toys for aggressive chewers](/best-dog-toys-for-aggressive-chewers/).

### Look at the material

The AKC suggests nylon or hard rubber because these are less likely to break apart. It also lists rubber teething toys that can be frozen, edible puppy teething rings, and flavored chews as options. Ridges and bumps can help clean teeth, but the AKC says dental toys don't replace brushing.

### Pick the right size

Follow the weight guide on the product page. KONG also says your puppy should be able to chew the toy comfortably but not fit it all the way in their mouth. Nylabone says to choose a size big enough that your dog chews from the side, not the top and bottom. If you have more than one dog, Nylabone says to choose for the biggest, strongest chewer.

### Plan for growth

Puppies grow fast. KONG says its puppy toys aren't for puppies over 9 months, and suggests moving up to a larger toy or a KONG Classic. Nylabone says its softest teething toys aren't for puppies with adult teeth. The AKC says baby teeth start falling out at around 3 to 4 months and should be gone by 6 months.

## Safety tips

- **Supervise chewing.** KONG, Nylabone and the AKC all say to watch your puppy while they chew.
- **Check the toy before each use.** Nylabone says to replace it if it's worn, damaged or too small to chew safely. KONG says to stop using a damaged toy.
- **Throw out torn toys.** The AKC says even a small puncture can let stuffing come loose, which can cause a blockage in the gut. Replace any toy your puppy can chew chunks off.
- **If your puppy may have swallowed a piece,** take the toy away and call your vet. Nylabone gives this same advice.
- **Skip sticks.** The AKC calls them hazardous.
- **Remove packaging** before you hand over a new toy.
- **Ask your vet** if your puppy is drooling a lot or you see blood on toys. The AKC says this can point to a bigger problem. Also tell your vet if baby teeth are still there at 6 months.
