Yes, dogs can eat bananas. Peeled banana is a safe, healthy treat in small amounts, but it's high in natural sugar, so
think of it as an occasional snack, not a daily one. Always take the peel off first.

<Callout type="info" title="The short version">

- Peel it, slice it, and start small.
- Keep it occasional, because bananas are sugary.
- Treats, including banana, should stay under about 10% of your dog's daily calories.

</Callout>

## Why bananas are okay for dogs

Bananas aren't toxic to dogs, and they bring some real nutrition along with the sweet taste. According to the AKC,
they're high in:

- **Potassium**
- **Vitamin B6**
- **Vitamin C**

They also contain **fiber**, which may help some dogs with digestive issues, and **magnesium**, a mineral that
supports bone growth and helps the body make protein and absorb vitamins.

The AKC also notes that some vets suggest bananas as a better choice than treats that are high in fat or salt. If
your dog is used to fatty table scraps, a few slices of banana are a lighter swap.

## Why moderation matters

The catch with bananas is sugar. They're naturally sweet, and too much banana can mean:

- **Extra calories**, which can lead to weight gain over time
- **Digestive upset** in some dogs, especially if they get a lot at once

So bananas work best as an occasional treat. If your dog has a sensitive stomach, start with just a small piece and
see how they handle it before offering more.

If your dog has a health condition, such as diabetes or a weight problem, ask your vet before adding sugary fruit to
the menu.

## What about banana peels?

Don't feed the peel. Banana peels aren't toxic, but they're hard for dogs to digest. A dog that eats a peel may get
an upset stomach, and large pieces can cause an intestinal blockage in some cases.

<Callout type="warn" title="If your dog ate a banana peel">
  Keep an eye on your dog. If they swallowed a large piece, or seem to have stomach trouble or just aren't acting
  right afterward, call your vet.
</Callout>

Keep peels in a covered trash can, especially if your dog likes to raid the garbage.

## How to serve bananas safely

Plain, fresh banana is all you need. A few easy ideas from the AKC:

- **Slice it** into small, bite-sized pieces.
- **Mash it** into your dog's regular food.
- **Freeze it.** Peel the banana, freeze it, then cut it into pieces sized for your dog so they don't pose a choking
  risk. Frozen banana is a nice cool snack in hot weather.
- **Stuff a toy.** Blend banana with a little plain peanut butter, spread it inside a stuffable toy, and freeze it for
  a longer-lasting treat.

If you use peanut butter, **check the label for xylitol** first. Xylitol (sometimes called birch sugar or wood sugar)
is a sweetener that's dangerous for dogs, and the FDA notes that some peanut and nut butters contain it. Our guide on
[whether dogs can eat peanut butter](/can-dogs-eat-peanut-butter/) explains what to look for.

### Skip banana-flavored people foods

Stick to the real fruit. Banana bread, muffins, and other baked goods can hide ingredients dogs shouldn't have. The
FDA notes that xylitol turns up in some baked goods, and raisins are common in breads and muffins too. When in doubt,
leave it out.

## How much banana is too much?

There's no single amount that's right for every dog. The AKC says it depends on your dog's size, age, weight, and
overall diet, so a small dog should get much less than a large one.

The simplest guide is the 10% rule: all treats combined, banana included, should make up no more than about 10% of
your dog's daily calories. The rest should come from a complete, balanced dog food. If you're not sure how much is
right for your dog, your vet can help.

## Other fruits to try

If your dog enjoys banana, a few other plain fruits make good occasional treats too. The AKC suggests frozen chunks of:

- Apples
- Strawberries
- Raspberries
- Pineapple

Introduce new foods one at a time and in small amounts, so you can tell how your dog reacts. And some fruits are
truly dangerous: never share grapes or raisins, which can cause kidney failure (see
[can dogs eat grapes?](/can-dogs-eat-grapes/)). For the full list, read our guide to
[foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
