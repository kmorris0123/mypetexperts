Chorizo is not a good food for dogs. It is fatty and salty, and it is made with strong seasonings. A small bite will
not hurt most dogs, but a large amount can cause an upset stomach or a painful belly problem. **Keep it out of your dog's
bowl.** Plain cooked egg is a much better thing to share.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate a lot of chorizo">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline if your dog ate a large amount, or if you see vomiting, a
  hunched back, or tremors. Also call if the chorizo had garlic or onion in it.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is chorizo bad for dogs?

Chorizo is a problem for three reasons: fat, salt, and seasonings.

**Fat.** Rich, fatty food can make a dog sick. The AKC says a single large serving of high-fat food can bring on
pancreatitis in some dogs. This is a painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ that helps with digestion. Holiday meals
and table scraps are common causes.

**Salt.** The ASPCA warns that too much salt can make dogs very thirsty and make them pee more. In bad cases it can cause
vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. Pet Poison Helpline lists similar signs.

**Seasonings.** Recipes for chorizo differ, so check the label or ask the cook. The ASPCA says garlic and onion can upset
a dog's stomach and damage red blood cells, which can lead to anemia. How much it hurts depends on how much the dog eats.
The sources I read did not say how much chorizo is too much, so there is no amount we can call safe.

## What signs should I watch for?

After a dog eats fatty or salty food, watch for these signs:

- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Not wanting to eat
- Tiredness or weakness
- A hunched back or signs of belly pain
- Drinking and peeing much more than usual
- Tremors or seizures (an emergency)

The Merck Veterinary Manual says pancreatitis can cause belly pain, vomiting, weakness, and dehydration. Some dogs
stand with their front legs down and their back end up, which can be a sign of pain. The AKC says a dog with several of
these signs should see a vet as soon as possible. Go right away if your dog's gums look dry, your dog will not drink, or
your dog cannot keep water down.

## What should I do if my dog ate chorizo?

1. **Take away any chorizo that is left,** and check how much is missing.
2. **Read the label** or ask the cook about garlic, onion, and other ingredients.
3. **Call your vet or a poison hotline** if your dog ate more than a small bite, is small, or has any signs above.
4. **Do not make your dog vomit** unless a vet tells you to.
5. **Offer fresh water,** and keep watching your dog.

If a dog drinks lots of water after eating salty food and still gets worse, that needs a vet. Treatment for salt
poisoning is often fluids through a vein, according to Pet Poison Helpline.

## Can dogs eat chorizo and eggs?

Your dog can have the egg, but not the chorizo. The AKC says eggs should be fully cooked. Cook them plain, with no oil,
butter, salt, or spices. Scrambled or hard-boiled eggs are fine.

Raw eggs can carry Salmonella and E. coli, the ASPCA says. Raw egg white also contains something that can block a
vitamin your dog needs called biotin. If your dog ate a raw egg, watch for fever, vomiting, diarrhea, or tiredness.

Eggs are rich in calories, so give them once in a while and in small bites. Our guide to
[whether dogs can eat eggs](/can-dogs-eat-eggs/) has more detail.

## What about other kinds of chorizo and sausage?

### Raw or uncooked chorizo

Some chorizo is sold raw. The sources I read did not cover raw sausage, so the safest choice is to keep it away from
your dog and call your vet if your dog ate some.

### Other sausages

Other sausages are often also fatty and salty, so the same advice applies. Check the label for garlic or onion. We do not
have a separate guide on sausage yet.

## What can I share with my dog instead?

Try a small piece of plain cooked egg. If you are wondering about another salty meat, read our guide on
[whether dogs can eat ham](/can-dogs-eat-ham/). Treats should be no more than
about 10% of your dog's daily calories. For more foods to avoid, see our list of
[foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
