## How to choose a covered litter box

### Ask first if your cat will use a lid

Cornell says covers help people with odor and stray litter, but they can trap smells for cats. It says most cats prefer an uncovered box with a full view around them. The ASPCA goes further and says to avoid covered boxes. Studies of every cat do not exist, so your cat's behavior is the best guide. Buy one and keep your old box nearby for a while. Petmate suggests the same short transition.

### Pick a size your cat fits in

Cornell says large and overweight cats need bigger boxes. Check the outside size and the door opening before you buy. Not every maker lists a weight limit, so measure your cat and the opening.

### Think about entry height

Cornell and the ASPCA say kittens, older cats and cats with joint problems need low sides. A top-entry box is the opposite of that. A front door with a low step is easier for these cats.

### Look at the filter and cleaning

A filter reduces smell for you, but it does not replace scooping. Check how the filter is replaced and how often. Pick a lid that comes off or opens wide so you can scoop and wash the box.

### Count your boxes and place them well

Both sources say to have one box per cat, plus one extra. Put them in quiet, easy-to-reach spots away from food and water. The ASPCA says cats like a spot where they can see people or pets coming and have more than one way out. A hood limits that view, so place a covered box in a calm area.

## Safety tips

- **Watch how your cat uses it.** If your cat starts going outside the box, take the lid off or go back to an open box.
- **Scoop every day.** The ASPCA says to scoop at least once a day, and Cornell says to change litter often enough that it looks and smells clean.
- **Wash weekly.** The ASPCA says to wash the box with warm water and unscented soap or baking soda once a week, and to avoid scented cleaners.
- **Replace worn boxes.** Cornell says to replace boxes that are cracked or smell bad.
- **See your vet about changes.** Cornell says a cat with ongoing litter box trouble should be examined, because medical problems are a common cause.
