No, BBQ pork is not a good choice for dogs. Plain cooked pork is fine, but BBQ sauce and spice rubs often contain
garlic, onion, salt, and sugar. Garlic and onion can damage a dog's red blood cells. Skip the sauce, skip the bones, and
give your dog something plain instead.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate BBQ pork with garlic or onion">

  poisoning can take days to show up.

## Why is BBQ pork bad for dogs?

The pork itself is not the main problem. The AKC says plain, cooked pork is fine for dogs if you keep it simple. The trouble is everything added to it.

- **Spice rubs.** The AKC warns that rubs with onion powder, garlic powder, or nutmeg are highly toxic to dogs.
- **BBQ sauce.** The AKC advises against it. Sauces are often high in salt and sugar, and may contain garlic or onion.
- **Fat.** Pork is rich in fat. Dogs find fat hard to digest, and it can lead to an upset stomach or pancreatitis, a painful swelling of the pancreas.

## How dangerous are garlic and onion?

They are the biggest worry. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered forms have all caused poisoning. Concentrated forms like powders and flakes are most often linked to it. It also says garlic is 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.

These foods damage red blood cells. Signs don't usually appear until a few days after your dog ate them. They can include tiredness, weakness, not wanting to eat, fast breathing, yellow gums or skin, and dark urine.

## What about the salt?

Salt is a second concern. The Merck Veterinary Manual says dogs are unlikely to have trouble with salt when fresh water is available. Problems come with a large amount of salt, especially without water. Signs can start with vomiting, then move to weakness, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures.

How much is too much depends on the size of your dog, so call your vet if your dog ate a lot of salty, sauced meat.

## Other kinds of BBQ pork

### Is pulled pork okay for dogs?

Not usually. Pulled pork is almost always cooked with a spice rub and mixed with sauce. Plain pork with nothing added is fine in a small amount. If you can't be sure what is in it, don't share it.

### Are BBQ ribs okay for dogs?

No. The AKC says pork bones are not safe, raw or cooked. They are likely to splinter and crack. Pieces can cause choking, a blockage, or damage to the throat or gut. Ribs are also coated in sauce.

If your dog eats a bone, the AKC says to take it away and watch closely. Signs of a blockage include vomiting, drooling a lot, odd bowel movements, and not wanting to eat. See a vet right away if you notice any.

### What about raw or undercooked pork?

Don't share it. The AKC says raw or undercooked pork can carry a parasite that causes trichinosis. Signs can include vomiting, diarrhea, fever, tiredness, and sore or stiff muscles.

## What should I do if my dog ate BBQ pork?

1. **Check what was in it.** Look at the sauce and rub ingredients for garlic, onion, or their powders.
2. **Take away any bones** and leftovers.
3. **Call your vet or a poison hotline** if garlic or onion was in it, if the amount was large, or if your dog ate bones.
4. **Watch your dog for several days.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says garlic and onion signs can be delayed. The AKC says to call a vet right away if a reaction develops after barbecued pork.

If you are not sure what was in the sauce or rub, ask your vet what to watch for.

## What can my dog have instead?

- A small piece of plain, fully cooked pork with no fat, bones, or seasoning
- Plain boiled chicken, which the AKC describes as an easy-to-digest option
- Your dog's usual treats
