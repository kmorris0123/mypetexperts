No, cats should not eat onions. Raw, cooked, or dried, onions can damage a cat's red blood cells and lead to anemia, which means
the blood can't carry enough oxygen. Cats are more sensitive than dogs, so even a small amount is a reason to call your vet.

## Why are onions bad for cats?

Onions belong to the Allium family, along with garlic, chives, and leeks. The Merck Veterinary Manual says these plants
contain sulfur compounds that damage red blood cells. The damaged cells form clumps called Heinz bodies, and the body
removes them too fast. This is called hemolytic anemia.

Merck calls cats the most sensitive species, with dogs next. The ASPCA also says cats are more at risk than dogs. Merck
notes that very small amounts can be a problem for a cat. The ASPCA names the harmful compound as N-propyl disulfide.

## Are cooked onions safe for cats?

No. Cooking does not remove the danger. Merck says raw, cooked, dried, and powdered onion can all be toxic. This
includes onion in soup, gravy, stuffing, and sauces. Onion powder is also found in some seasonings and human foods.

## Other kinds of onion

### Are green onions safe for cats?

No. Green onions (also called scallions) are Alliums too. Pet Poison Helpline lists green onions, chives, leeks, and
shallots as poisonous to cats, dogs, and livestock. The same goes for the white and green parts. Treat them like any
other onion.

### What about chives, leeks, and garlic?

Also no. Pet Poison Helpline and the ASPCA list chives, leeks, and garlic as risky for cats. Merck says garlic is
about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.

### What about onion powder and cooked onion?

No. As above, Merck says dehydrated and cooked onion are toxic too. Check ingredient labels before sharing any human food.

## What are the signs of onion poisoning in cats?

The ASPCA lists these signs:

- Vomiting
- Weakness
- Fast heart rate and panting
- Blood in the urine

Pet Poison Helpline also lists nausea and diarrhea. Merck says signs of anemia usually appear several days after eating
onion, about 3 to 5 days. They include tiredness, not wanting to eat, fast breathing, yellow skin or gums, dark
urine, and collapse. Don't wait for these signs before you call.

## What should I do if my cat ate onion?

1. Take any onion or food away from your cat.
2. Call your vet or a poison hotline right away. Tell them what your cat ate, how much, and when.
3. Follow their advice. Merck says early treatment to remove the onion from the stomach helps. Cats with anemia may
   need oxygen, fluids, or blood transfusions.

Don't try to make your cat vomit yourself unless a vet tells you to.

## How much onion is too much?

No safe amount is known. Merck says even very small amounts can cause harm in cats, so the best plan is none at all.
Keep your cat out of the kitchen when you cook, and clean up scraps right away. Don't share table scraps that might
have onion or onion powder in them.
