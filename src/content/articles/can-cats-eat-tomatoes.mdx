A ripe, red tomato is not toxic to cats, so a small nibble won't poison your cat. But **green tomatoes, leaves, and
stems can make a cat sick**, and tomato sauce often has onion or garlic, which are dangerous. Cats don't need tomatoes
at all, so the safest choice is to skip them.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate tomato leaves, green tomatoes, or sauce with onion or garlic">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline. Do this even if your cat seems fine, because some
  poisons take days to show.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a consultation fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why are tomato plants bad for cats?

The ASPCA lists the tomato plant as toxic to cats and names solanine as the harmful substance. Pet Poison Helpline
says toxic plant compounds are found in all parts of the plant. Ripe tomatoes have the least. Green tomatoes, leaves,
and stems are the riskiest parts.

If you grow tomatoes, keep your cat away from the vines and from any green fruit that falls. Pick up fallen
tomatoes and trim dying leaves where your cat can reach them.

## Is a ripe red tomato safe for cats?

The ASPCA says the ripe fruit is non-toxic, and Pet Poison Helpline agrees that ripe tomatoes are considered safe for
cats. That means a lick or a small bite of plain, ripe tomato is not an emergency.

It's still not a good treat. Cornell Feline Health Center explains that cats are obligate carnivores. This means they
rely on nutrients found only in animal products. Tomato gives your cat nothing a complete cat food doesn't already
provide. The sources we read don't give a safe amount, so if you do share, keep it to a tiny piece, once in a while.

## What are the signs of tomato plant poisoning?

According to the ASPCA, a cat that ate tomato plant may show:

- Drooling
- Not wanting to eat
- Bad stomach upset
- Sadness or low energy
- Weakness
- Wide (dilated) pupils
- A slow heart rate

Pet Poison Helpline also lists vomiting, diarrhea, stumbling, and confusion after a cat eats large amounts of green
tomato or plant. How sick a cat gets depends on how much it ate, its size, and other factors, so call a vet or a
hotline for advice.

## What should you do if your cat ate tomato plant?

1. Take away any plant or fruit your cat can still reach.
2. Note what your cat ate, how much, and when.
3. Call your vet or one of the hotlines above. Both of the poison sources we read say to get professional help if
   you think your cat ate something toxic.
4. Don't try to make your cat vomit unless a vet tells you to.

## Other kinds of tomato

### Can cats eat tomato sauce?

No, skip it. Plain tomato sauce is made from ripe tomatoes, but most sauces also have onion or garlic. The Merck
Veterinary Manual says cats are the most susceptible animals to garlic and onion poisoning. It reports poisoning in
cats after they ate less than a teaspoon of cooked onion. Garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.

These foods damage red blood cells and can lead to anemia, a shortage of healthy red blood cells. Merck says signs
usually show up several days after eating, and include weakness, pale gums, yellow skin or eyes, and collapse. So a
cat that licked pasta sauce and seems fine today still needs a call to the vet. Check the label for onion powder and
garlic powder too. Merck names powders and dried flakes as the most concentrated forms.

You can't tell from the front of a jar whether a sauce has onion or garlic, so read the ingredients list. If you
aren't sure, call your vet.

### What about green or unripe tomatoes?

Don't offer them. Pet Poison Helpline lists green tomatoes among the riskiest parts, along with leaves and stems.

### What about tomato leaves, stems, and vines?

These are the parts to keep your cat away from. The ASPCA lists the plant as toxic to cats.

## What can you give your cat instead?

Your cat's regular, complete food should make up nearly everything it eats. Cornell says a good rule is to keep
treats to 10 to 15 percent of a cat's daily calories, and it notes that treats are not a complete diet. If you want
to offer something special, a small piece of cooked plain meat is closer to what a cat's body is built for than a
vegetable. Ask your vet what suits your cat.
