Yes, dogs can eat a small amount of plain, cooked pasta. It is not toxic. The danger is what usually goes with it:
sauce, garlic, onion, salt, and butter. **Plain noodles are okay once in a while. Pasta with sauce is not.**

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate pasta with garlic or onion">
  Garlic and onion can damage a dog's red blood cells. Call your vet or a poison hotline, even if your dog seems fine.
  Signs can take days to show up.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Is plain pasta okay for dogs?

None of the veterinary sources we read list it as a poison.
The AKC even mentions cooked pasta as a home option for a dog with low blood sugar, which suggests it is gentle on dogs.

It is still mostly starch. It does not replace dog food. If you share some, boil it until soft, let it cool, and add
nothing. The AKC advises against adding seasonings, salt, sugar, oil, or butter to people food for dogs.

One exception: some dogs are allergic to wheat. The AKC lists wheat among the top ingredients dogs can react to. If your
dog itches or gets an upset stomach after pasta, stop and ask your vet.

## Why is pasta sauce bad for dogs?

Most spaghetti and pasta sauces are made with onion, garlic, salt, or sugar. The AKC says tomato products often contain
these things, and that they can harm or irritate dogs.

Onion and garlic are part of the allium family, along with chives and leeks. Pet Poison Helpline says all of them are
poisonous to dogs. They can cause stomach upset and damage red blood cells, which leads to anemia (not enough healthy red
blood cells to carry oxygen).

The Merck Veterinary Manual adds three details that matter in the kitchen:

- Cooked, raw, dried, and powdered forms are all toxic.
- Garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.
- Signs of anemia often show up 3 to 5 days later, so a dog can look fine at first.

The ASPCA also warns that too much salt can cause thirst, vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. Jarred sauce can be
salty.

## What are the signs of garlic or onion poisoning?

Watch for these, which can start days after your dog eats it:

- Weakness or tiredness
- Pale gums
- Fast heartbeat or fast breathing
- Not wanting to eat
- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Dark or red urine, or yellow-looking skin or eyes
- Collapse

If you see any of these, go to a vet right away.

## Other kinds of pasta dishes

### Can dogs eat spaghetti and meatballs?

No. Meatballs often have onion or garlic mixed in, and the sauce on top usually does too. Meat that is greasy can also
upset a dog's stomach. The ASPCA notes that very fatty foods can cause vomiting, diarrhea, and pancreatitis (a painful
swollen pancreas) in some dogs. If your dog ate some, call your vet and watch for signs for several days.

### Can dogs eat spaghetti bolognese?

No. Bolognese is a meat and tomato sauce, and many recipes use onion and garlic, so check before sharing. Treat it
the same way as sauce and meatballs. A lick of a dropped noodle is a small risk, but a full bowl is a reason to call
your vet.

### Can dogs eat spaghetti sauce or pasta sauce?

No. Plain tomato on its own is not the issue. The AKC says ripe tomatoes are non-toxic in small amounts. It is the
added garlic, onion, salt, and sugar in sauce that cause trouble. Check the label for garlic, onion, and their powders.
If you cannot find a sauce with none of those, it is easier to serve plain noodles.

### Can dogs eat spaghetti hoops?

Not as a treat. Canned spaghetti hoops come in a tomato-based sauce. The AKC says tomato products often contain garlic,
onions, salt, or sugar. A single hoop that fell on the floor is unlikely to be a big problem, but read the label and
do not share a serving.

### Can dogs eat raw spaghetti or uncooked pasta?

We did not find a veterinary source that speaks to dry pasta, so we cannot say how risky it is. Hard, dry sticks could
be tough to chew and swallow, so cook pasta before you share it. Do not confuse dry pasta with raw bread dough, which the
ASPCA warns can swell in the stomach and release alcohol as it rises.

## How much pasta can a dog eat?

None of our sources give a safe pasta amount, so we will not guess. Use the AKC's treat rule: treats and table scraps
together should be no more than 10% of your dog's daily calories. For most dogs, a few plain noodles is plenty.

## What to do if your dog ate pasta

1. **Was it plain?** If it was only plain noodles, none of our sources suggest a danger.
2. **Did it have sauce, meat, or garlic bread?** Find out what was in it. Check labels for garlic and onion.
3. **Call your vet or a poison hotline** if there was any garlic or onion, or if you are not sure. Have your dog's weight
   and what they ate ready.
