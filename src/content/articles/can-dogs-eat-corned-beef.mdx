Corned beef is not a good food for dogs. It is cured with a lot of salt, and too much salt can make a dog sick. One
small, plain bite is unlikely to hurt a healthy dog. But it is not worth sharing, and a big helping can cause trouble.

<Callout type="warn" title="If your dog ate a lot of corned beef">
  Take away the food and let your dog drink fresh water. Call your vet if your dog ate a large amount, or if you see
  vomiting, diarrhea, shaking, weakness, or strange behavior. You can also call a poison hotline:

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is corned beef a problem for dogs?

The main problem is salt. The ASPCA says too much salt can make dogs very thirsty and make them pee more. Worse cases
can bring vomiting, diarrhea, low energy, shaking, and seizures.

The Merck Veterinary Manual explains that salt poisoning mostly happens when a dog eats too much salt and can't get
enough water. It is unlikely when fresh water is always available. Merck gives no safe amount of salt for dogs. We
also found no source that lists how much salt is in corned beef, so we can't give you a number.

For a sense of scale, the AKC says a 33-pound dog needs only about 200 mg of sodium a day. Sodium is the part of salt
that matters here. Salty food adds up fast.

Fatty table scraps are a separate worry. The Merck Veterinary Manual says pancreatitis, a painful swelling of the pancreas, is
often linked to eating table scraps or other unsuitable food. Some dogs get it more easily than others.

## What about the other things on the plate?

Corned beef is rarely served alone. Check the rest of the plate too.

- **Onion and garlic:** The ASPCA says these can upset the stomach and damage red blood cells, which can lead to
  anemia. See our guides on [onions](/can-dogs-eat-onions/) and [garlic](/can-dogs-eat-garlic/).
- **Seasoning:** The AKC warns that garlic and onion powder in seasoned meat can be toxic to dogs. Check labels on
  canned or packaged meat.
- **Bones and raw meat:** The ASPCA says raw meat can carry bacteria like Salmonella and E. coli. It also says bones
  can hurt the stomach and gut or block them.

## Other kinds of corned beef

### Canned corned beef and corned beef hash

Better to skip both. We found no source that gives a safe amount. Hash often has extra ingredients, so check the label
for onion and garlic.

### Fresh or raw corned beef

Don't give it. It is still cured with salt, and the ASPCA warns that raw meat can carry bacteria.

### Corned beef and cabbage

The salty meat is the main problem. Also watch for onion or garlic cooked in the pot, and keep any bones away from your dog.

## What should I do if my dog ate corned beef?

1. **Check how much.** Note the amount, and whether onion, garlic, or bones were in it.
2. **Offer fresh water.** Merck says to give water in small amounts, often. Big gulps all at once can make nerve
   signs worse in a dog that has had too much salt.
3. **Call your vet** if it was a lot, if your dog is small, or if you see any signs. Don't wait for signs to get
   worse.
4. **Watch for pancreatitis.** According to Merck, signs include no appetite, vomiting, weakness, belly pain, and
   diarrhea. Mild cases may show only low energy or an upset stomach. See a vet quickly if you notice these.

A tiny bite that fell on the floor is usually not an emergency. If you are unsure, a call to your vet is the safest
step.

## What can I give my dog instead?

The AKC suggests plain beef, pork, or chicken with no salt or seasoning, cut into small pieces. Learn more in our
guide on [whether dogs can eat beef](/can-dogs-eat-beef/).

Keep treats to about 10% of your dog's daily calories. For more foods to avoid, see our list of
[foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
