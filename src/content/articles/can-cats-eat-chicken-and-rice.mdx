Yes, a cat can eat plain, fully cooked chicken with plain cooked rice once in a while. But this mix is **not complete food**. It leaves out nutrients your cat needs, so it should not replace regular cat food. If your cat is sick, call your vet before you change its diet.

<Callout type="warn" title="If your cat is throwing up or has diarrhea">
  Call your vet if your cat vomits more than once or twice a day, vomits blood, or seems weak, tired, or in pain. Also
  call if you see signs of dehydration, blood in the stool, or if your cat stops eating. Chicken and rice will not fix
  a serious problem.
</Callout>

## Is chicken and rice good for cats?

Chicken is a good fit in one way. The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats need some animal protein, because plant foods
do not have taurine. Taurine is an amino acid (a building block of protein) that cats must get from food. Without
enough, cats can get heart and eye problems.

Rice is a different story. Merck says cats do not need carbohydrates like rice, because their bodies can make energy
from protein and fat. It also says cats handle moderate amounts of properly cooked grains well. So a few spoonfuls
of plain rice will not hurt, but rice is not adding anything your cat must have.

## Why chicken and rice can't be the main meal

Cats need many nutrients in the right amounts. Merck says home-cooked diets made from a small number of foods are
often not complete, and these diets are a common cause of nutrition problems in pets. The same page warns that a diet
made mostly of meat and rice (it names hamburger and rice) can lead to low calcium in dogs and cats.

Cornell agrees. It says homemade cat food is hard to get right, and that vets usually recommend a store-bought food
that is complete and balanced. Look for a label that says the food is "complete and balanced" for your cat's life
stage.

## Is chicken and rice okay when my cat has an upset stomach?

You may have heard that chicken and rice is the food to give a sick pet. The vet pages I read for this article do not
recommend it by name for cats, so I can't say it is right for your cat.

Here is what those pages do say:

- **Vomiting:** Merck says that for short-term vomiting, a vet may have you hold back food for about 24 hours. Don't
  hold back water unless your vet says to, because dehydration makes things worse. Once your cat has stopped
  vomiting for 24 hours, food can usually start again in small amounts.
- **Diarrhea:** Merck says treatment depends on the cause. A vet may have you hold back food for 24 to 48 hours, then
  change the diet. Some cats need a special diet fed for weeks.
- **Don't force it:** Merck says not to force food on a sick cat or let it go hungry. Talk to your vet if your cat
  refuses the food you were told to give.

Because the cause matters, a quick call to your vet is the safest first step. This is true for kittens and older cats
especially. The sources I read do not give separate advice for them.

## How to serve chicken and rice safely

If your vet says it is fine, or you just want to share a small treat:

1. **Cook the chicken all the way through.** Merck says raw meat is not safe for cats, and Cornell warns it can carry
   harmful germs and parasites.
2. **Keep it plain.** Skip salt, butter, oil, sauces, and seasonings. Our guide to
   [foods that are toxic to cats](/foods-toxic-to-cats/) lists the ones to avoid.
3. **Remove all bones and skin.**
4. **Cook the rice until it is soft.**
5. **Cut the chicken into tiny pieces** so your cat can eat it easily.

## How much chicken and rice can a cat eat?

The sources I read do not give an exact amount for chicken and rice. Cornell does say that treats are not complete
food and should stay at about 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories. Treat chicken and rice as a treat unless
your vet gives you a different plan.

## What if my cat has a long-term stomach problem?

Merck says some cats with ongoing diarrhea need a special diet. Your vet may try a protein your cat has never eaten
before, such as rabbit or venison, to look for a food allergy. These diets are usually fed alone for at least 4 to 6
weeks, with no treats unless your vet says yes. Don't start this on your own.

## What can I feed my cat instead?

A complete and balanced cat food is the best everyday choice. Merck says that in the U.S., good foods carry a
statement that they are complete and balanced for a life stage. If your cat wants something extra, a few small pieces
of plain cooked chicken are a better treat than a full bowl of chicken and rice. For more on chicken, see our guide on
whether [cats can eat chicken](/can-cats-eat-chicken/), and for the grain side, see
[can cats eat rice](/can-cats-eat-rice/).
