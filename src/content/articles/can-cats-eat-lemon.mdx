No, cats should not eat lemon. The ASPCA lists lemon as toxic to cats. The skin and plant parts cause the most trouble because they hold strong oils. The fruit itself is called edible, but cats do not need it and may get an upset stomach.

## Why is lemon a problem for cats?

The ASPCA says the toxic parts of lemon are **essential oils** and **psoralens**. Essential oils are the strong-smelling oils in a plant. Psoralens are natural chemicals that some plants make.

On its lemon page, the ASPCA says the fruit is edible, but the skins and plant material can cause problems. Its list of foods to avoid adds that citrus stems, leaves, peels, fruit and seeds contain citric acid and oils. These can irritate the body. It says small amounts, such as a bit of fruit, are unlikely to cause more than a mild upset stomach.

Cats are also more sensitive to oils than many other animals. Pet Poison Helpline says cats lack a liver enzyme that helps break down some of these oils. It lists citrus oil (called d-limonene) among the oils known to poison cats.

## What signs should I watch for?

For lemon, the ASPCA lists these signs:

- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Depression (your cat seems dull or very tired)
- Possible skin irritation

Pet Poison Helpline lists more serious signs for strong oils in general. These include drooling, tremors, wobbly walking, trouble breathing, and liver failure. How bad it gets depends on the oil and how much your cat took in. Call your vet if you see any of these signs.

## What should I do if my cat ate lemon?

1. **Take away what is left.** Note how much your cat ate and which part (flesh, peel, or leaves).
2. **Call your vet or a poison hotline** if your cat ate peel, leaves, or oil, or if your cat seems unwell.
3. **Watch your cat** for vomiting, diarrhea, or unusual tiredness.

A small lick or bite of flesh may only upset the stomach, according to the ASPCA. If you are unsure, a call to your vet is a good idea.

## Other lemon products and citrus

### Can cats eat lime?

No. We did not find a separate ASPCA page for lime, so we rely on what the ASPCA says about citrus in general. Its guidance is that citrus peels, leaves, stems and seeds can irritate pets. The same advice applies as for lemon. Keep the peel away from your cat and don't offer the fruit as a treat. You can also read about [oranges for cats](/can-cats-eat-oranges/).

### Can cats have lemon juice?

We did not find a source on lemon juice for cats, so we can't say how it affects them. Cats do not need it.

### What about lemon-scented cleaners or oils?

Pet Poison Helpline lists citrus oil among the essential oils that can poison cats. It says oils are absorbed quickly through the mouth and skin, and that stronger oils are a bigger risk. It also says never to put concentrated essential oils directly on a cat. Keep citrus oils and strong cleaners out of your cat's reach. If your cat has trouble breathing near a diffused oil, move your cat into fresh air. Get emergency care if it does not quickly pass.

## What can I give my cat instead?

The simplest treat is a store-bought treat made for cats. Keep treats small, and only a little of your cat's day of food. For more foods to keep away from your cat, see our list of [foods that are toxic to cats](/foods-toxic-to-cats/).
