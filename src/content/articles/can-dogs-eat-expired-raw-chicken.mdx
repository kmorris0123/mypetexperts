No, don't feed your dog expired raw chicken. Raw chicken can carry bacteria like Salmonella even when it is fresh, and spoiled food can make a dog very sick. Dogs often eat it without a problem, but you can't tell ahead of time which dog will get ill. Throw old chicken away where your dog can't reach it.

## Why is expired raw chicken bad for dogs?

There are two problems. The first is the chicken itself. The ASPCA says raw meat can carry harmful bacteria such as Salmonella and E. coli, which can make both pets and people sick. The Merck Veterinary Manual says raw meat diets have been linked to Salmonella illness in dogs. It also says homemade diets with uncooked meat are more likely to test positive for these germs than cooked commercial food.

The second problem is spoilage. The AKC lists spoiled food and garbage as a cause of food poisoning in dogs. It names several bacteria that can do this, including Salmonella, E. coli, Listeria, and Staphylococcus. Staphylococcus can make a dog sick soon after eating.

Dates on the package are not a test. We found no source that gives a safe number of days past the date, so we won't make one up.

## Do all dogs get sick from it?

No, and that is what makes it tricky. The Merck Veterinary Manual says many dogs carry Salmonella and are not ill. Dogs usually get really sick when other things are going on, such as another illness or a very large dose of germs in a puppy.

The FDA agrees that puppies are more likely than adult dogs to show signs. So a dog that ate old chicken once and was fine is not proof that it is safe.

## What signs should I watch for?

According to the AKC, the most common signs of food poisoning in dogs are:

- Vomiting
- Diarrhea
- Tiredness
- Eating less than usual
- Belly discomfort
- Fever

Less often, dogs may have bloody stool, dehydration, or yellow gums and skin (jaundice). The FDA adds that Salmonella can cause bloody diarrhea. The AKC says signs often start within hours, though the timing depends on the cause.

## What should I do if my dog ate expired chicken?

1. **Take away the rest** so your dog can't eat more.
2. **Call your vet** and tell them what your dog ate, how much, and when. You can also call a poison hotline.
3. **Watch your dog closely.** The AKC says most dogs recover with care that focuses on fluids and comfort, but bad or lasting cases need a vet.
4. **Go in right away** for bloody stool, signs of dehydration, yellow skin or gums, extreme tiredness, or trouble breathing.

Dogs can also carry Salmonella without any signs, and the FDA says they can pass it to people through stool and saliva. Children under 5, pregnant women, older adults, and people with weak immune systems are at higher risk of severe illness. The AKC advises washing your hands after handling dog food, treats, stool, bedding, and bowls.

## How can I keep my dog away from old chicken?

- **Seal the trash.** Use a bin with a tight lid or keep it in a closed cupboard. The AKC says to keep dogs away from garbage, compost, spoiled food, and dead animals.
- **Don't offer it as a treat.** If the chicken is too old for you to eat, it is too old for your dog.
- **Clean up after thawing or prep.** The FDA warns that bacteria can spread from contaminated food and utensils to your hands and mouth. Wash your hands and anything the raw meat touched.
- **Watch on walks.** The AKC suggests supervising your dog in places they don't know well.

## What can I give my dog instead?

Plain chicken that is fresh and fully cooked, with no bones, salt, onion, or garlic, is a much better choice. We explain it in our guide on [whether dogs can eat chicken](/can-dogs-eat-chicken/). For other foods to skip, see our list of [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).

Treats, including chicken, should be no more than about 10% of your dog's daily calories.
