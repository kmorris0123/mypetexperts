Ice cream isn't a good treat for cats. It is mostly dairy, and the ASPCA says milk and dairy can cause diarrhea or other
stomach upset in pets. A lick of plain vanilla is unlikely to be an emergency. **Chocolate ice cream, or any flavor with
xylitol, is a different story, so call your vet.**

<Callout type="danger" title="If your cat ate chocolate ice cream or a sugar-free kind">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline. Tell them the flavor, how much your cat ate, and your
  cat's size.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is ice cream a poor choice for cats?

Dairy is the main problem. The ASPCA explains that pets don't have much lactase, the enzyme that breaks down lactose
(the sugar in milk). Without it, dairy can cause diarrhea and an upset stomach. The Merck Veterinary Manual says the
same about cats in particular: milk is not a recommended treat and can upset a cat's stomach.

Ice cream also has fat and sugar. The ASPCA notes that fatty foods can cause vomiting and diarrhea, and sometimes
pancreatitis (a painful swelling of the pancreas) in some pets. None of this helps a cat that already eats a complete
cat food.

## Can cats eat vanilla ice cream?

Plain vanilla isn't poisonous in itself, but it is still dairy and sugar. A small lick may cause no trouble, but it can
cause diarrhea or an upset stomach. It's best not to offer it on purpose. Check the label first if your cat got some.
Sugar-free kinds may have xylitol (see below).

## Other kinds of ice cream

### Can cats eat chocolate ice cream?

No. Chocolate has methylxanthines, which the ASPCA says can cause vomiting, diarrhea, fast heartbeat, tremors and
seizures. The Merck Veterinary Manual says darker chocolate is much more dangerous than milk chocolate, and white
chocolate has very little. Merck mostly describes dogs, so doses for cats aren't given here. Cats are small, so call
your vet or a hotline about any chocolate flavor.

### Can cats eat dog ice cream?

Dog ice cream is made with dogs in mind, not cats. We didn't find a veterinary source that reviews these products for
cats. Some are made with dairy, so the same stomach upset can happen. Read the label and don't offer it as a treat.
If your cat ate some, call your vet if the label lists xylitol or chocolate.

### What about sugar-free ice cream and xylitol?

Xylitol is a sweetener used in some gum, candy and baked goods. It can cause dangerously low blood sugar and liver
damage in dogs, says the ASPCA. Pet Poison Helpline says there are reports that cats may be sensitive to it, but there
is no published data. Because the risk in cats is unclear, treat xylitol as a reason to call a vet.

## What are signs of a problem?

After dairy, watch for:

- Diarrhea
- Vomiting
- Not wanting to eat

After chocolate, the Merck Veterinary Manual says signs of poisoning usually start within 6 to 12 hours. They include
thirst, vomiting, diarrhea, restlessness, and in worse cases tremors and seizures. Don't wait for signs if you know your
cat ate chocolate.

## What should I do if my cat ate ice cream?

1. Find the flavor and check the ingredients for chocolate or xylitol.
2. If it was plain, watch your cat for stomach upset. Call your vet if it doesn't pass quickly or your cat seems unwell.
3. If it had chocolate or xylitol, or you aren't sure, call your vet or a hotline right away.

## What can I give my cat instead?

Your cat doesn't need ice cream to feel included. Small bits of plain cooked meat or a cat-made treat are better ideas.
Keep any treats a small part of the day's food. The Merck Veterinary Manual warns that overfeeding can lead to obesity.
