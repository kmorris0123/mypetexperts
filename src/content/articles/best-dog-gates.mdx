## How to choose a dog gate

### Pick the mounting type

The AKC describes three types. **Pressure-mounted** gates need no tools and are easy to move, which suits renters, but they are less secure. **Hardware-mounted** gates attach to the wall or frame and are the most secure. **Freestanding** gates need no setup and work for odd spaces, but they are the least secure.

### Measure your opening

Measure side to side where the gate will go. Pick a gate whose width range covers that number. North States notes that mounting can be harder at the very low and very high ends of a range. Check which extensions you need.

### Choose the right height

The AKC says about 20 inches may be enough for puppies or small breeds. Bigger dogs and jumpers likely need at least 30 inches. Also look for furniture a dog could use to climb over.

### Look at materials, bars and latch

The AKC says metal is the most durable and chew-resistant. Wood looks nice but needs a pet-safe finish. Plastic costs less but may not last with large or determined dogs. Avoid horizontal bars a dog can use as footholds. Choose gaps too small for a head or paw to get stuck. Pick a latch adults can use easily but dogs can't open.

## Safety tips

- **Top of stairs:** Use only a hardware-mounted gate there. North States also says it should swing toward the landing, not out over the stairs, and have no threshold to step over. Confirm with the maker that the gate is approved for stairs.
- **Check the hardware often.** Look at tension knobs and screws for looseness.
- **Follow the instructions.** Install the gate exactly as the manufacturer describes.
- **Introduce it slowly.** The AKC suggests letting your dog sniff the open gate, then closing it and rewarding calm behavior. Most dogs adjust over days or weeks.
- **Babies and toddlers:** The AKC says a dog gate shouldn't replace a safety device for a child.
