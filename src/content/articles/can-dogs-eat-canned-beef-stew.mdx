It's better not to feed your dog canned beef stew. Plain cooked beef is fine, but canned stew often has onion, garlic, and a lot of salt. Onion and garlic can damage a dog's red blood cells, and too much salt can make a dog sick. If you share, pick out only plain beef pieces.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate a lot of stew, or stew with onion or garlic">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline. Do this even if your dog seems fine, because onion problems can show up days later.

  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

## Why is canned beef stew a problem for dogs?

The beef is not the problem. The AKC says plain, cooked, unseasoned beef can be a healthy source of protein for dogs. The trouble is everything else in the can. Stew is a mix of meat, vegetables, gravy, and seasoning. You can't easily see what is in it, and the label may not tell you how much.

I could not find a source that tests canned stew in particular. So the advice here comes from what vet sources say about each ingredient.

## Is onion or garlic in stew dangerous for dogs?

It can be. The ASPCA says onion, garlic, and chives can upset the stomach and damage red blood cells. That can lead to anemia, which means the blood can't carry enough oxygen.

The Merck Veterinary Manual adds these points:

- Cooking does not make onion or garlic safe. Dogs can be hurt by raw, cooked, dried, and powdered forms.
- Garlic is about 3 to 5 times more toxic than onion.
- Concentrated forms, such as powders, flakes, and dry soup mixes, are the ones most often linked to poisoning.
- Signs often show up 3 to 5 days later. They include tiredness, weakness, not eating, fast breathing, yellow skin or gums, dark urine, and collapse.

The Manual gives toxic amounts for raw onion only. It gives none for stew. That is why checking the label matters. For more detail, read our guides on [onions](/can-dogs-eat-onions/) and [garlic](/can-dogs-eat-garlic/).

## Is the salt in stew a problem?

It can be, if your dog eats a lot. The Merck Veterinary Manual says too much salt makes dogs vomit within a few hours. Worse cases can lead to weakness, diarrhea, muscle tremors, and seizures. The ASPCA also lists extra thirst and extra peeing.

A few bites of stew are far less than the amounts the Manual describes, which are measured by body weight. Still, canned foods are often salty, and I found no source that names a safe amount. The Manual says salt poisoning is unlikely when a dog has fresh water to drink. So keep a full water bowl out.

## Can the fat in stew make a dog sick?

Possibly. The Merck Veterinary Manual says eating trash, large amounts of table scraps, or other unsuitable food is believed to be a common risk factor for pancreatitis in dogs. Pancreatitis is a painful swelling of the pancreas, an organ that helps with digestion.

Signs of a bad case include:

- Throwing up
- Not wanting to eat
- Belly pain
- Weakness
- Diarrhea

The AKC says to trim visible fat from beef before you serve it. Greasy gravy is the opposite of that. Call your vet if your dog has these signs after eating rich food.

## How can I share a little beef safely?

If you want to give your dog a taste, keep it simple:

1. Check the ingredient list. If you see onion, garlic, onion powder, or garlic powder, skip it.
2. Pick out plain beef pieces. Leave the gravy and the seasoned vegetables.
3. Trim off any fat, and cut the beef into small pieces.
4. Give only a little, and only once in a while.
5. Never share cooked bones. The AKC says they can splinter and block the gut.

Plain beef without any seasoning is a better choice from the start. Our guide on [whether dogs can eat beef](/can-dogs-eat-beef/) covers this in more detail.

## What should I do if my dog ate stew?

1. Take the food away and look at the label or recipe. Note whether it has onion or garlic, and about how much your dog ate.
2. Call your vet or a poison hotline and tell them what you found. The ASPCA also asks you to note the amount.
3. Offer fresh water.
4. Watch your dog for several days. Vomiting can start within hours. Signs of onion trouble can take days to appear.

## What can I feed my dog instead?

- A few pieces of plain, cooked beef with no seasoning
- Plain cooked [carrots](/can-dogs-eat-carrots/) or [peas](/can-dogs-eat-peas/)
- Plain cooked [potatoes](/can-dogs-eat-potatoes/), with no butter or salt
- A dog food made for dogs, topped with a spoonful of plain beef

Treats and extras should stay under about 10% of your dog's daily calories. To see more foods to keep away from your dog, read our list of [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
