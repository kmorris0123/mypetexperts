## How to choose a dog water bottle

### Think about how far you walk

The American Kennel Club says to bring water whenever you take your dog out, and that dogs need at least one ounce per pound of body weight each day. Heat and activity raise that need. For short loops, a 20 to 24 oz bottle may be enough. For long hikes or big dogs, plan for more, or pack a bowl and a larger bottle.

### Pick a bowl style

- **Squeeze-to-fill bottles** put the bowl on the bottle. You fill it with one hand.
- **Bottle plus snap-on cup** is simple, but you pour the water.
- **Collapsible bowls** are small and light but don't carry water.

### Check the materials and cleaning

Look for BPA-free materials and parts you can wash well. The American Kennel Club says to wash water bowls daily and to replace cracked bowls or bottles. Check whether the maker says the product is dishwasher safe or hand wash only.

### Match the bowl to your dog

A small cup is fine for a small dog but slow for a big one. Check the bowl size on the maker's page. Not every page lists it.

## Safety tips

- **Skip shared water.** The American Kennel Club says communal bowls, puddles and standing water can carry germs and parasites, such as Giardia and the bacteria that cause leptospirosis. If you must use a shared bowl, check that the water is clear and clean first.
- **Offer water often in hot weather.** The American Kennel Club notes that heavy panting causes fluid loss, and it advises limiting hard exercise in the heat.
- **Encourage picky drinkers.** The American Kennel Club suggests ice cubes to chew on or water flavored with bone broth.
- **Know the signs of dehydration.** These include dry or sticky gums, thick saliva, low energy and sunken eyes. Puppies, senior dogs and toy breeds are at higher risk.
- **Call your vet right away** if your dog seems dehydrated and keeps vomiting, or if you think your dog has heatstroke.
- **Wash and check your gear.** Clean the bottle and bowl often, and replace any that crack.
