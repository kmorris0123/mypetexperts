Almost nothing else is needed. Kittens grow very fast, so their main food should be a complete **kitten food**, wet or dry. Experts say to keep kittens on it until they are about 9 to 12 months old. Small extras are fine, but they should never replace the bowl.

## Why do kittens need kitten food?

Kittens need more calories, protein, fat, vitamins, and minerals than adult cats because they are growing so fast. International Cat Care says they should eat food made for this life stage, not adult cat food. The Merck Veterinary Manual says to feed kittens 3 to 4 times a day until they are about 4 months old.

On the label, look for a statement that the food is "complete and balanced." Cornell says this tells you the food meets standards for a certain life stage. Choose one made for kittens, or for all life stages.

## What can a young kitten eat while moving off milk?

International Cat Care says weaning can start around 3 to 4 weeks of age. Weaning means moving from milk to solid food. Here is what it suggests:

- Start with small amounts of good quality **wet kitten food**.
- Or soak **kitten dry food** in water until it is soft.
- Offering both wet and dry food can help kittens accept different textures later in life.
- Weaning is usually done by about 8 weeks.

If you are caring for a kitten with no mother, ask your vet how to feed it. Do not guess.

## Can kittens have treats or people food?

The sources we read do not give a list of kitten-safe people foods, so ask your vet before sharing any. Here is what they do say:

- **Treats are extras.** Cornell says treats should be about 10 to 15 percent of a cat's daily calories at most. Kittens still need the rest from kitten food.
- **Skip table scraps.** The Merck Veterinary Manual says to avoid them.
- **Don't feed dog food as a regular diet.** Cats and dogs need very different nutrients.
- **Keep supplements for the vet.** Cornell says a complete food usually needs none.

For plain cooked meat, check our guides on [chicken](/can-cats-eat-chicken/) and [eggs](/can-cats-eat-eggs/) before you share.

## Which foods should kittens avoid?

Here is what the sources say to keep away from cats:

- **Milk and dairy.** International Cat Care says to avoid cow's milk. Cornell adds that many cats are lactose intolerant, which means milk upsets their stomach. Read more in [can cats drink milk?](/can-cats-drink-milk/)
- **Raw meat and raw eggs.** The ASPCA says they can carry germs like Salmonella and E. coli. Cornell notes raw meat also carries a risk of toxoplasmosis, an infection from a parasite.
- **Raw or home-cooked diets.** Cornell says they may carry germs and have no clear health benefit. Merck says homemade diets often lack key vitamins and minerals.
- **Onions, garlic, and chives.** The ASPCA says these can damage red blood cells and cause anemia, and that cats are more at risk than dogs.
- **Chocolate.** The ASPCA says it can cause vomiting, diarrhea, heart rhythm problems, tremors, and seizures.
- **Bones.** The ASPCA says raw bones can hurt or block the gut.
- **Canned fish made for people.** Cornell links it to nerve problems in cats.

## What if my kitten won't eat kitten food?

Try warming the food a little, since International Cat Care says cats prefer food near body temperature and the smell matters. Use a ceramic or glass bowl, because plastic can pick up smells cats dislike. Change foods slowly by mixing the new food in with the old. If your kitten stops eating or seems weak, call your vet.
