Yes, dogs can eat hamburger meat when it is plain and fully cooked. It is a good source of protein. Raw
meat, seasoned meat, and burgers with onion or garlic are the problem. Share a small bite of plain meat, not a
burger from the grill or the drive-through.

## Is plain cooked hamburger safe for dogs?

Yes. The American Kennel Club (AKC) says plain, unseasoned, cooked hamburger meat can be a healthy protein for
your dog. Think of it as an extra on top of your dog's regular, balanced food. It should not replace it.

Cook the meat all the way through and skip the seasoning. Cut it into small pieces, as AKC advises, so your dog
can't choke.

## What makes a burger a problem for dogs?

Most of the trouble comes from what is added to the meat.

- **Onion and garlic.** AKC says both are toxic to dogs. Onion has a compound that breaks down red blood cells.
  This can cause anemia, which means the body does not have enough healthy red blood cells.
- **Seasoning.** AKC says seasoned meat can mean an upset stomach at best and pancreatitis at worst.
  Pancreatitis is a painful swelling of the pancreas. The ASPCA also warns that too much salt can cause
  vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, and seizures.
- **Fat.** AKC warns that high-fat foods are dangerous for dogs. The ASPCA lists fatty foods as a cause of
  vomiting, diarrhea, and pancreatitis.
- **Bones.** Cooked bones can splinter and hurt your dog's mouth, throat, or gut, says AKC. Check any meat
  from the grill for bones.

## Why is onion such a big deal?

Onion is dangerous in every form: raw, cooked, fried, or powdered. AKC says powdered onion is strong and shows
up in many foods, such as soups and sauces.

AKC reports that about 100 grams of onion for every 20 kilograms of dog weight can cause harmful effects. That is
roughly one medium-to-large onion for a 45-pound dog. A burger has much less than that, but the amount in a
sauce or seasoning mix is hard to know. Don't take the chance.

Signs listed by AKC include being tired or weak, eating less, pale gums, red urine, vomiting, a fast heartbeat, and
panting. Signs may be delayed, so call a vet early instead of waiting.

## Signs to watch for after a burger

If your dog ate a seasoned or greasy burger, watch for vomiting, diarrhea, and a sore or swollen belly. These can
point to an upset stomach or pancreatitis. Call your vet if they start or if your dog seems unwell.

## Other kinds of hamburger

### Can dogs eat beef patties?

Yes, if the patty is plain, cooked all the way through, and has no onion, garlic, or seasoning. Many ready-made
and restaurant patties are seasoned, so check the label or ask. Homemade is easier to control: cook a bit of plain
beef with nothing added.

### Can dogs eat raw hamburger?

No. AKC and the ASPCA both warn that raw meat can carry bacteria such as salmonella and E. coli. These can make
dogs and people sick. Keep uncooked meat out of your dog's reach.

### Can dogs eat a burger with the works?

No. Cheese, sauces, pickles, and toppings can hide onion or garlic, and AKC warns against toppings like these. Give your
dog a bit of plain meat instead of a loaded burger. If your dog stole one, check what was on it and call your vet
if onion or garlic was included.

## How much hamburger is okay?

None of the sources we read gave a set amount. A small bite or two is a reasonable treat. Treats should make up no
more than about 10% of your dog's daily calories. Ask your vet if your dog has a health problem, such as a past
case of pancreatitis, before you share fatty meat.
