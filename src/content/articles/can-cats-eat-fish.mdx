Yes, cats can eat a small amount of plain, cooked fish as a treat. Fish should not be the main meal, and raw fish is
a bad idea. Cooked fish with no salt, oil, onion, or garlic is the safest way to share it, a few bites at a time.

<Callout type="warn" title="Fish with onion or garlic can make a cat sick">
  Fish dishes are often cooked with onion or garlic. The Merck Veterinary Manual says cats are the most sensitive
  animals to these foods, and that even a little cooked onion has made cats sick. Signs can take several days to show.
  If your cat ate fish cooked with onion or garlic, call your vet or a poison hotline.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your cat vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is fish okay for cats in small amounts?

Cats are meat eaters. The Merck Veterinary Manual says oily fish are among the best food sources of omega-3 fats, which
help cats grow and have kittens. Most cats already get what they need from complete cat food. So fish is a nice extra,
not a must.

The trouble starts when fish becomes most of the diet. Merck says diets made from just a few foods are often not
balanced for cats. It also says cats that ate large amounts of canned tuna became short on vitamin E and developed a
painful condition called yellow fat disease. We cover that in [can cats eat tuna?](/can-cats-eat-tuna/)

Cornell's Feline Health Center adds that some cats that ate canned fish made for people developed serious nerve
problems.

## Is raw fish safe for cats?

No. There are two reasons to skip raw fish.

**Germs.** Cornell says raw meat is not recommended for cats, even as a treat. It can carry germs that cause infections
in cats and in the people who handle it, including toxoplasmosis.

**Vitamin B1.** Merck explains that raw freshwater fish and clams can contain enzymes called thiaminases. They destroy
thiamine (vitamin B1), which cats need. Low thiamine can cause poor appetite, weakness, nerve problems, and seizures.
Cooking destroys these enzymes. Merck says the heat used to can fish in cat food is enough, too.

## How do I serve fish safely?

- **Cook it all the way,** with no oil, butter, salt, or seasoning.
- **Remove every bone.** The ASPCA warns that bones can hurt a cat's gut or block it, and that surgery may be needed.
  See [can cats eat fish bones?](/can-cats-eat-fish-bones/)
- **Let it cool,** then offer a few small flakes.
- **Skip fish with onion, garlic, sauce, or breading.**
- **Don't make it a daily habit.**

## How much fish is too much?

Merck and Cornell don't give a set amount of fish. Cornell does say treats should be no more than 10 to 15 percent of
your cat's daily calories, and only given once in a while. For fish, that means a few flakes, not a whole fillet. Ask
your vet first if your cat has kidney disease, a food allergy, or any other health problem.

## Other kinds of fish

### Can cats eat cooked fish?

Yes, in small amounts. Plain cooked fish with no bones, salt, or seasoning is fine as an occasional treat. For one
type in detail, see [can cats eat salmon?](/can-cats-eat-salmon/)

### Can cats eat canned fish?

Be careful. Cornell says some cats that ate canned fish made for people developed serious nerve problems. Canned fish
for people can also be salty or packed in oil or sauce. The ASPCA says too much salt can cause thirst, vomiting,
diarrhea, tremors, and seizures. If you offer some, pick plain fish with no added salt, drain it, and give just a few
flakes.

### Can cats eat fish cat food?

Yes. Cornell says a food with a "complete and balanced" label is made to meet a cat's needs, and the label should name
the life stage it is for. Fish-flavored food with that label is fine to feed every day. Fish treats are different.
They are not complete, so keep them small.

### What about sardines, goldfish, and other specific fish?

I could not find a reliable vet source on these, so I can't give a clear answer. The same rules apply. Choose plain
cooked fish, take out the bones, skip salt and oil, and keep it a small treat. Live pet fish are not a food for your
cat. Goldfish crackers are a snack for people and are not made for cats.

## What if my cat ate raw or seasoned fish?

A small bite of raw fish is not always an emergency, but watch your cat. Call your vet if you see vomiting, diarrhea,
no appetite, weakness, or odd behavior. If the fish had onion or garlic, call your vet or a poison hotline right away.
