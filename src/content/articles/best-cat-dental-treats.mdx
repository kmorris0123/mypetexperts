## How to choose cat dental treats

### Start with brushing

Cornell Feline Health Center says daily brushing prevents most dental disease. Use a cat toothbrush and toothpaste made for cats. Cornell also says human toothpaste is not safe for cats. The Veterinary Oral Health Council also calls daily brushing the gold standard. Think of treats as a helper, not a fix.

### Look for the VOHC seal

The Veterinary Oral Health Council (VOHC) gives its seal to products that show they control plaque, tartar, or both. It does not test products itself. A council of veterinary dentists and dental scientists reviews data from the maker. You can check its [accepted products list](https://vohc.org/accepted-products/) before you buy. A product without the seal isn't proven bad, but its dental claims haven't been through that review.

### Check the label and the calories

Treats add calories. Both Greenies and WHIMZEES tell owners to cut back the main meal to make up for them. Check the age on the package. Check the ingredients too if your cat has food allergies. Follow the feeding directions on your bag, because they can differ from product to product.

### Know when a treat is not enough

Cornell says between 50 and 90 percent of cats older than four have some kind of dental disease. Signs include bad breath, drooling, turning the head while chewing, and not wanting to eat. Red, swollen or bleeding gums are another sign. Treats can't fix this. Cornell says removing plaque by scaling usually needs anesthesia. Cats with painful gums may also find brushing hurts, so ask your vet first.

## Safety tips

- **Ask your vet first** if your cat has bad breath, sore gums or trouble eating. These can point to a health problem.
- **Watch your cat** while they eat treats, as the Greenies page advises.
- **Offer fresh water.** Greenies' directions say to provide fresh water.
- **Cut back the main meal** a little to make up for treat calories.
- **Skip human dental products.** Use only toothpaste and brushes made for cats.
- **Stop and call your vet** if a treat seems to upset your cat's stomach. VetriScience says to stop use if a pet's condition worsens.
