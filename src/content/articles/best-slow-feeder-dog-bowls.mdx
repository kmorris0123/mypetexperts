## How to choose a slow feeder dog bowl

### Why slow down a fast eater?

The AKC says dogs that eat very fast can choke or vomit. It also says gulping air with food may raise the risk of bloat. Bloat is when the stomach swells and may twist, and it is life-threatening. An AKC article on bloat says fast eaters have a higher risk than slower eaters, and that slow feeder bowls physically slow dogs down.

### Pick the right size

Choose a bowl that holds your dog's whole meal. Check the cup size on the product page. Sizes in this guide range from 3/4 cup to 5 cups. Ask your vet how much food your dog needs each day.

### Plastic or stainless steel?

Plastic bowls come in many maze shapes. Look for a listing that says BPA-free, as Outward Hound does for its bowls. Stainless steel bowls, like the Neater and the stainless Outward Hound, avoid plastic. Whichever you pick, check for dishwasher-safe care instructions so cleaning is easy.

### Look for a non-slip base

A bowl that slides around or tips makes a mess and can make mealtime frustrating. Non-slip bases and non-tip designs help keep it in place.

### Other ways to slow down eating

The AKC also suggests puzzle toys, spreading food on a cookie sheet, and smaller meals two or three times a day. Some dogs may do well with one of these instead of a bowl.

## Safety tips

- Watch your dog the first few meals. A maze that is too hard may frustrate your dog.
- Use supervised mealtimes for feeders like the KONG Wobbler. KONG says to stop using it if it is damaged.
- Check bowls for cracks or chewed edges and replace damaged ones.
- Call your vet right away if your dog's belly looks swollen or your dog seems unwell after eating. Bloat is an emergency.
- Ask your vet about portions. To keep other food hazards in mind, see our list of [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
