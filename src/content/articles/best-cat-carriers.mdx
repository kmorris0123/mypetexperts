## How to choose a cat carrier

### Pick a sturdy, secure style

International Cat Care says a carrier should be safe and secure so your cat can't escape. It prefers a sturdy plastic carrier because it is easy to clean. It advises you to avoid soft fabric carriers, which can collapse. It also warns that backpack-style carriers can cause too much movement.

### Look for a top opening or a removable top

International Cat Care notes that carriers without a removable top or a top opening make it harder for the vet team to examine your cat. With a top that comes off, your cat can stay in the bottom half and feel safer during the exam.

### Check size and weight limits

Brands list the inside size and a weight limit for each carrier. Look at both before you buy. For example, Petmate's 19-inch two-door kennel is for pets up to 10 pounds, and its 24-inch size is for pets up to 15 pounds.

### Plan for one carrier per cat

International Cat Care says each cat should have its own carrier. Cats that get along at home can act differently when they are stressed and shut in.

### Think about flights

Airline rules vary. Always check with your airline before you buy a carrier for a flight. Sleepypod says the Air is approved for in-cabin travel, but you should still confirm the rules for your trip.

## How to get your cat used to the carrier

International Cat Care suggests keeping the carrier in a part of the house your cat likes, rather than in a garage or basement. Then your cat can use it as a place to rest. Put in something warm, soft and familiar, such as a blanket your cat has slept on. A small treat or a toy can help too.

## Safety tips

- **Secure the carrier in the car.** The American Association of Feline Practitioners says to place the carrier securely in the vehicle. Your cat should not roam loose in the car.
- **Support the carrier from below.** International Cat Care says holding a carrier by the handle alone can make your cat feel unsteady. Keep it level and support it underneath.
- **Cover it.** International Cat Care suggests draping a towel or blanket that smells familiar over the carrier to help your cat feel safe.
- **Check the fit and the latches.** Make sure the doors latch and the carrier is not too small or too heavy for your cat.
- **Ask your vet.** If your cat is very afraid of the carrier, talk with your vet about the best way to help.
