Strawberry jam is not a good treat for dogs. Jam is a sweet food your dog does not need.
Some jams also contain **xylitol**, a sweetener that is dangerous for dogs. Read the label before you decide anything.
If it lists xylitol, call a vet.

<Callout type="danger" title="If your dog ate jam that has xylitol">
  Call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline **now**, even if your dog seems fine. Signs can start within
  minutes, and some can take many hours to show.

  - ASPCA Animal Poison Control: [(888) 426-4435](tel:+18884264435)
  - Pet Poison Helpline: [(855) 764-7661](tel:+18557647661)

  Both may charge a fee. Don't make your dog vomit at home unless a vet tells you to.
</Callout>

## Why is strawberry jam a problem for dogs?

There are two problems: xylitol and sugar.

**Xylitol** is a sugar substitute. It is used in many "sugar-free" and "low sugar" foods. Pet Poison Helpline lists jams
among the foods that may contain it. It says the amount in products varies a lot, so you can't guess from the jar's
front. The FDA warns that xylitol can make a dog's blood sugar drop to a dangerous level. Pet Poison Helpline also says
it can cause sudden liver failure.

**Sugar** is the second problem. The AKC says fresh strawberries have natural sugar, which matters for dogs with
diabetes or dogs that gain weight easily. It advises against strawberries in syrup because of the extra sugar. The sources we read do not give a specific warning about jam's sugar. The AKC's advice on syrup is the closest
guidance.

## How do I check a jam label?

Look at the ingredient list, not just the front of the jar. Be extra careful with any jam marked "sugar-free" or "low
sugar." The FDA says to check labels for xylitol, especially on those kinds of products. If you
see xylitol, keep the jar away from your dog. Ask your vet if you are not sure what an ingredient is.

## What are the signs of xylitol poisoning in dogs?

The FDA says dogs usually start with vomiting. Then the signs of low blood sugar appear:

- Less activity or weakness
- Staggering or poor balance
- Collapse
- Seizures

Low blood sugar can start within 10 to 60 minutes. The FDA also says some serious effects may not appear for 12 to 24
hours. A dog may need to stay at the hospital so the vet can watch it. That's why you should call right away and not
wait for signs.

## What should I do if my dog ate strawberry jam?

1. **Find the jar and read the label.** Look for xylitol.
2. **If it has xylitol,** call your vet, an emergency clinic, or a poison hotline right away. Tell them what your dog
   ate and when. Don't wait for signs.
3. **If it has no xylitol,** the sources we read do not give a safe amount of jam. Call your vet if your dog ate a lot
   or seems unwell.
4. **If you can't read the label,** call a vet or hotline and ask.

## Is strawberry jelly the same?

Jelly has the same two worries as jam: a lot of sugar, and possibly xylitol. Pet Poison Helpline names jam, not jelly,
but the label check works the same way. Skip the jelly, too.

## What can I give my dog instead?

Fresh strawberries are a much better choice. The AKC says dogs can eat them in small amounts. Wash them, remove the
stems and leaves, and cut them into small pieces. Don't give canned strawberries or strawberries in syrup.

The AKC says treats should be no more than 10% of your dog's daily calories. Check with your vet first if your dog has
diabetes, is overweight, or has a sensitive stomach. Our guide to [strawberries for dogs](/can-dogs-eat-strawberries/)
has more, and you can see our list of [foods that are toxic to dogs](/foods-toxic-to-dogs/).
