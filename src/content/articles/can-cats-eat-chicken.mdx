Yes, cats can eat chicken, as long as it is **plain, fully cooked, and has no bones**. Cats need nutrients that only come
from animal foods, so a small bite of plain chicken is a good treat. Raw chicken, bones, and chicken cooked with onion,
garlic, salt, or grease can make your cat sick.

<Callout type="warn" title="Quick checklist before you share chicken">
  - Cooked all the way through, with no pink inside
  - No bones, and no skin or fatty bits
  - No onion, garlic, sauce, breading, or heavy salt
  - A small piece, not a meal
</Callout>

## Why is plain cooked chicken okay for cats?

Cornell's Feline Health Center explains that cats rely on nutrients found only in animal products. Wild cats eat prey
with a lot of protein, some fat, and very few carbohydrates. Chicken meat fits that pattern better than most
people food.

It still isn't a full meal. Cornell warns that homemade cat food often lacks the right amounts of nutrients. It
recommends a balanced commercial cat food unless your vet prescribes a home recipe. Think of chicken as a treat.

## Is raw chicken safe for cats?

No. The ASPCA says raw meat can carry germs such as *Salmonella* and *E. coli*. These can make pets and people sick.
Cornell also says raw diets may hold live bacteria, viruses, and parasites that can infect cats and their owners. It
adds that proof of any benefit is lacking.

The FDA raises one more worry. Bird flu (H5N1) has spread to cats through food, including uncooked meat. The FDA says
the virus can be deadly to cats and encourages owners to think carefully before feeding any uncooked meat. It also says
heat treatment inactivates H5N1 in meat.

## How should I serve chicken to my cat?

- Cook it fully, then let it cool.
- Pull off the skin and take out every bone.
- Cut it into small, bite-size pieces.
- Cook your cat's portion separately, with no oil, butter, salt, or spices.

Treats should not go over 10 to 15 percent of your cat's daily calories, according to Cornell. A few small pieces
fit easily inside that.

## Other kinds of chicken

### Can cats eat chicken breast or boiled chicken?

Yes. Plain boiled or baked chicken breast is the simplest way to serve chicken. Take off the skin, check for bones,
and skip the salt.

### Can cats eat chicken bones?

No. The ASPCA says raw bones can hurt the stomach and gut or block them, and a block may need surgery. None of the
sources I read say cooked bones are safer, so skip them too. If your cat swallowed a bone, call your vet.

### Can cats eat raw chicken?

No. See the raw chicken section above. Germs and bird flu are the main reasons to keep it cooked.

### Can cats eat chicken liver?

Only a little, and not often. Merck says cats fed a diet made mostly of liver can get too much vitamin A. This can
cause neck pain, stiffness, and a limp in the front legs. Cooked liver as a rare tiny treat is a different thing
from a liver diet, but the sources I read give no safe amount. Ask your vet.

### Can cats eat rotisserie chicken?

Take care. The meat can be fine, but rotisserie chicken is usually salted and seasoned, and the skin is greasy. The
ASPCA says too much salt can cause thirst, vomiting, and diarrhea, and in bad cases seizures. It also warns that onion
and garlic can harm red blood cells, and cats are more sensitive than dogs. Pick out a few plain pieces from the
inside, with no skin, seasoning, or bones.

### Can cats eat fried chicken or chicken nuggets?

No. Breading, oil, and salt make these poor choices. The ASPCA says fatty foods can cause vomiting, diarrhea, and
sometimes pancreatitis. Nuggets and fried chicken may also include onion or garlic powder. A tiny bite will probably
not hurt, but don't make it a habit.

### Can cats eat chicken broth or chicken noodle soup?

Only if it is made for pets, or you know it has no onion, garlic, or lots of salt. Store-bought broth and soup often
have all three. The ASPCA warns about onion, garlic, and salt for cats. Soup also has noodles and other
ingredients that cats don't need.

### Can cats eat canned chicken?

Sometimes. Read the label. Canned chicken for people often has added salt. Look for plain chicken with little or no
added salt, or buy cat food or cat treats made from chicken instead.

### Can cats eat chicken and rice?

Plain cooked chicken is fine, as above. I did not find a veterinary source that says how much rice to give, or that
it helps a sick cat. If your cat has an upset stomach, call your vet instead of making a home diet.

## What if my cat ate raw chicken?

Watch your cat closely and wash your hands and any surfaces. The FDA says to call your vet if your cat has a fever,
is tired, eats less, has red or runny eyes or nose, has trouble breathing, or has tremors or seizures.
